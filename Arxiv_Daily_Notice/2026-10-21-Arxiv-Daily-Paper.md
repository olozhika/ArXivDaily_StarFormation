#### PSR~B0355+54: An Unexpected Compendium of Nearly All Scintillation Arc Morphologies
 - **Authors:** Zhen-Long Liao, Zhen Yan, Zhi-Qiang Shen, Rui Wang, Xun Shi, Ke-Jia Lee, Ju-Mei Yao, Xiao-Wei Wang, Zhi-Peng Huang, Kuo Liu, Yong-Hua Xu, Chu-Yuan Zhang, Fan Yang, Yang-Yang Lin
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27005

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27005

 - **Abstract**
 Scintillation arcs are sensitive probes of multipath propagation, offering a window into the distribution of scattering material in the interstellar medium. Contrary to the common observational finding that individual pulsars exhibit only a limited range of scintillation arc morphologies, we report that PSR~B0355+54, observed simultaneously at 2.25/8.60~GHz with the Shanghai Tianma Radio Telescope, displays nearly the full range of known arc patterns, many of which evolve rapidly. Across 54 observations from 2020 to 2026, we detect scintillation arcs in 53/53 usable observations at 2.25~GHz and 33/49 at 8.60~GHz. At 2.25~GHz, the secondary spectra exhibit five broad morphological types: forward arcs, inverted arclets, diffuse arcs, diffuse power, and irregular structures, with prominent displaced arclets appearing in several observations. In contrast, the 8.60-GHz spectra are generally dominated by faint forward arcs. Despite these marked morphological differences, the scaled curvatures and their annual variations agree between the two frequencies, showing that both trace the same dominant scattering screen at a fractional distance of $s=0.069^{+0.015}_{-0.014}$ near the pulsar, further supporting a possible association with the \ion{H}{ii} region Sh~2-205. Simulations show that this diversity can arise from variations in the scattered-image distribution within this screen, while brightness reconstructions at 2.25~GHz resolve localized structure down to $\sim0.02~\mathrm{mas}$. Tracking four arclets over 10~h at 8.60~GHz, including two displaced arclets, reveals a common evolutionary trend consistent with the line of sight sweeping across small-scale scattering structure, helping explain the rapid arc evolution.

#### Beyond static torques: Self-consistent migration and dynamical trapping of low-mass planets in dusty disks
 - **Authors:** Jose L. Gomez, Pablo Benitez-Llambay, Ximena S. Ramos, Octavio M. Guilera
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27071

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27071

 - **Abstract**
 The rapid inward migration of low-mass planets remains a primary theoretical challenge in planet formation. While recent studies show that the solid component of protoplanetary disks can exert strong positive torques to counteract the negative gas torques, traditional static fixed-orbit models fail to capture the fully coupled dynamics of a migrating planet. In this work, we present 2D high-resolution, two-fluid hydrodynamical simulations using a comoving reference frame to track the self-consistent orbital evolution of embedded planets across a comprehensive six-dimensional parameter space. By allowing the planet to dynamically respond to disk-exerted torques, we identify a wide diversity of migration regimes, involving inward/outward migration and oscillatory radial confinement. We also find that massive cores can overcome dust-induced barriers, experiencing dust-mediated runaway outward or inward migration. Conversely, in dust-rich environments continuous gas-dust momentum exchange combined with planet torques dynamically regenerates co-orbital dust asymmetries, creating robust dynamical attractors where super-Earths undergo stable, oscillatory confinement, effectively halting inward drift without requiring pre-existing pressure bumps or disk substructures. This self-induced trapping mechanism could be relevant in the outer disk, beyond the water-ice line in environments with a enhanced local dust-to-gas ratio, where icy pebbles are moderately coupled to the gas. In low-metallicity or highly diffusive disks, planets generally adopt monotonic inward decay, apart from cases with outward migration. Ultimately, the diversity of these self-consistent trajectories demonstrates that static torque approximations are insufficient. Evaluating fully coupled kinematic feedback is essential for accurately predicting planetary survival and the final architecture of planetary systems.

 - **Authors:** Nico Cappelluti, Fabio Pacucci

 - **Abstract**
 Gaia BH3, a $32.7\,M_\odot$ black hole (BH) on an 11.6-yr, $e=0.73$ orbit around a metal-poor giant at $\approx590$ pc, accretes from its wind. Neither its mass-loss rate $\dot M_{\rm w}$ nor the fraction of captured gas reaching the BH is known. We ask whether optical and ultraviolet lines from gas photoionized by the hot flow are observable and what they reveal. Unlike X-ray and radio data, they count the ionizing photons the gas absorbs and trace its density and velocity. With Cloudy we compute the H$\alpha$ and He II 4686 emission of the wind and inflow along the orbit for $\dot M_{\rm w}=10^{-11}$-$10^{-8}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$, Eddington ratios $f_{\rm Edd}=10^{-8}$-$10^{-5}$ and wind speeds of 30 (slow) and 100 km s$^{-1}$ (fast), and test each model against archival UVES and ESPRESSO spectra. $f_{\rm Edd}$ mainly sets the lines; in dense models they come from gas falling freely onto the BH, spread over thousands of km s$^{-1}$. The archival spectra exclude $\dot M_{\rm w}>2\times10^{-9}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ at $f_{\rm Edd}=2\times10^{-6}$ and $>2.6\times10^{-10}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ at $10^{-5}$ (95%, slow wind), Eddington ratios already excluded by the apastron Chandra limit, $f_{\rm Edd}<7.6\times10^{-7}$. Of two simulated campaigns, only 25 epochs around the 2029 periastron go beyond existing limits. For a slow wind, whose capture rate is constant, a $\ge5\sigma$ H$\alpha$ detection would imply an accretion state 2-3 times above the 2024 limit. For a fast wind, whose captured fraction rises 14-fold toward periastron, the campaign detects $\dot M_{\rm w}\simeq10^{-8}\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr^{-1}}$ for $f_{\rm Edd}\gtrsim1.2\times10^{-6}$, which the apastron limit allows. Below the Chandra limit, a slow wind produces no detectable line.

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27090

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27090

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in the DR21 molecular cloud using far infrared polarimetry from SOFIA HAWC+ and molecular spectroscopy from the Nobeyama 45~m telescope. The Velocity Channel Gradient (VChG) method is applied to thin velocity slices of the 12CO and 13CO J=1-0 emission to determine the antenna temperature gradients and their orientations in the plane of the sky. The relative orientations of the gradients with respect to the magnetic field position angles enable an evaluation of theoretically predicted alignment of elongated, turbulent eddies for Alfvenic, fast and slow modes and self-gravity. The projected Rayleigh statistic is calculated for both the full cloud and 6 regions of interest to provide a quantitative assessment of these alignments. These perpendicular relative orientations may be a product of Alfven and slow modes in a magnetized, sub-Alfvenic medium even if the sub-filament is accreting onto the Main Ridge.

 - **Abstract**
 Shock breakout is the first electromagnetic signal of a supernova, generating a highly luminous and fast-timescale burst of thermal radiation. Motivated by recent theoretical progress in producing successful three-dimensional supernova explosions of massive stars, we undertake a detailed numerical study on the impact that the core-collapse asymmetries might have on the breakout signal. We perform the first fully 3D multi-group radiation hydrodynamics simulations of shock breakout for the case of a red supergiant explosion. We find that large-scale asymmetries formed during the core-collapse process can extend the breakout flash duration to days, with the timescale set by the supernova shock fully wrapping around the progenitor star. Our results show how multi-dimensional effects originating from the core-collapse mechanism can play a major role in setting the shock breakout properties, and are particularly relevant to upcoming UV space missions such as ULTRASAT and UVEX.

#### An improved self-gravity treatment in general relativistic smoothed particle hydrodynamics
 - **Authors:** Pritam Banerjee, Kowsona Chakraborty, Niles Mondal, Tapobrata Sarkar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27775

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27775

 - **Abstract**
 General relativistic smoothed particle hydrodynamics (GRSPH) on a fixed black hole (BH) spacetime is suitable for fluid bodies that are much less massive than the BH, but the self-gravity of the fluid is added as an extra term in the equation of motion. Standard GRSPH implementations add this as a Newtonian source term to the momentum equation, which is no longer valid when the fluid moves at a sizeable fraction of the speed of light, close to the BH. We develop an improved prescription by deriving the weak gravitational field of each fluid element in its local rest frame and transforming it to the global frame where the momentum equation is solved. The resulting force uses rest-frame distances and softening lengths, accounts for the time dilation of the receiving particle, and its motion relative to the source particle. The derivation applies to any weakly self-gravitating flow on a fixed BH background, provided the body is small compared with its distance from the BH. Implemented in a tree-based GRSPH code, the prescription reproduces the self-gravity of a boosted polytrope with a median error below 1\% up to a Lorentz factor of 2, and keeps a binary of two polytropes stable over ten orbits. As an application, we study tidal disruption events (TDEs) of solar-type stars by supermassive BHs. The treatment of self-gravity shifts the disruption threshold and changes both the remnant core mass and the debris energy distribution. Newtonian self-gravity leaves substantial surviving cores in encounters that our prescription predicts to be nearly complete disruptions.

Applying this technique to MUSE data of Abell S1063, we find that both components are old (~9 Gyr), but the ICL is more metal-poor than the BCG, implying distinct progenitor pathways. Within 100 kpc, the ICL comprises 55 per cent of the BCG+ICL stellar mass - substantially more than inferred from photometric decompositions. The BCG and ICL possess distinct velocity distributions, which remain nearly constant with radius, with an ICL velocity dispersion more than twice that of the BCG, and a line-of-sight velocity offset of 86 +- 18 km/s. Two-dimensional Sérsic fits yield an ICL centroid offset of 5.2 +- 1.5 kpc from the BCG. These phase-space offsets are indicative of the BCG oscillating in a broad potential minimum. Furthermore, the ICL surface-brightness profile matches that of strong-lensing estimates assuming a cored halo. Simulations indicate that the inner halo profile varies systematically for different dark matter models. Applied to larger samples, our method therefore offers a complementary route to testing the nature of dark matter, while simultaneously revealing the stellar origins of the ICL.

 - **Abstract**
 The search for the particle identity of dark matter (DM) remains one of the foremost challenges in modern physics. In this work, we derive new constraints on the DM annihilation cross section $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ by considering the inverse Compton (IC) scattering of DM annihilation injected $e^+e^-$ off the starlight radiation field in Omega Centauri. As a likely remnant core of a dwarf galaxy, Omega Centauri provides a dense stellar environment that enhances the predict IC signal. Using multi-wavelength observations from Chandra and Fermi-LAT, we establish upper limits on $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ over a wide DM mass range from MeV to TeV. Notably, in the tens-of-MeV range, our results surpass existing constraints by approximately two orders of magnitude, exceeding $\langle\sigma v\rangle \sim 10^{-31}~{\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}$. At several GeV to the TeV DM masses range, our constraints also remain stronger than existing limits. Our results demonstrate that starlight induced IC emission is an important component for indirect DM searches in dense stellar systems, establishing Omega Centauri as a powerful probe of the nature of DM.
#### The T-matrix method for modeling the optical properties of grains in exoplanetary atmospheres
 - **Authors:** Giambattista Aresu, Giovanni La Mura, Giacomo Mulas, Cesare Cecchi-Pestellini, Maria Antonia Iatì, Shadi Rezaei, Antonino Petralia, Daniele Locci, Giuseppina Micela, Franz Saija, Rosalba Saija

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28117

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28117

 - **Abstract**
 Observations of exoplanetary atmospheres have revealed a rather complex co-existence between the gaseous and condensed phases, ranging from atmospheres apparently covered in clouds to clear ones, across a myriad of intermediate regimes. The interaction between solids and stellar radiation is of fundamental importance when forward or retrieval models are compared to observations. The simplest approach is to assume spherical particles, whose scattering and absorption properties are readily computed with Mie theory. More advanced computational methods, however, can also treat non-spherical condensates. Among these, the transition matrix approach has proven to provide a rigorous and efficient calculation of the extinction by non-spherical particles over a broad range of sizes and refractive indices. We here focus on the absorption and scattering cross sections of forsterite, spinel and silicon monoxide from the near to the far infrared range for particles of different sizes, with shapes ranging from compact ensembles of spheres to porous aggregates. We find that, depending on the size and the configuration chosen, the differences in extinction can be as high as a factor of 2-3, with structured grains being more efficient absorbers in the near infrared for sizes greater than 1 $\mu$m. In a fiducial transmission spectrum we show that in the $\lambda = 1-5\,\mu$m range the amount of spherical material needed to match the contribution of the structured grains can be a factor 2 higher. Such calculations, available for the community through a Zenodo repository, prove to be useful for interpreting recent and future observations of exoplanetary atmospheres.
#### Dynamical stability of Magnetic Supramassive Neutron Stars
 - **Authors:** Jorge L. Rodríguez-Monteverde, Sebastiano Bernuzzi, William Cook, Maximilian Jacobi, Brynmor Haskell
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28609

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28609

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the dynamical stability of strongly magnetized supramassive neutron stars close to the mass-shedding limit using fully general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations in three spatial dimensions. Equilibrium configurations are constructed using a microphysical equation of state and considering poloidal magnetic fields up to maximum strengths ${\sim} 10^{17}\,\mathrm{G}$. The initially unstable magnetic fields reorganize into mixed poloidal--toroidal configurations on Alfvén timescales shorter than the stellar rotation period. The subsequent dynamical evolution is governed by timescales set by the Lehnert number. For stars with masses sufficiently lower than the maximum mass, magnetic stresses redistribute angular momentum and drive the development of differential rotation, leading to a new quasi-stationary configuration that remains dynamically stable over several rotation periods. Magnetically-driven winds develop from the outer layers around the equatorial plane and remain baryon-matter dominated with magnetization ${\lesssim}0.2$. Over the simulated timescale, winds carry a total rest mass of order ${\sim}10^{-3}\mathrm{M}_\odot$ at outflow rates of up to a few $M_\odot/\mathrm{s}$. For stars close to (or beyond) the maximum-mass turning point along the stellar equilibrium sequence, strong magnetic fields trigger gravitational collapse by redistributing angular momentum away from the stellar core. The resulting black hole remnants are characterized by angular velocities up to ${\sim}49\%$ larger and angular momentum ${\lesssim}8\%$ smaller than those of the initial equilibrium stars. Our results show that extreme magnetic-field instabilities can significantly alter the structure of supramassive neutron stars, with possible implications for magnetars and long-lived binary neutron-star merger remnants.
