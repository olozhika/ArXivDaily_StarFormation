#### Kinetic Tomography with Diffuse Interstellar Bands in APOGEE DR17
 - **Authors:** Philipp Frank, Andrew K. Saydjari, Catherine Zucker, Susan E. Clark, Douglas Finkbeiner, Torsten Enßlin, Andrew R. Casey, Alexandre Roman-Lopes, José G. Fernández-Trincado, Dhanesh Krishnarao, John C. Wilson
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25084

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25084

 - **Abstract**
 Context: Mapping gas flows in the interstellar medium is key to understanding the formation of structure in our Milky Way. Methods that simultaneously reconstruct clouds' distances and line-of-sight velocities are called kinetic tomography (KT). Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are powerful tracers for such maps because they trace the integrated dust density and the dust-weighted velocity. Previous DIB-KT works had small samples with coarse kpc-scale resolution and could not capture vertical disk structure. Aims: Make 4D (3D position + radial velocity) maps of the Milky Way from new, high-quality 15,273 Å DIB detections in APOGEE DR17. Methods: We combine variational inference and Gaussian processes with a mixture likelihood for low signal-to-noise detections and marginalization over distance uncertainties. We calibrate the model on mock fields and spectral injection tests and validate the map against independent tracers of the dust density (3D dust maps) and velocity substructure (masers and young stellar clusters). Results: We recover a posterior distribution over 3D maps of the density and velocity structure of the 15,273 Å DIB within 6 kpc of the Sun. We measure the large-scale structure of the DIB disk (scale length, height and flare) and detect vertical distortions consistent with the warp. On smaller scales, the map resolves distance ambiguities in gas longitude-velocity diagrams. From the second-order velocity structure function, we measure a slope (0.64 $\pm$ 0.04) consistent with Kolmogorov turbulence, amplitude consistent with expectations for molecular clouds, and a preliminary ratio of compressive-to-solenoidal modes. Conclusions: With larger DIB catalogs expected from SDSS-V, this framework should resolve kinematic substructure at the scales needed to constrain the formation and dynamics of individual clouds and voids. #### A novel pathway to stellar-mass black hole growth: Re-accretion of gas from stellar wind bubbles
 - **Authors:** D. Kománek, R. Wünsch, S. Martínez-González, S. Jiménez, D. Szécsi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25110

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25110

 - **Abstract**
 The formation of high-mass stellar black holes (BHs) observed in mergers by gravitational wave observatories remains difficult to explain using standard models of stellar evolution. We investigate whether newly formed stellar-mass BHs in dense molecular clouds can significantly increase their final mass by accreting gas from pre-existing, wind-blown interstellar bubbles produced by their progenitor stars. We performed 1D and 3D hydrodynamic simulations of interstellar bubbles driven by stellar feedback, incorporating thermal conduction, radiative cooling, gravity, ionizing radiation, stellar winds, and supernova (SN) explosions. We modeled the subsequent accretion onto the central BH for two formation scenarios: core-collapse SN and direct collapse. Our parameter space study encompasses over 500 1D simulations, varying initial cloud densities, gravitational binding energies, progenitor masses ($40-257\,M_\odot$), and metallicities ($0.66\,Z_\odot$ and $0.016\,Z_\odot$), and tracks the bubble evolution for millions of years post-BH formation. Scenarios exhibiting significant re-accretion were further validated using high-resolution 3D simulations. Our simulations show that BHs formed via direct collapse from low-metallicity progenitors can accrete up to $6.9\,M_\odot$ of gas from massive inner shells formed by late-stage slow winds, resulting in final BH masses of up to $93\,M_\odot$. Conversely, we find no significant re-accretion in scenarios involving an SN explosion or high-metallicity progenitors; in these cases, fast winds or SN ejecta efficiently evacuate dense structures from the bubble interior, leaving behind a hot, low-density medium. [abridged] This mechanism may help explain the unexpectedly high masses of BHs observed in gravitational wave events.

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25203

 - **Abstract**
 WASP-12 b is the only exoplanet with confirmed orbital decay. Angular momentum transferred from its decaying orbit should have realigned the star's thin convective layer, yet an earlier Rossiter--McLaughlin (RM) measurement suggested a $\sim$60$^\circ$ spin--orbit misalignment. We present two new Keck Planet Finder RM measurements. We measure a sky-projected obliquity of $\lambda=-1.3\pm8.7^\circ$; and the star's projected rotation velocity is $v\sin i_\star=1.3\pm0.1~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, an order of magnitude lower than expected for a star of its mass and age. With WASP-12's low chromospheric activity, $\log R^\prime_{\rm HK}=-5.5$, our analysis favors an aligned system in which $v\sin i_\star$ reflects slow surface rotation with $P_{\rm rot}\sim60$ days, rather than a projection effect. Our evolution models favor a late-main-sequence star rather than a subgiant, with a central convective core found in 99\% of posterior samples. This internal structure suppresses the central-focusing and nonlinear wave-breaking mechanisms for inward-propagating gravity waves. We propose that WASP-12 b was initially placed on a retrograde orbit after high-eccentricity migration, when its host rotated at $v\sin i_\star\approx14$~km~s$^{-1}$ (or $P_{\rm rot}\sim$6 days). Tidal decay transferred orbital angular momentum to the convective envelope, aligning the surface. The retrograde interior acts as an angular-momentum reservoir, while interior--envelope coupling slows the surface rotation. A semi-empirical relation gives an envelope--interior angular-momentum exchange timescale of $\sim4$~Myr, similar to the observed orbital-decay timescale of $\sim3.25$~Myr. The angular-momentum budget also permits this initially retrograde evolution. In contrast, an initially prograde planet would have spun up the surface rotation to a rate substantially higher than observed.

 - **Authors:** Charles Willard, Yakov Faerman, Samuel McCarty, Matthew McQuinn, Nir Mandelker, Thomas Quinn
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25220

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25220

 - **Abstract**
 We present high-resolution cosmological simulations of individual cosmic sheets ($z=3-6$) using the moving-mesh code AREPO to study cold gas ($T\approx10^4\rm ~K$) in the diffuse cosmic web. We find cosmic sheet gas forms a fragmented morphology, evident as kiloparsec-scale variations in the neutral hydrogen column density ($N_{\rm HI}$). Fragmentation arises from the bending and disruption of the cold-dense sheet core into a multiphase medium. Nonlinear thin shell instability (NTSI) and later shear-induced vorticity drive this bending, rather than previously suggested thermal or Richtmyer-Meshkov instabilities. Higher resolution enhances fragmentation. Across a fourfold range in spatial resolution, $N_{\rm HI}$ covering fractions increase by factors of $\approx 1.5-3.5$ for partial Lyman-limit systems (pLLS) and Lyman-limit systems (LLS). An order-of-magnitude estimate suggests sheets may host a large fraction of the ultra-low metallicity (p)LLS population. Equivalent MP-Gadget simulations show qualitatively similar fragmentation, but numerical smoothing effects are present. Cosmic-sheet gas properties remain unconverged despite our high resolutions.

 Among the current population of exoplanets there are very few sub-Neptunes that have their atmospheric composition identified. This often produces a spectrum that presents itself as a featureless spectrum especially in the near-IR. #### 3D Binary Evolution of a Merging Binary Black Hole Progenitor
 - **Authors:** David Dickson, Pablo Marchant, Jon Sundqvist, Mattias Fabry
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25310

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25310

 - **Abstract**
 Mass transfer is a highly dynamical 3D process that spans evolutionary timescales in binary stars. It is key to the production of gravitational waves through formation of binary black holes and other double compact objects. We integrate global 3D hydrodynamics into stellar evolutionary modeling to resolve mass transfer across nuclear timescales. We employ the shellular approximation in the stellar evolution code MESA to map a 3D spatial structure by equipotential shells in the Roche geometry to maintain hydrostatic equilibrium across tidally-distorted surfaces. We employ the method of time-incremented multiscale evolution to alternate between stellar evolution and 3D hydrodynamics to span large timescales with dynamical feedback. We employ this framework to examine a likely merging binary black hole progenitor system of a low-metallicity 30Msol donor star and 9.3Msol black hole from the end of the main sequence to core Carbon depletion. We modeled two complete mass transfer phases in 3D while self-consistently accounting for stellar evolution. These comprised a thermal-timescale early Case B phase and a nuclear-timescale late Case B phase. The prescription of Kolb & Ritter (1990) closely predicted the results of 3D evolution, except at the end of the first phase of mass transfer; our 3D model remained well-contained by the outer Lagrange point, despite 1D predictions of substantial L3 overflow during this stage. We find incorporating 3D hydrodynamics during Case B mass transfer did not significantly impact expected merger times. Our model never overflowed L3 as predicted by 1D prescriptions. L2/L3 outflows formed even while underflowing those equipotentials. Our model suggests these outflows can be neglected for the evolution of similar systems, but were dense enough to potentially impact X-ray observations.

 - **Authors:** Xianyu Tan, Yanhong Lai, Jinghao Zhang, Thaddeus D. Komacek, Tao Cai
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25803

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25803

 - **Abstract**
 We perform general circulation modeling of hot and ultra-hot Jupiters across equilibrium temperatures of T_eq=1600 to 2800 K, incorporating cloud formation and its feedback on atmospheric dynamics. This study specifically examines whether clouds explain the systematically low nightside brightness observed in Spitzer thermal phase curves of ultra-hot Jupiters. We found that clouds generally increase phase-curve amplitudes and reduce eastward offsets, and these effects are most pronounced at relatively low T_eq and gradually weaken as T_eq increases. In both clear and cloudy models, drag weakens the dependence of Spitzer nightside brightness temperature on equilibrium temperature; clouds further lower the nightside brightness temperature with the same drag prescription. Clouds also dampen amplitudes of thermal spectra on the nightside and, under small-particle conditions, can even produce emission features. Due to limited dayside cloud cover, our models yield negligible Bond albedos. Finally, interpreting simulated thermal phase curves with energy balance models in near-IR and IR bands tends to overestimate the Bond albedos of ultra-hot Jupiters by an order of magnitude or more.

#### Demographic inference in exoplanet chemistry is shaped by retrieval assumptions
 - **Authors:** Dwaipayan Dubey, Karan Molaverdikhani, Barbara Ercolano, Surya Shivaprasad, Fabian Grübel, Rosa Arenales-Lope
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26548

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26548

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding the chemical diversity of exoplanet atmospheres is crucial for linking atmospheric composition to planetary formation history. Atmospheric C/O can provide clues about where and how planets form, particularly through the accretion of gas and solids across disk snowlines. Since planetary migration can change orbital distances and hence equilibrium temperatures ($T_{eq}$), investigating C/O across different planetary populations may provide insights into their formation and evolution. As observational precision improves with the JWST, the limits of standard atmospheric retrieval methods that assume altitude-independent molecular abundances have become apparent. We present atmospheric retrievals for 20 transiting planets with published JWST transmission spectra, including complementary HST spectra for targets where such data are available, spanning gas giants, warm Neptunes, and temperate/warm sub-Neptunes, to test how population-level chemical inferences depend on retrieval assumptions. Free-chemistry retrievals show a visually non-monotonic C/O-$T_{eq}$ distribution, but this apparent pattern is not statistically robust and is sensitive to sample composition. When molecular abundances are constrained by a grid of disequilibrium models, the inferred C/O distribution becomes more restricted, showing that demographic chemical trends can depend strongly on the adopted retrieval parameterization. Several hot Jupiters converge to low C/O ($\leq$ 0.05), whereas colder, cloudy planets retain broad posteriors that reflect limited information content of their spectra. Our results suggest that population-level chemical trends inferred from free-chemistry retrievals should be interpreted with caution unless sample selection, residual structure, prior sensitivity, and chemical assumptions are explicitly tested.
#### Chemical Cartography Across Time: The Age-Dependent Chemical Structure of the Milky Way with a Homogeneous Sample of 580,000 Giants from Five Surveys
 - **Authors:** Jeff Shen, Neige Frankel, David W. Hogg, Joshua S. Speagle, Shirley Ho
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26550

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26550

 - **Abstract**
 Mapping the distribution of elements in the Milky Way and understanding how that distribution has changed over time can give us insight into the formation and evolution of our Galaxy. Here we use homogeneously estimated atmospheric parameters, elemental abundances, distances, and ages for ~580,000 giants from five major spectroscopic surveys---all simultaneously placed on a single, self-consistent scale by one model---allowing us, for the first time, to use a large sample of stars with uniform labels and ages that cumulatively span much of the disk to trace how the metallicity structure of the Milky Way depends on stellar age. Our chrono-chemical analysis shows that the radial metallicity gradient of the disk depends non-monotonically on age: it is shallow for the youngest stars, steepening to a maximum of ~-0.07 dex/kpc at intermediate ages (~5-6 Gyr), and flattening to near zero for the oldest stars. This "U-shaped" age dependence is driven largely by the low-alpha (thin-disk) population, with the high-alpha (thick-disk) sequence having weak gradients at all ages. In contrast to [Fe/H], the radial [Mg/Fe] gradient is weak (<~0.01 dex/kpc) at all ages. Together, these trends are consistent with an inside-out formation scenario in which the gradient of the star-forming gas has flattened over at least the last ~6 Gyr, while the near-zero gradient of the oldest stars reflects their formation in a well-mixed disk, efficient radial migration, or both.
