#### The Impact of a Magnetic Field on stochastically generated g-modes
 - **Authors:** T. M. Rogers, L. M. Corrigan, L. Barrault, R. P. Ratnasingam
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13082

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13082

 - **Abstract**
 Magnetic fields have been proposed as a mechanism for suppressing low-frequency gravity modes in stellar interiors, providing a means of constraining internal magnetic field strengths. However, previous suppression criteria have largely relied on linear theory. Here, we use two-dimensional nonlinear magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations in a core convecting star to investigate the interaction of gravity waves with magnetic fields of varying strengths and geometries. Using both global and local field geometries, we find that amplitude reduction is gradual and frequency dependent, with low-frequency modes preferentially attenuated while higher-frequency modes can be amplified. In order to achieve significant suppression we require a magnetic field strength at least an order of magnitude larger than predicted by commonly used linear criteria. Its likely that this difference arises because magnetic fields can themselves excite waves through Maxwell stresses, offsetting magnetic attenuation. Our results demonstrate that strong magnetic modifications to wave propagation do not necessarily result in equivalent suppression of the global oscillation spectrum. The linear criteria typically used to infer field strengths based on mode suppression does not capture all relevant physics and should be used with care.

Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13260

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13260

 - **Abstract**
 Atmospheric variability provides a unique window into the three-dimensional structure, dynamics, and evolution of brown dwarf and giant exoplanet atmospheres. Recent JWST time-resolved spectroscopic observations have revealed complex wavelength-dependent variability, offering unprecedented insights into cloud structures, thermal perturbations, and atmospheric chemistry. However, existing programs have focused primarily on individual benchmarks and specific scientific questions, resulting in heterogeneous spectral and temporal coverage and leaving important regions of the substellar population underexplored, particularly across a broad range of rotation periods, spectral types, and viewing geometries. A JWST Multi-Cycle Treasury program could systematically investigate substellar weather across a diverse, well-characterized population. By combining broad infrared spectroscopic coverage with observations spanning multiple rotations and strategically selected epochs, this program would constrain the contributions of different physical mechanisms while investigating how atmospheric structures depend on rotation, viewing geometry, and fundamental physical properties. Extended monitoring would also improve rotational-period determinations and reveal how atmospheric features evolve from rotation to rotation and over longer timescales. This coordinated effort would provide a consistently designed and uniformly processed dataset of time-resolved spectra and variability diagnostics, enabling population-level comparisons, testing predictions from three-dimensional circulation models, and establishing a framework for interpreting the atmospheric diversity and evolution of brown dwarfs and directly imaged giant exoplanets.
#### Molecular signatures of shocked gas and enhanced cosmic-ray ionization in supernova remnant W49B revealed by ALMA
 - **Authors:** Wenjin Yang, Ping Zhou, Zhi-Yu Zhang, Gao-Yuan Zhang, Bon-Chul Koo, Yan Gong, Ruo-Yu Liu, Hidetoshi Sano, Tian-Yu Tu, Yang Chen, Tram N. Le, Lianghao Lin, Friedrich Wyrowski
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13519

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13519

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic supernova remnants (SNRs) interacting with molecular clouds (MCs) provide important laboratories for investigating how supernova feedback is transferred to the interstellar medium. We performed observations of the HCO+ J=1-0, 3-2, and 4-3 lines, as well as the CO J=3-2 line, toward the eastern and western regions of SNR W49B using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the Atacama Pathfinder Experiment (APEX) telescope. In W49B west, we identify twelve regions with broad-line profiles in both HCO+ and CO emission, with FWHM of 25-60 km/s, providing clear evidence of SNR-MC interaction. These emissions are compact (at $\sim$5 arcsec resolution) and spatially coincident with 2.12~$\mu$m H$_2$ emission, but generally offset from the 1.64~$\mu$m [Fe~II] and radio continuum peaks. Non-LTE analyses yield shocked-gas densities of (0.5-1.6) $\times 10^5$~cm$^{-3}$ and high $N_{\rm HCO+}/N_{\rm CO}$ abundance ratios of (4.2-14.5) $\times 10^{-4}$. Reproducing such high abundance ratios in shocked MCs requires high cosmic ray ionization rates (CRIRs) of a few 10$^{-14}$-10$^{-13}$~s$^{-1}$, about 3-4 orders of magnitude above the standard Galactic level. Some regions exhibit enhanced HCO+/CO intensity ratios in their high-velocity line wings, possibly due to multiple unresolved shocks spanning a range of velocities and gas densities, spatial variations in the CRIR, or both. By contrast, the ratio remains nearly constant with velocity in other regions. For these regions, we further explore the propagation of low-energy CRs through the post-shock layer. An energy-budget analysis of a representative shocked clump indicates that the shock-associated kinetic-energy reservoir substantially exceeds cumulative CR and X-ray heating inputs, with CR heating exceeding X-ray heating.[abridged]
#### Angular momentum transport in a newborn protostar--disc system: magnetic braking, spiral arms and the magneto-rotational instability
 - **Authors:** Antonin Borderies, Benoît Commerçon, Adnan Ali Ahmad
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13729

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13729

 - **Abstract**
 Angular momentum transport in young protostar--disc systems involves magnetic braking, magneto-rotational turbulence and gravitational instability, mechanisms that are usually studied separately and on different scales. We present a three-dimensional non-ideal magnetohydrodynamic simulation, including Ohmic and ambipolar diffusion, that follows the collapse of a $1~M_\odot$ dense core continuously from the cloud scale to the formation of the protostar, and then the resulting protostar--disc system for $\sim 64~\mathrm{yr}$ at a resolution of $\sim 10^{-4}~\mathrm{AU}$ at the protostellar surface. We decompose the azimuthally averaged angular momentum budget into Reynolds, Maxwell and gravitational torques, each split into a radial and a meridional component, over a quasi-steady phase of the evolution. The collapse produces a thick disc an order of magnitude more massive than the protostar, whose specific angular momentum decreases as it accretes. Accretion through the disc is vertically layered, with accreting and decreting layers at the same radius, a pattern set layer by layer by the torques. The transport separates into three regimes delimited by the coupling of the magnetic field to the gas. Magnetic braking operates in the envelope. Maxwell stresses dominate inside $\sim 0.1~\mathrm{AU}$, where the field recouples and the magneto-rotational instability develops. In the ambipolar dead zone that makes up the bulk of the disc, the Reynolds stress dominates, carried by $m=2$ spiral arms in a marginally unstable disc, with the gravitational stress as a secondary contributor. Meridional transport is as important as radial transport, so that a one-dimensional viscous model fitted to the net mass flux would require a negative $\alpha$ over part of the disc.
#### On the Existence of an Equilibrium Density Profile for Massive Collisional Star Clusters
 - **Authors:** Ambreesh Khurana, Renyue Cen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13825

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13825

 - **Abstract**
 The dynamical evolution of massive, dense star clusters is governed by a complex interplay of multiple physical processes, including two-body relaxation, strong encounters, stellar evolution, and galactic tides, superimposed on the orbital motion of individual stars and binaries within the cluster potential. These processes can alter the internal evolution of individual stars and binaries, produce exotic stellar objects, and collectively determine the global structural evolution of the cluster. In this paper, we use the Monte Carlo star cluster evolution code CMC-COSMIC to study how the density profiles of dense star clusters evolve under the combined effects of these processes. We find that for a given initial mass and binding energy, cluster models converge toward a characteristic density profile largely independent of their initial density distributions. This convergence occurs during the late-time, quasi-static phase of evolution, when gravitational contraction is balanced by energy generated through black hole binaries in the cluster core. The shape of this profile is well represented by a King profile with W_0=6.8 but with the virial radius of the final cluster larger than the initial by a factor of about 7. We further show that this equilibrium profile is dynamically stable, i.e., perturbations away from it decay through the cluster's natural dynamical evolution, driving the system back toward the equilibrium profile.
#### Two-component $γ$-Ray Structure from the CR Sources
 - **Authors:** Lin Nie, Ming-Jun Chen, Zong-Kang Zeng, Yi-Qing Guo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14107

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14107

 - **Abstract**
 With the advent of a new generation of observational experiments and the accumulation of data, an increasing number of cosmic-ray sources have been found to exhibit pronounced two-component structures in their gamma-ray spectra above GeV energies. To investigate whether these structures share a common physical origin, we conduct a statistical study of sources exhibiting such features using a two-component model of cosmic-ray sources and examine their gamma-ray emission mechanisms. We find that the ambient environment significantly affects the spectral morphology. Sources located in low-density environments but surrounded by dense molecular clumps exhibit two clearly separated spectral peaks. In contrast, sources embedded in dense environments with additional dense molecular clumps nearby retain two-component spectral features, but the components overlap more smoothly, with the high-energy component appearing primarily as a relatively broad shoulder rather than a distinct peak. Our results lend further support to a physical picture in which high-energy cosmic rays are predominantly supplied by local sources, whereas low-energy cosmic rays arise mainly from the collective contribution of sources in other regions of the Galaxy. We also calculate the neutrino spectra of these sources, providing theoretical predictions for future tests of the model through observations of point-source and diffuse neutrino emission.
#### Empirical constraints on cosmic-ray scattering in NGC 1333 from dust polarisation
 - **Authors:** Szu-Ting Chen, Ellis R. Owen, Alison M. W. Mitchell, Shih-Ping Lai, Sheng-Jun Lin
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14203

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14203

 - **Abstract**
 Cosmic rays (CRs) regulate molecular-cloud ionization and chemistry. Gyro-resonant scattering by magnetic fluctuations sets their mean free path and, on larger scales, their effective diffusion coefficient. However, coefficients inferred from CR distributions averaged over cloud to Galactic scales leave local magnetic structure unresolved. We use the magnetic-field structure of NGC 1333 and its embedded protostellar core IRAS 4A to estimate local CR scattering rates and effective diffusion coefficients, $D(E)$, and examine their variation with environment and scale. We analyse JCMT/POL-2 maps of nine NGC 1333 regions and ALMA Band 7 maps of two IRAS 4A regions. We estimate plane-of-sky field strengths and derive fluctuation spectra from polarisation-angle structure functions. Where these spectra are sufficiently constrained, we apply quasi-linear gyro-resonant theory to calculate CR pitch-angle scattering rates and $D(E)$. The measured fluctuations constrain CR scattering over proton energy intervals of approximately 0.24-5.3 PeV in NGC 1333 and 10-57 TeV in IRAS 4A. Within these intervals, $D(E)$ spans approximately $3\times10^{28}$-$10^{31}\,{\rm cm^2\,s^{-1}}$. Scattering varies substantially between regions and is stronger where the magnetic-field angular dispersion is greater. Extrapolation to common energies suggests scattering in IRAS 4A is one to two orders of magnitude stronger than its surroundings. Within the directly probed intervals, the scattering mean free paths exceed the individual region sizes. The inferred diffusion coefficients therefore characterise local scattering conditions. A cloud-scale diffusive regime requires CRs to sample multiple regions with different scattering rates. Dust polarisation observations therefore provide constraints on CR scattering in clouds, connecting scattering to cloud-scale transport at energies accessible to $\gamma$-ray observatories.

 - **Abstract**
 Ultrahigh energy neutrinos ($E_\nu \gtrsim 100$ PeV) are detectable through Askaryan emission---the nanosecond-scale radio impulse emitted by relativistic particle cascades in a dielectric medium. The Askaryan Radio Array (ARA), an Askaryan detector array located at the South Pole, has been taking data for more than a decade. We present the first array-wide search for diffuse ultrahigh energy neutrinos with ARA. The search found no events on an expected background of $0.13\pm0.01\text{ (stat.)}^{+0.05}_{-0.06}\text{ (syst.)}$ events. The resulting upper limit is the most stringent from $50$ EeV to $1000$ EeV and provides the first constraints on the flux reported by the KM3NeT Collaboration that are complementary to optical Cherenkov and particle detectors. This limit also constrains UHECR source models, and excludes some models with trans-GZK proton populations at the $90\%$ confidence level. This represents the largest Askaryan detector dataset ever analyzed, demonstrating the feasibility and sensitivity of searches with large detector arrays acting as a single observatory. It also underscores the high acceptance and exposure of Askaryan detector arrays to ultrahigh energy neutrinos.
#### r-Process Abundance Variations in Globular Clusters as Fossils of Turbulent Mixing
 - **Authors:** Lailani Kenoly, Isabelle Connor, Melinda Soares-Furtado, Evan N. Kirby, Nia Imara, Enrico Ramirez-Ruiz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14472

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14472

 - **Abstract**
 Several metal-poor Galactic globular clusters exhibit substantial star-to-star $r$-process abundance variations at nearly fixed [Fe/H], with the ensemble suggesting smaller variations toward increasing metallicity. We investigate whether this chemical structure can be inherited from incomplete mixing in the galactic environments in which the clusters formed. Using parsec-scale simulations of rare-element transport in a supernova-driven turbulent disk, we follow the redistribution of $r$-process ejecta and characterize the abundance structure of gas selected to trace star-forming environments. Rare enrichment events generate strong [Eu/Fe] inhomogeneities that persist across a range of physical scales. As chemical evolution proceeds, the available abundance variance decreases, while spatial averaging over larger regions reduces the differences between their characteristic abundances. The simulations provide substantial pre-collapse $r$-process variance at low [Fe/H] and show the same broad decline toward higher metallicity suggested by the globular-cluster measurements. Our results support an interpretation in which at least some of the observed $r$-process variations are inherited from pre-existing chemical structure in the natal environment, allowing globular clusters to preserve a fossil record of the mixing history between rare enrichment and star formation.
