#### Carbon dioxide and amorphous water ices in the debris disk around $β$ Pictoris
 - **Authors:** S. K. Betti, C. H. Chen, C. Xie, K. Worthen, S. Brittain, J. Najita, I. Rebollido, T. Beck, B. T. Bolin, D. Hines, C. Ingebretsen, C. Lisse, M. D. Perrin, L. Pueyo, I. Wong, C. Stark
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18918

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18918

 - **Abstract**
 The star $\beta$ Pictoris is orbited by a dusty debris disk, within which planet formation is occurring. The disk extends far from the star such that molecules in the outer disk should freeze. We observed the $\beta$ Pictoris disk in the near-infrared using the James Webb Space Telescope. The spectrum shows features due to amorphous water ice (<7% crystallinity), and carbon dioxide ice in both the $^{12}$CO$_2$ and $^{13}$CO$_2$ isotopologues. We infer that the CO$_2$ ice is intimately mixed with the water ice, at a ratio of 1:10. This implies that the ices formed in cold conditions, likely in the interstellar medium during a phase of amorphous H$_2$O-dominated formation. Frequent collisions within the debris disk are required to explain the observed uniform distribution of icy dust grains.
#### Neural Surrogates for Tabulated Nuclear Equations of State in Core-Collapse Supernova Simulations
 - **Authors:** Youhei Masada, Jin Matsumoto, Tomoya Takiwaki
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Computational Physics (physics.comp-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19400

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19400

 - **Abstract**
 Large-scale core-collapse supernova simulations rely on tabulated nuclear equations of state (EOSs), whose memory requirements can become a practical constraint in massively parallel calculations. We explore a compact alternative by replacing the tabulated thermodynamic mapping with a neural-network surrogate, using the Lattimer--Swesty EOS as a representative reference model. The surrogate directly predicts pressure, sound speed, and effective adiabatic index from $(\log \rho, \log e, Y_e)$ and accurately reconstructs the nonlinear thermodynamic structure of the EOS, including its behavior across phase-transition regions. It reproduces one-dimensional shock-tube solutions with hydrodynamic errors at the $\sim 10^{-3}$ level under different thermodynamic conditions and yields hydrostatic equilibrium configurations closely matching those obtained with the reference EOS. We find that smaller regression errors do not necessarily yield better hydrodynamic performance, demonstrating the importance of hydrodynamic validation in surrogate model selection. A compact network with only $\sim 10^4$ parameters retains this accuracy while reducing the memory required to store the EOS from $\sim 160$ MB to $\sim 100$ KB, corresponding to a reduction by a factor of $\sim 1500$. The surrogate also remains stable through collapse, bounce, shock formation, and post-bounce evolution in a one-dimensional adiabatic core-collapse calculation. These results demonstrate the potential of compact neural EOS surrogates for reducing memory requirements in large-scale core-collapse supernova simulations.
#### High-inclination Centaur or Captured Halley-Type Comet? Observational and Dynamical Characterization of C/2024 L1 (PANSTARRS)
 - **Authors:** Theodore Kareta, John W. Noonan, Benjamin N.L. Sharkey
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19581

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19581

 - **Abstract**
 The high-inclination (`high-i') Centaurs of the middle and outer Solar System are of uncertain origin. While many have been captured directly from the Oort Cloud, making them less-evolved analogues to the Halley-Type Comets, this process alone cannot produce the number of observed objects. The most discussed alternate origin is direct excitation of trans-Neptunian Objects into high-i orbits by the action of some unfound massive planet beyond Neptune. It is thus critical to find ways to discern the origins of these objects to provide new constraints on the various unfound planet hypotheses. This work assesses the only known active high-i Centaur: C/2024 L1 (PANSTARRS). Our dynamical investigations show that L1 has not decreased its semimajor axis in at least the past 1810 years, unlike all known TNO-derived active Centaurs at lower-i whose activity initiates after orbital changes. We also imaged L1 on five nights in the year before its April 2025 perihelion and found its brightness and optical colors to be stable and that its tail grew steadily. While this is like many Halley-type comets, few active low-i Centaurs have such stable activity. We thus propose that L1 is a captured Oort Cloud comet analogous to lower perihelion Halley-types and is thus a highly attractive target for large aperture follow up. The number and active fraction of these high-i objects should help us discern their origins and survey telescopes like Roman and Rubin could help us figure out where in the Outer Solar System the mystery lies.
#### Stripped White Dwarfs as Spectroscopic Clocks: Abundance Transitions in Partial Tidal Disruptions
 - **Authors:** Taylor Yi-Ching Chong, Jin-Hong Chen, Lixin Dai
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19867

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19867

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate partial tidal disruptions of He and CO white dwarfs (WDs) by a $10^{4}\,M_{\odot}$ intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) using three-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations. We construct realistic WD structures and abundance profiles with MESA and map them into FLASH to follow the remnant response and the time-dependent composition of the fallback debris in single pericentre passages on tightly bound orbits ($e=0.9$). The element-specific fallback rates exhibit pre-peak compositional transitions that trace the WD's stratified layers. In the grazing encounter ($\beta=0.55$), hydrogen dominates the earliest returning debris, followed by helium, showing a H$\rightarrow$He transition. Deeper encounters ($\beta=0.7$ and $0.8$) penetrate the CO core and produce a He$\rightarrow$C transition, that occurs earlier relative to peak fallback and is stronger in the deeper encounter. These signatures provide a potential spectroscopic diagnostic for identifying CO-WD tidal disruption events (TDEs). We then develop a semi-analytic model for repeated tidal stripping and predict passage-to-passage compositional evolution, in which the stripped material becomes progressively enriched in heavier elements as deeper layers are exposed, leading to stronger and earlier abundance transitions in later flares. We find that WDs can survive several dozen ($\sim 30$-$80$) passages, depending on their initial mass and compactness. Our results link time-dependent abundance signatures to repeating WD disruptions and provide testable observable predictions for identifying IMBH-powered transients.
#### Inferring accretion bursts in intermediate-mass protostars from asteroseismic imprints
 - **Authors:** Johannes Jørgensen, Eduard Vorobyov, Konstanze Zwintz, Ebraheem Farag, Adolfo Carvalho, Jan Henneco
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20154

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20154

 - **Abstract**
 During star formation, objects may gain the majority of their final mass through regular and intense bursts of mass accretion. For bursts of the FU Orionis-type (FUor), accretion rates can climb to $10^{-3} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$ during short windows of time, amounting to just a few percent of the main accretion phase. To study the occurrence of such events observationally, signatures of the current accretion bursts and tracers of recent bursts are crucial. We investigate the potential to identify accretion bursts in protostars and pre-main sequence stars of intermediate mass using asteroseismology. We use the stellar structure and evolution code MESA to evolve stellar models from the protostellar seed phase of $0.01 \ \text{M}_\odot$ and $1.5 \ \text{R}_\odot$ up to $4.0 \ \text{M}_\odot$ with a constant accretion rate of $10^{-6} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$. A single accretion burst is initialized during the accretion phase. The stellar oscillation code GYRE is employed to compute the resulting asteroseismic properties of the models. Accretion bursts of up to $10^{-3} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$ are sufficient to cause the convective cores of the models to recede, leaving behind a chemical trace which manifests itself in the form of quasi-periodic dips in the g-mode period spacing. This effect is strongest during the outburst, but also lingers for up to 475 years before the convective core is re-established and the dips are gone. Our experiment shows that a single accretion burst leaves an imprint in the deep interiors of protostars and pre-MS stars of intermediate mass, and that this imprint can be captured with asteroseismology. Our results are most conclusive for burst magnitudes greater than $1\times10^{-4} \ \text{M}_\odot \text{yr}^{-1}$, corresponding to FUor-type bursts.
#### Demographic dynamics of the Japanese astronomy and astrophysics publication community during 1996-2025
 - **Authors:** Hideaki Fujiwara
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Physics and Society (physics.soc-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20357

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20357

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the demographic evolution of the Japanese astronomy and astrophysics publication community during 1996-2025 using bibliometric data from 23,274 publications in major journals in the field. The number of active Japan-affiliated authors increased from approximately 600 in the late 1990s to more than 2,000 by the early 2020s, while annual publication output reached approximately 1,400 by 2025. Participation was highly heterogeneous, with 46% of authors observed in only one publication year within the study period and a smaller core showing long observed participation periods. We define the Community Renewal Rate (CRR) as a community-level measure of renewal, calculated as the fraction of active authors not observed during a specified preceding interval. The 5-year CRR typically remained near one quarter of the active community, with annual values ranging from approximately 21% to 33%, indicating persistent renewal despite substantial community growth. An order-of-magnitude comparison suggests that newly entering students account for only part of the renewal-author population. The community therefore expanded while sustaining both continuity through authors with long observed participation periods and renewal through recurring changes in active membership.

 - **Abstract**
 The $Gaia$ high-resolution spectroscopic observations using Radial Velocity Spectrometer offers a unique opportunity to study stellar Ca II infrared triplet (IRT) activity. A key step in estimating activity indices is subtracting the photospheric contribution. The canonical template-based method often fails because, for many stars, the observed line cores are deeper than the model predictions, which prevents valid activity measurements. In this work, we explore an alternative approach that constructs a baseline from less active stars as a function of effective temperature (and metallicity) to represent the photospheric contribution. Our results show that, for dwarf stars, compared with the canonical method, the baseline method not only yields far more objects with valid activity measurements, but also avoids many extremely low and unreliable values (e.g., log$R'_{\rm IRT} \lesssim -6$). Our findings suggest that the baseline method can serve as a robust alternative for deriving accurate activity estimates, and will be particularly useful for obtaining activity indices for tens of millions of stars with the upcoming $Gaia$ Data Release 4.
#### A 3+1 formalism for self-similar expanding grids in computational relativistic and Newtonian magnetohydrodynamics
 - **Authors:** N. Bucciantini (INAF- Arcetri), L. Del Zanna (UniFi)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20865

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20865

 - **Abstract**
 Astrophysical systems frequently undergo spatial expansion during their evolution. Simulating these systems with standard conservative codes for hydrodynamics and magnetohydrodynamics using fixed grids is challenging, particularly when attempting to preserve high resolution across vast changes in physical scale. While moving or expanding meshes, or adapting mesh refinement, can address this issue, they introduce computational complexity and are notoriously difficult to implement robustly. We aim to overcome these barriers by providing an easily implementable strategy that empowers the community to achieve high-fidelity results and establishes expanding-grid approaches as a highly accessible standard practice. Leveraging the gauge freedom of the 3+1 formalism for MHD on curved manifolds, a foundation common to all general relativistic fluid dynamics codes, we demonstrate how an expanding coordinate system can be implemented purely through a simple modification of the underlying metric. This requires zero modifications to the numerical mesh or the core structure of the code, and the necessary addition or modification of metric terms can be easily accomplished even in Newtonian MHD. We apply this framework to standard test problems in both relativistic and Newtonian hydrodynamics and MHD, directly comparing the results of fixed and expanding coordinates. Our tests demonstrate the full consistency, accuracy, and robustness of this metric-based expansion approach, as the original conservative formulations is fully preserved. Through a simple coordinate transformation and a re-definition of the metric tensor, any general relativistic MHD code can be effortlessly transformed into an expanding-grid code. This approach elegantly guarantees both fundamental conservation properties and the solenoidal condition for the magnetic field, and can be readily extended to include non-ideal effects.

 - **Abstract**
 JWST observations have revealed evidence for NH$_4^+$ in the ices of the Chamaeleon I dark molecular cloud, with HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ likely counter ions. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated that ammonium formate and ammonium cyanate can form through acid-base reactions involving NH$_3$ at cryogenic temperatures. The formation of these two ammonium salts are simulated to investigate their formation in dark-cloud ices and assess their sensitivity to uncertainties in the chemical reaction network, including the recently considered HOCO-mediated pathway for CO$_2$ formation, as well as variations in the cosmic-ray ionization rate, cloud collapse timescale and density, and elemental depletion. Our modeling framework consists of microscopic kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations of grain-surface chemistry augmented by UCLCHEM gas-phase and physical cloud evolution simulations. The microscopic KMC method provides for explicit simulation of the location, movement and reaction of ice species, making it particularly well-suited to model acid-base chemistry of NH$_4^+$-salt formation, occurring within the ice mantle over extended timescales. The simulations produce the canonical ice-evolution sequence, with an initially H$_2$O-rich ice followed by a layer dominated by CO and CO$_2$. Salt production is dominated by NH$_4^+$HCOO$^-$, which is found to form predominantly during the H$_2$O-rich stage as its production is limited by the availability of NH$_3$. NH$_4^+$OCN$^-$ is found to form later at the interface between the H$_2$O- and CO-rich ice when the formation of its precursor acid HNCO is enabled. Despite the variations explored, the total NH$_4^+$ abundance, particularly relative to H$_2$O, remains remarkably robust and consistent with the observed abundance. Depending on the variation, a redistribution between counterions HCOO$^-$ and OCN$^-$ is observed.
#### Gravitational Waves from Population III Black Hole Mergers in Primordial Gas Disks: Constraining Astrophysical Scenarios with the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna and the Einstein Telescope
 - **Authors:** Maulik Bhatt, Lucio Mayer, Pedro R. Capelo, Simona Gallerani, Andrea Pallottini
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21580

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21580

 - **Abstract**
 The first generation of metal-free Population III (Pop~III) stars provides a possible formation channel for the black hole (BH) seeds in the early Universe. Their remnants may grow through gas accretion and mergers, producing gravitational wave (GW) signals detectable by future observatories. We develop a semi-analytical model of fragmentation in a primordial self-gravitating gas disk within minihaloes at $z > 15$, motivated by high-resolution cosmological simulations. The resulting Pop~III stars form around a central primary, some leaving massive BH remnants that migrate towards the central BH through dynamical friction from gas and dark matter, while the central BH grows through accretion and mergers. For a $10^6~{\rm M_\odot}$ halo at $z=20$, we explore how the initial mass function (IMF) and BH accretion efficiency affect detectability with the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) and the Einstein Telescope (ET). We find that both detectors can potentially reveal mergers of Pop III remnant BHs, but favor different astrophysical conditions. LISA pinpoints more massive central BHs ($\sim10^3$-$10^4 {\rm M_\odot}$), grown rapidly through mutual mergers alongside efficient gas accretion ($f_{\rm Edd}=1$), and a relatively top-heavy IMF ($\alpha=0.8$). In contrast, detectability with ET favors lower-mass BHs, with less efficient accretion ($f_{\rm Edd}=0.01$) and a steeper IMF ($\alpha=1.5$). These results highlight the potential of LISA and ET to probe Pop~III BH formation, growth, and mergers across different regions of parameter spaces, jointly constraining Pop~III star formation and early BH seed evolution.
#### Compositions of Exoplanetary Debris Shaped by Milky Way Stellar Abundance Patterns
 - **Authors:** Isabella L. Trierweiler, Kendra Nguyen, Lou Baya Ould Rouis, Pratik Gandhi, Laura K. Rogers, Carey M. Lisse, Malena Rice, Damanveer S. Grewal, Edward Young
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21583

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21583

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) predicts large-scale variations in exoplanet compositions with host star elemental abundances, reflecting the Milky Way's history of star formation and elemental enrichment from stellar feedback. We investigate the impact of GCE on the compositions of exoplanetary bodies using polluted white dwarfs (WDs), which probe the bulk elemental abundances of planetary debris. Using a literature sample of 70 polluted WDs, we calculate iron mass fractions for the accreted bodies and compare these to tracers of the WD progenitor's initial abundances, including age and kinematics. We demonstrate predicted trends using a sample of main sequence stars, and we show that these are generally reflected in the polluted WD sample. In particular, H-dominated WDs show preferentially larger iron mass fractions amongst younger and less dynamically excited systems. This trend is weaker amongst He-dominated WDs, and we show that this may be attributed to uncertainties in their observed phases of accretion. Continuing to combine WD pollution compositions with stellar parameters and kinematics could be a valuable way to constrain large-scale versus local drivers of planetary compositions.
