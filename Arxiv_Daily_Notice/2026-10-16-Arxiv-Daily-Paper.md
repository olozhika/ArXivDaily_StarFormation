#### The impact of nonlinear Landau damping on cosmic ray streaming and the role of external turbulence
 - **Authors:** Rouven Lemmerz, Mohamad Shalaby, Ellen Zweibel, Christoph Pfrommer, Timon Thomas
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Plasma Physics (physics.plasm-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20695

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20695

 - **Abstract**
 Cosmic rays (CRs) streaming faster than the Alfvén speed generate Alfvén waves that scatter them. The saturated wave amplitude is regulated by a damping mechanism. This damping controls the CR scattering rate and drift velocity, and thus the rate at which CRs transfer momentum and energy to the ambient plasma, which sets the strength of CR feedback. In the ionized interstellar medium, nonlinear Landau damping (NLLD) is commonly invoked as the dominant damping mechanism for Alfvén waves. We test this assumption with fluid-PIC simulations in which thermal ions and electrons are modeled as fluids with optional Landau closures, allowing us to compare otherwise identical runs with and without NLLD. When waves are self-generated by monoenergetic CRs, runs with and without Landau closures are indistinguishable: NLLD is inefficient and the CRs isotropize in the wave frame. Once high-amplitude Alfvén waves mimicking external turbulence are seeded, NLLD curbs wave growth and equalizes forward- and backward-propagating waves. We show that the saturated drift velocity, which falls below the Alfvén speed, can be accurately predicted from the wave spectra alone. If the CR distribution is undersampled, a numerical instability corrupts the largest resonant scales: unphysical, unresolved pitch-angle gradients resonantly generate backward-propagating waves, driving an inverse cascade that should be absent from purely streaming-driven waves. While power-law CR distributions may permit stronger NLLD, our results indicate that NLLD is unlikely to be an intrinsic saturation mechanism of the streaming instability; instead, its effectiveness hinges on the interplay of streaming-generated modes with those of external origin.

 - **Abstract**
 Tailored narrow-band photometric observations are an efficient way to measure the metallicity of Milky Way stars. The Pristine survey has been mapping the northern sky since 2015 using narrow-band $CaHK$ photometry obtained with the MegaCam imager on the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope (CFHT). Data Release 1 (DR1) provided a high-precision absolute calibration of the survey observed before October 2022 using \Gaia DR3 XP synthetic photometry, yielding photometric metallicities over $\sim$6,500 deg$^2$, but was limited in depth to the availability of \Gaia spectro-photometry ($G \lesssim 17.65$). We present the Pristine Data Release 2 (DR2) that includes all Pristine observations until January 2025, corresponding to an additional 1,500\,deg$^2$ of coverage, and including all faint sources down to $G\sim20.5$. This release aims to exploit the full depth of the Pristine observations to uncover the faint metal-poor halo. We process all images observed from the start of the survey in March 2015 through to January 2025 following the methodology presented in DR1. The core of the calibration utilizes the subset of bright stars with \Gaia synthetic $CaHK_\mathrm{syn}$ magnitudes to train the \texttt{PhotCalib} neural network. We then derive photometric metallicities using the established Pristine ($CaHK, G, G_{\rm BP}, G_{\rm RP}$) to [Fe/H] model. The resulting catalogue covers approximately $\sim$ 8,000 deg$^{2}$ and provides photometric metallicities for 15 millions FGK stars, which show good agreement with recently published, independent Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) metallicities. With its increased coverage and access to a significantly fainter regime compared to DR1, DR2 significantly increases the volume of the survey. We report more than 200,000 and 6,500 very and extremely metal-poor star candidates, respectively.

 - **Abstract**
 Existing sub-mm observatories such as ALMA have provided statistics on the planet-forming mass reservoirs within circumstellar disks around stars in stellar clusters out to distances of $\sim$400pc. However, these disks are not where most planets form. Many stars, and hence planets, form in more massive clusters, which are only found at larger distances. Environmental conditions that affect disks - such as UV radiation field, ambient gaseous mass reservoir, and stellar density - change significantly as a function of cluster mass, implying that our current knowledge of the disk population from current facilities is severely biased. Characterising planet formation in stellar clusters towards the peak of the cluster mass function requires determining the distribution of planet forming disk masses out to $\sim$3kpc, which encapsulates the nearby parts of the Sagittarius arm of the Milky Way and contains multiple massive stellar clusters. ALMA2040 will provide the leap in sensitivity needed to move beyond detections of only a handful of disks and robustly measure the slope and median of the disk mass distribution at these distances. This will mark the transition from studying individual detections to population-level studies of planet formation across diverse cluster environments. This has synergy with existing and upcoming measurements of accretion rates in these distant clusters, which combined provide a measure of the accretion timescale, enabling us to distinguish various evolutionary pathways and assess how they depend on cluster mass and star-forming environment.

 - **Abstract**
 Perhaps the most visually striking phenomenon during star formation is the ejection of material along the direction of the protostar's rotational axis. Observed in the form of molecular outflows and/or atomic jets, these features carry away mass, energy and angular momentum from the system. Given the known dust content of protoplanetary disks, and the energetics of outflows, it has been suggested that it should be possible to lift dust from disks via winds, yet limited observational evidence has been presented in the literature. Any detection of dust within protostellar outflows not only puts constraints on the dust content in outflows, but if a sufficient amount of dust can be removed from the system, the mass budget for planet formation could also be affected. We present re-imaging of archival, multi-frequency dust continuum observations taken with the Atacama Large Millimeter/sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) towards the HH212 protostellar system. We detect an extension in the dust emission, and using multi-frequency data in Bands 7 and 9, we estimate the spectral index of the emitting dust and the dust mass in the wind launching region. We find a small but still significant amount of dust mass coincident with the molecular outflow cavity. This finding is assessed in relation to the hypothesis that the outflow could be capable of transporting dust away from the HH212 protoplanetary disk.
#### Beyond Mergers: the Role of Metallicity in Forming a Reservoir of Massive and Detached Binary Black Hole Systems
 - **Authors:** Siyuan Chen, Monica Gallegos-Garcia
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20736

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20736

 - **Abstract**
 Isolated binary evolution is a key pathway to forming binary black hole (BBH) mergers. However, the observed gravitational-wave population indicates it may not account for all mergers, especially those with high primary black hole mass ($\gtrsim 30 M_{\odot}$). Using the binary population synthesis code \texttt{POSYDON}, we simulate populations of binary systems at eight metallicities from $10^{-4}$ to $2\,Z_\odot$ and classify each BH-forming binary as a merging BBH, a detached BBH, or a system disrupted during BH formation. We find that metallicity shapes these populations differently, especially for high BH masses ($\gtrsim 25 M_{\odot}$). At lower metallicities, between $10^{-2}$ and $0.45\,Z_\odot$, massive BHs continue to form but become increasingly unlikely to merge: the number of massive merging BBHs declines sharply, while the detached population remains abundant. For $25$--$35\,M_\odot$ BHs, the detached-to-merging ratio increases by more than two orders of magnitude across this metallicity range. After weighting by cosmic star formation, massive BHs are found predominantly in detached binaries with orbital periods $\gtrsim 10^2$ days. Therefore, isolated binary evolution can form massive BBHs even if it rarely produces massive BBH mergers, leaving behind a massive and detached population that may be driven to merger through dynamical interactions in dense stellar environments.

 - **Authors:** Nanase Harada, Yuri Nishimura, Toshiki Saito, Mario Tafalla, Jin Koda, Doris Arzoumanian, Sophia K. Stuber, Akihiko Hirota, Yoshimasa Watanabe

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21142

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21142

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic observations have shown that N2H+ intensities preferentially trace star-forming dense molecular gas compared with HCN or HCO+, which are conventionally used as dense-gas tracers in extragalactic studies. In this study, we aim to verify this trend in the nearby star-forming galaxy M83. Using ALMA, we observed the J=1-0 transitions of N2H+ and HCN, along with the 1-mm and 3-mm continuum, and analyzed the archival CO(1-0) data. The data achieved a spatial resolution of 48 pc in the southwestern bar-end region of M83, covering an area of 2.1 kpc x 1.8 kpc. N2H+ and HCN intensities are approximately linearly correlated, indicating that HCN luminosity can still reasonably predict the amount of dense gas as well as N2H+ luminosity does. However, in regions with high column densities, N2H+ becomes a super-linear function of HCN, suggesting that the N2H+ emission is more sensitive to dense gas than HCN. We employed a radiative transfer model that predicts line intensities as a function of column density, adopting parametric column density probability distribution functions (N-PDFs) to describe the underlying column density distribution. The model demonstrates that the observed linear or superlinear trend can be explained by a log-normal distribution only or a log-normal and a power-law tail distribution in the N-PDF. Furthermore, the N2H+ intensity is significantly more sensitive to the power-law tail than the HCN intensity. In addition, our analysis incorporating archival data shows that N2H+ is a less reliable tracer of dense gas in galactic center regions, where warmer temperatures may promote N2H+ destruction.

 - **Abstract**
 Radio interferometers now deliver wide bandwidths and high sensitivity, and with them data volumes that are awkward to reduce by hand and slow to reduce automatically. The limit is not always the algorithms themselves but the way the work is laid out across the computing available. We present CHARIZARD, a pipeline that carries a raw measurement set through flagging, cross-calibration, self-calibration and imaging to full-Stokes images, and which is built around distributing that work. The observing band is repartitioned into sub-bands that stay independent through cross-calibration, the unit of work becomes the target field and sub-band pair at self-calibration, and stages touching disjoint data are run together. A $1.5$~TB track completes $48.6$ core-hours of work in $18.4$~hours with self-calibration and polarization calibration. The next generation of the radio telescopes will not be short of data. CHARIZARD attempts to show how the distribution of the work provides a useful alternative for handling the staggering processing requirements.
