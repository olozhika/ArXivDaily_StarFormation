#### Missing Mergers: On the Unexpected Delay-Time Distribution of Binary Neutron Star Mergers
 - **Authors:** Ryan J. Foley
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40782

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40782

 - **Abstract**
 The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) collaboration has detected two binary neutron star (BNS) mergers, GW170817 and GW190425. While no clear electromagnetic counterpart was discovered for GW190425, a coincident counterpart to GW170817 (GRB170817A, AT 2017gfo) was discovered in an early-type galaxy, NGC 4993. Using broad-band photometry and an integrated optical spectrum, we measure a mass-weighted age of $12.4^{+0.5}_{-0.7}$ Gyr (90% credible) for NGC 4993, which is an estimate of the delay time between star formation and merger for the GW170817 progenitor. BNS systems are expected to have a power-law delay-time distribution (DTD) with a minimum delay time, $t_{\rm min}$, of ~10 Myr and index $\alpha \approx -1$. This canonical DTD, convolved with the cosmic star-formation history, predicts ~16 BNS mergers at delay times of ~100 Myr for every BNS merger at 10 Gyr; under it, the chance of detecting even one merger as old as GW170817 is ~1.8%. The long delay time of GW170817 therefore implies many "missing" shorter-delay mergers if the canonical DTD holds. A DTD with $t_{\rm min} = 12.6$ Gyr and $\alpha = -1$ is favored over the canonical DTD by a factor of 40-140, and over short gamma-ray burst and Galactic double-NS DTDs by factors of 80-280. These results are in tension with current population synthesis models, the Milky Way double NS population, and the SGRB population. Explaining the LVK population requires either a dramatically different separation distribution than commonly assumed, an additional channel with a long delay time and comparable rate to an isolated-binary formation scenario, or a large population of BNS mergers missed in the LVK analysis. This result has wide-ranging implications for binary-population synthesis, chemical enrichment models, connections between BNS mergers and SGRBs, and future gravitational wave observations.
#### Bridging Scales: Multi-Zone GRMHD-Informed Anisotropic AGN Feedback in Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Kung-Yi Su, Hyerin Cho, Priyamvada Natarajan, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère, Ramesh Narayan, Angelo Ricarte, Antonio J. Porras-Valverde, Cora Prather, Philip F. Hopkins
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40784

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40784

 - **Abstract**
 We present a galaxy-scale black hole (BH) accretion and feedback model incorporating results from multi-zone general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations spanning the event horizon to the Bondi radius. The model replaces a fixed suppression of the Bondi rate with a nuclear-environment-dependent factor, $\kappa^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$, set by the local ratio of Bondi to gravitational radius, and distributes outflow mass, kinetic energy, and thermal energy according to the angular structure measured in the large-dynamic-range GRMHD calculations. We apply it to idealized isolated simulations of an M87*-like massive elliptical and a Milky Way/Sgr A*-like galaxy, spanning over three orders of magnitude in BH mass without tuning accretion or feedback efficiencies to match observables. In the M87*-like system, the GRMHD-informed angle-dependent feedback model suppresses late-time stellar mass growth more effectively than the isotropic reference model, despite similar long-term BH accretion rates. Varying the uncertain mass loading of the collimated polar component has a modest impact on BH growth and star formation, indicating that feedback anisotropy matters more than its detailed mass loading. We find that $\kappa^{\rm hot}_{\rm cho}$ varies with nuclear environment and time, yielding BH growth histories markedly different from fixed-suppression models. For Sgr A*, this variable factor yields an accretion rate more consistent with Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) constraints than a fixed, suppressed Bondi model. These results show that nuclear-environment-dependent accretion and feedback angular structure affect BH-galaxy evolution, requiring subgrid models to include more than a universal Bondi suppression factor and a scalar feedback efficiency.
#### Kinematic Decomposition of the BCG and ICL in Abell S1063 and MACS J1206
 - **Authors:** Hyungjin Joo, Myungkook James Jee
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40792

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40792

 - **Abstract**
 The brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) and the intracluster light (ICL) overlap in space and merge smoothly in surface brightness. Beyond the nearest clusters, measurements of the ICL have therefore relied on photometric definitions. We present a spectral decomposition of the light in cluster cores into BCG and ICL, using deep VLT/MUSE integral-field spectroscopy of Abell S1063 (z=0.35) and MACS J1206.2$-$0847 (z=0.44). Spectral templates of the two components are derived iteratively from the BCG center and the ICL-dominated outskirts, and their weighted sum is fitted to the spectrum of every spatial bin. This enables us to measure the kinematics and the light of the BCG and ICL separately without assuming a boundary or a functional form for their light profiles. The velocity dispersions of the BCG and ICL are $316\pm5$ and $753\pm25\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$, respectively, in AS1063, whereas we obtain $313\pm5$ and $695^{+47}_{-45}\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$ in MACS1206. The ICL is blueshifted relative to the BCG by $75\pm22$ and $148\pm38\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$, respectively, and is ~0.7 dex more metal-poor, at a combined statistical significance of ~$2\sigma$. Defining the ICL as the light fainter than $\mu_{B}=25\ \mathrm{mag\ arcsec^{-2}}$ assigns two-thirds to three-quarters of the kinematically defined ICL to the BCG, while a double Sersic model fits the total profiles well but leaves the division between the two components poorly constrained given the depth and resolution of the current MUSE data. Our kinematic decomposition provides a direct, physically motivated reference for photometric definitions of the ICL.
#### The Importance of Curium-250 to the $γ$-Ray Signature of $r$-Process Remnants
 - **Authors:** Axel Gross, Samuel Cupp, G. Wendell Misch, Matthew R. Mumpower
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Nuclear Theory (nucl-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40907

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40907

 - **Abstract**
 Identifying the astrophysical sites of the heavy rapid neutron-capture process ($r$-process) remains one of the most prominent unsolved problems in nuclear astrophysics. Emitted $\gamma$-rays from the decay of unstable nuclei have been identified as a potential observable marker on both prompt and delayed timescales which could provide conclusive evidence of the presence of $r$-process activity. In this work, we identify and discuss the importance of $^{250}$Cm to the $\gamma$-ray spectra of remnants, showing that the decay spectrum of $^{250}$Cm dominates the $\gamma$-ray spectra of $r$-process ejecta across a broad timescale ($\sim$ 2,000 - 20,000 years). We highlight the uncertain nature of this spectra: $^{250}$Cm may decay via $\alpha$-decay, $\beta$-decay, and spontaneous fission, but the decay branchings are not experimentally known, and each decay channel produces unique spectral lines which may be observable. We examine the potential for observation of the most prominent spectral signatures, finding that for conventional neutron star merger remnants, $\gamma$-ray lines will be detectable in current and future detectors only in the very near galaxy ($\sim$ 1 kpc), but other sites which could produce a larger ejecta mass may have greater observable distances. Our findings highlight the potential of actinide decay chains to indicate r-process nucleosynthesis and further underscore the necessity of measuring the nuclear properties that define their characteristic spectral signatures.
#### MAGIKS: Multimodal Alignment and Gravitational-Wave-Conditioned Interaction for Kilonova Search
 - **Authors:** Bingzhou Gao, Weichangfeng Guo, Jingqi Zhang, Alistair McLeod, Zhengyan Liu, Linqing Wen, Wen Zhao
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41119

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41119

 - **Abstract**
 We present MAGIKS, a multimodal framework for gravitational-wave (GW)-triggered kilonova identification that formulates counterpart search as query-conditioned gallery retrieval. Following an align-before-fuse design, MAGIKS combines GW source parameters and sky-localization maps with irregularly sampled multiband optical light curves and sky coordinates. A time-aware supervised contrastive objective provides coarse cross-modal alignment, while dual cross-attention evaluates consistency between optical coordinates and GW localization, and between GW parameters and optical evolution. We train and evaluate MAGIKS on simulated binary neutron star and neutron star-black hole mergers, Rubin Observatory kilonova light curves from regular survey observations and target-of-opportunity follow-up, GW-detected mergers without Rubin-detectable kilonova counterparts, and realistic non-kilonova contaminants. In independent galleries with one true counterpart and time- and sky-compatible distractors, MAGIKS outperforms optical-only and sky-localization-only ranking, with increasing gains as galleries grow. At 5000 candidates, it ranks the counterpart first for 39.8% of queries, versus 4.8% and 0.2% for these baselines. In a GW170817 study, we generate counterfactual Rubin observations using released GW parameters and three-dimensional localization, the measured counterpart position, and host distance. At the same gallery size, MAGIKS achieves top-1 and top-10 retrieval rates of 87.3% and 95.7%. It correctly classifies 94.2% of associated and non-associated GW-optical pairs. In a separate crossed-pair experiment, its matching score responds significantly to controlled changes in all five tested GW parameters. These results support GW-conditioned kilonova candidate prioritisation for future multi-messenger follow-up.
#### HAWC, Fermi-LAT and Chandra Detections of High Energy Particles in the S106 Star Forming Region
 - **Authors:** Rishi Babu, Stephen DiKerby, Tulun Ergin, Shuo Zhang, Mehr Un Nisa, the HAWC Collaboration
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41163

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41163

 - **Abstract**
 Local star-forming regions have recently been the subject of scrutiny as cosmic-ray accelerators and sources of ultra-high-energy gamma-ray emissions. The Cygnus-X region is one of the richest star forming regions in the galaxy and over the last few decades has been extensively studied. Here we report the detection of a star-forming region located in the southern part of Cygnus-X, Sharpless 106 (S106), using the HAWC Observatory. The S106 region is spatially located near the cataloged gamma-ray source 1LHAASO J2027+3657. In this work, we perform a multi-wavelength analysis of S106, by combining HAWC data with re-analysis of Fermi-LAT and archival Chandra data. We construct a fully leptonic model for the multi-wavelength emission from S106 from a single population of high-energy leptons, with the gamma-rays greater than 1 TeV being produced by inverse Compton scattering of the locally-produced IR photon field from S106 itself. Our models show that hadronic mechanisms are not always required to explain the observed flux from gamma-ray star-forming regions and that understanding the specific details of local photon fields is crucial to modeling the multi-wavelength emission from star-forming regions.
#### Effect of quark-hadron phase transitions on the neutron star mass distribution
 - **Authors:** Renyu Luo, XuRun Huang, Shuai Zha, Guoliang Lü, Chunhua Zhu, Sufen Guo, Helei Liu, Zhijun Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41242

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41242

 - **Abstract**
 The neutron star (NS) mass distribution serves as a crucial probe of the dense matter equation of state (EOS) and core-collapse supernova explosion mechanisms. Although observations reveal a bimodal NS mass distribution, existing theoretical simulations often struggle to naturally reproduce the high-mass peak, and the impact of the quark-hadron phase transition (PT) in high-density nuclear matter remains largely unexplored. Employing the 1D general-relativistic hydrodynamics code GR1D with hybrid EOSs incorporating a first-order PT (RDF1.1 and RDF1.2), we systematically simulate core-collapse supernovae for progenitors with zero-age main-sequence masses ranging from 13 to 120 $M_\odot$. Our results reveal a threefold impact of the PT onset density on the final supernova fate: in low-compactness progenitors, an early PT truncates accretion and produces less massive NSs; in the critical-compactness regime, it facilitates successful explosions; and in high-compactness progenitors, it accelerates black hole formation. Compared with the $\sim 1.4\,M_\odot$ peak typical of the conventional core-collapse scenario, the inclusion of the PT produces NSs with masses centered around $1.70\,M_\odot$ for RDF1.1 and within $1.55$--$1.70\,M_\odot$ for RDF1.2. Furthermore, we identify a novel formation channel whereby low-compactness progenitors with failed initial shocks can form more massive NSs via a PT-induced second shock. Due to the fixed nature of the PT boundary, the final NS masses exhibit a pronounced clustering effect. This study elucidates the critical role of the quark-hadron PT in shaping the NS mass distribution, providing important astrophysical constraints on the properties of high-density nuclear matter and the formation mechanism of massive NSs.
#### Kinematic Map of the Galactic Bulge Using HST Proper Motions
 - **Authors:** Zhenhao Wang, Shude Mao, Hongjing Yang, Andrzej Udalski, Weicheng Zang, T. Dex Bhadra
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41395

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41395

 - **Abstract**
 Constraining the Galactic bulge's non-axisymmetric dynamics requires proper motions in deep fields across many sightlines, yet crowding and extinction limit such measurements. Using multi-epoch observations of 27 fields in the southern Galactic bulge (-6$^\circ$ < l < 4$^\circ$, -5$^\circ$ < b < -1$^\circ$) from the ultraviolet-visible (UVIS) channel of Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3) on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) with time baselines of 3 to 13 years, and combining them with the absolute proper motion reference frame provided by Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3), we measure absolute proper motions for ~ 400,000 stars. Colour-magnitude diagrams are used to separate bulge stars from foreground disc stars, identify red clump stars and correct for interstellar extinction and reddening. Statistical analysis shows that the mean proper motion in Galactic latitude is consistent with that of the central supermassive black hole Sgr A*, while the mean proper motion in Galactic longitude is affected by disc stars but agrees with Sgr A* after extrapolation to the Galactic plane. Proper motion dispersions increase towards the Galactic centre and are asymmetric across Galactic longitude, with a steeper gradient for l < 0$^\circ$, revealing the bulge's non-axisymmetric bar structure. The velocity distribution shows clear anisotropy ($\sigma_l$/$\sigma_b$ = 1.131 $\pm$ 0.005). The normalised covariance is negative in the l > 0$^\circ$ region (~ -0.085) and close to zero in the l < 0$^\circ$ region. The proper motion distributions are non-Gaussian in both Galactic longitude and latitude, mainly due to contamination by foreground disc stars.
#### Resolving the Complex Line-Locking Structure in Quasar J004613+010425 with VLT/X-shooter Spectroscopy
 - **Authors:** Chen Chen, Wei-Jian Lu, Ying-Ru Lin, Bo Ma
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41427

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41427

 - **Abstract**
 Line-locking (LL) signatures in quasar absorption-line systems provide direct observational evidence for radiatively driven outflows. We present a detailed investigation of LL phenomena in the quasar SDSS J004613.54+010425.7 ($z_{\rm em} = 2.150$) using medium-resolution VLT/X-shooter spectra, as part of the TOLL project. Compared with previous studies based on Sloan Digital Sky Survey spectroscopy, the X-shooter data resolve the absorption complex into twelve narrow absorption line (NAL) components spanning $\sim$$2700$ km/s in the C IV, N V, Si IV, and Ly$\alpha$ transitions, a factor of $\sim$2.4 more components than identified from lower resolution spectra. We detect fourteen distinct LL pairs across three ionic species, forming one of the largest line-locking webs known to date: three Si IV pairs connecting components (2, 10), (4, 11), and (5, 12); six N V pairs organized into four groups; and five C IV pairs organized into three groups. Monte Carlo simulations show that the probability of this twelve-component LL web arising by chance is $< 10^{-5}$. Photoionization modeling with Cloudy yields ionization parameters of $\log U \approx -2.4$ to $-0.6$ and total hydrogen column densities of $\log N({\rm H}) \approx 18.4$-$20.6$ for the twelve components. A comparison of the properties of shadowed and shadowing clouds shows that ion column densities and ionization parameters typically agree within a factor of $\sim$3, broadly supporting the standard radiative acceleration picture, although several outliers suggest that multi-ion locking and cloud-cloud interactions may also regulate LL stability. These results demonstrate that medium- and high-resolution spectroscopy is essential for resolving the full complexity of line-locked quasar outflows.
#### Dark Halos, Intermediate-Mass BH and Nuclear Stellar Clusters in Dwarf Spheroidals
 - **Authors:** Rojin Bayati, Paolo Salucci

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41522

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41522

 - **Abstract**
 We exploit the halo densities of three dwarf spheroidals recently obtained to investigate their dark matter distribution. We address the well-known DM halo core-cusp issue by finding that the NFW $\Lambda$CDM halo profile is in strong disagreement with these inferred halo densities, which instead result very well-reproduced by the Burkert halo cored profile with the central density $\rho_0$ and the core radius $r_0$. #### Collisional excitation of HMgC$_3$N by He: rate coefficients and astrophysical implications
 - **Authors:** Liman Tian, Ziyu Xiang, Wenli Zou, Xiaolin Chen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41876

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41876

 - **Abstract**
 Reliable collisional data are essential for interpreting the rotational emission of HMgC$_3$N under the non-equilibrium conditions of molecular clouds. We investigate the rotational excitation and de-excitation of HMgC$_3$N induced by collisions with He, combining \textit{ab initio} calculations with quantum scattering theory. A two-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) for HMgC$_3$N--He was constructed using the CCSD(T)-F12A method in conjunction with the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set. Quantum close-coupling (CC) calculation were then performed to obtain state-to-state integral cross sections and temperature-dependent rate coefficients for rotational transitions with $\Delta j=\pm1$, $\pm2$, and $\pm3$, covering rotational levels up to $j=20$ and temperatures from $3$ to $50$~K. The rate coefficients exhibit a clear propensity for $\Delta j=\pm2$ transitions. The resulting collisional data were incorporated into radiative transfer modelling to constrain the physical conditions and column density of HMgC$_3$N from its observed spectral line emission. A $\chi^2$ minimization yields a best-fitting H$_2$ volume density of $n(\mathrm{H}_2)=1.233\times10^5~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ and column density of $N(\mathrm{HMgC}_3\mathrm{N})=1.831\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. The derived column density is in approximate agreement with the observational estimate of $(3.0\pm0.6)\times10^{12}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. These results provide new collisional data for non-LTE studies of HMgC$_3$N and support a more realistic treatment of its rotational excitation and radiative transfer in molecular clouds.

 We investigate past star formation in NGC 6357 using Gaia DR3 astrometry and Three-mm Ultimate Mopra Milky Way Survey (ThrUMMS) $^{12}$CO($J=1$--0) data. A Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) search independently recovers the previously cataloged cluster G352.8+0.6 in addition to Pismis 24, G353.1+0.6, and G353.2+0.6. #### Formation of Micron-sized Presolar Graphite Grains in Low-energy Supernova Ejecta
 - **Authors:** Kengo Shinoda, Ryo Tazaki, Takato Tokuno, Yudai Suwa
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41948

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41948

 - **Abstract**
 Presolar graphite grains with supernova isotopic signatures provide direct evidence that large graphite grains formed in supernova ejecta. However, theoretical calculations of dust formation in supernovae have typically predicted graphite grains smaller than $0.1\,{\rm \mu m}$, leaving unclear the physical conditions required to form those grains with radii of order $1\,{\rm \mu m}$. We systematically investigate graphite nucleation and growth in expanding supernova ejecta by varying the explosion energy, ejecta mass, initial ejecta radius, and ejected $^{56}$Ni mass. We use a simplified one-zone model that follows the temperature and density evolution including adiabatic cooling, radiative cooling, and heating by radioactive nickel. We show that the final graphite grain size is mainly determined by the product of the mass density of atomic carbon available for grain growth and the local cooling timescale, both evaluated at a reference temperature of $2000\,{\rm K}$. Our models show that graphite growth is favored by lower explosion energy, larger ejecta mass, and smaller initial ejecta radius, which keep the gas denser near the graphite-condensation temperature. When radioactive heating and radiative cooling are included, explosions with $E_{\rm exp}\sim10^{50}\,{\rm erg}$ and $M_{\rm Ni}\lesssim10^{-3}\,M_\odot$ can form $1$-$10\,\mu{\rm m}$ graphite grains in the ejected helium layer or carbon-oxygen core, whereas $E_{\rm exp}\sim10^{51}\,{\rm erg}$ models do not. These results suggest that micron-sized presolar supernova graphite preferentially forms in faint-supernova-like explosions with low explosion energies, little ejected nickel, and helium layer or carbon-oxygen core ejecta.
#### Distinguishing N$_2$-Dominated from O$_2$-Dominated Atmospheres on Terrestrial Exoplanets with Reflected-Light Spectroscopy
 - **Authors:** Nick Burri, Mario Damiano, Renyu Hu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42035

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42035

 - **Abstract**
 Reflected-light spectra of Earth-sized exoplanets promise to probe habitability and biosignatures, but interpreting atmospheric oxygen can be challenging because $O_2$ can arise from both biological and abiotic processes. In addition, reflected-light retrievals exhibit degeneracies among atmospheric composition, clouds, surface reflectance, and gravity. We use the Bayesian retrieval framework ExoReL$^\Re$, leveraging recent model developments that incorporate (i) $O_2$-$O_2$ collision-induced absorption (CIA), which produces the $O_4$ bands, and (ii) a wavelength-dependent (step-function) surface albedo parameterization. Focusing on a modern Earth analog, we compare retrievals with and without $O_2$-$O_2$ CIA using spectra within 0.25-1.8 $\mu$m, and perform signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) sweeps for two observing configurations motivated by the Habitable Worlds Observatory: a resolved-visible case ($R_{VIS}=140$) and a broadband-visible case (three broad bins). Omitting $O_2$-$O_2$ CIA allows the retrieval to fit the spectrum with an incorrect $O_2$-dominated atmosphere. Including $O_2$-$O_2$ CIA leverages the $O_4$ bands to break this degeneracy and recover the correct $N_2$-dominated, $O_2$-rich solution without requiring a tight mass prior. Adopting a robustness criterion of $\ge95\%$ posterior mass in the $N_2$-dominated region, both configurations require SNR $\gtrsim20$ at 0.75 $\mu$m (SNR $\approx14$ near the 1.27 $\mu$m $O_4$ band for $R_{NIR}=70$) to identify the correct dominant gas, with higher cloud fraction increasing this threshold. Reducing near-IR coverage to 1.4 $\mu$m prevents recovery of the correct $N_2$-dominated solution across the explored SNR range, and instead favors a spurious $O_2$-dominated, $CO_2$-rich atmosphere. These results support near-IR coverage to $\sim1.7$ $\mu$m and SNR $\gtrsim20$ at 0.75 $\mu$m for robust oxygen interpretation on Earth-like planets.
