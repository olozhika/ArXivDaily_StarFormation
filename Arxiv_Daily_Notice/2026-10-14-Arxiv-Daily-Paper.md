#### Stellar discs and intermediate-mass black holes in galactic nuclei II. Fragmenting the disc and forming the S-stars in a live nuclear star cluster
 - **Authors:** Taras Panamarev, Bence Kocsis
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14179

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14179

 - **Abstract**
 We present direct $N$-body simulations of a stellar disc around the supermassive black hole Sgr~A*, perturbed by an intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) and embedded in a live nuclear star cluster. A retrograde IMBH with $m_\bullet=2000\,M_\odot$, relative inclination $\theta_\bullet=150^\circ$, and initial semi-major axis $a_\bullet=0.15$~pc breaks the disc into three disc-like components and an angular momentum filament. The stochastic torques of the cluster, together with the IMBH's inward migration, increase the angular separation between these substructures. By ${\sim}5$~Myr, their separations become comparable to those among the observed Galactic-centre young star structures. The innermost ($a\le0.04$~pc) disc stars evolve into an S-star-like population. Relaxation driven by the cluster disperses their orbital planes, excites their eccentricities and depletes the `zone of avoidance' within ${\sim}10$~Myr even without an IMBH. The IMBH further accelerates their orbital-plane reorientation. However, the most eccentric S-stars may require an additional formation channel, such as the Hills mechanism. The distinctive signatures of the IMBH are the well-separated disc components and the eccentricity excitation confined to the radial range swept by its orbit. Dynamical friction from the cluster reduces the orbital size of a $2000\,M_\odot$ IMBH initially at $0.15$~pc by more than a factor of two within $10$~Myr. Prograde IMBHs tend to align with the stellar disc, whereas a $2000\,M_\odot$ retrograde IMBH can anti-align with the radially overlapping disc stars. We also quantify how the IMBH affects the supply of stars to the loss cone and the rates of stellar tidal disruptions and binary separations.
#### What does self-supervision add to engineered features for stellar light curves?
 - **Authors:** Zijun Liu, Yue Ma, Christopher A. Theissen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14283

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14283

 - **Abstract**
 TESS light curves encode a star's pulsation, rotation, binarity and oscillations, among other physical properties, but the survey produces far more light curves than can be labelled by hand. Astronomers have engineered a few dozen physically motivated features from them, which together remain a strong baseline. Previous work has trained encoders on light curves and judged them by their own downstream scores, but has not asked what they add to the engineered features. We pre-train a convolutional encoder on unlabelled TESS light curves to predict each window's latent representation from those of its neighbours. We then freeze it, add its features to the 25 engineered features, and read the combination out with one fixed linear probe on ten tasks: five variability classes, four asteroseismic targets and rotation period. Adding the learned features improves the readout on 7 of 10 tasks over the engineered features alone, where an untrained encoder does not. The combination also outperforms two networks trained end-to-end on the same labelled stars on most tasks. An encoder trained without labels therefore gives astronomers new features that complement the ones they already use rather than replace them.

 - **Abstract**

#### Anisotropic Radiation in Super-Eddington Active Galactic Nuclei: The Responsivity of Broad-Line Regions to Continuum Variability
 - **Authors:** Feng-Na Fang, Pu Du, Jian-Min Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15255

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15255

 - **Abstract**
 Anisotropic ionizing radiation is a natural theoretical expectation for super-Eddington accretion in active galactic nuclei (AGNs), implying that the ionizing flux incident on the broad-line region (BLR) clouds differs from that measured along the observer's line of sight. Reverberation mapping studies have revealed systematically shortened time lags of the broad H$\beta$ emission line relative to continuum variations in super-Eddington AGNs, a phenomenon that can be naturally interpreted as a consequence of anisotropic ionizing continuum radiation. To further investigate this anisotropy, we perform a systematic analysis of the H$\beta$ line responsivity, defined as the ratio of the variability amplitude of H$\beta$ to that of the driving continuum, for both sub- and super-Eddington AGNs. By combining the effective responsivities measured relative to the driving continuum, after correcting for the continuum--emission-line delay, with photoionization calculations, we infer the ionizing radiation field received by their BLRs. We find that, in super-Eddington AGNs, the ionizing flux inferred from the H$\beta$ responsivity differs significantly from that observed along the line of sight, whereas the two are in closer agreement in sub-Eddington AGNs. These results provide compelling evidence for strong anisotropy of the incident ionizing radiation in super-Eddington AGNs. We conclude by discussing the broader implications of this anisotropy, including its potential relevance to the "little red dots" recently discovered by the James Webb Space Telescope.

 - **Abstract**
 We present a comprehensive broadband spectro-temporal analysis of the dynamically confirmed Galactic black hole transient GS 1354-64 during its 2025-26 outburst, using near-simultaneous observations from Insight-HXMT and XRISM. The high spectral resolution of the XRISM-Resolve microcalorimeter, coupled with the high-cadence broadband coverage of Insight-HXMT, reveals a dramatic evolution in the inner accretion geometry. During the early hard-intermediate state, the spectrum was dominated by a hot Comptonizing corona accompanied by relativistically broadened reflection features.  - **Abstract**
 We develop accelerated three-dimensional simulations of growing planet pairs that draw on a finite gas reservoir. An annulus compression (Scaling A) shortens the predicted clearing time and shrinks the computational domain at equal grid resolution while preserving key physical parameters. Planetary seeds start with Hill radii equal to the disk scale height, retaining three-dimensional accretion. We demonstrate the method in scaled PDS 70 and Solar-System configurations, assuming perfect Hill capture by growing planets on circular orbits.
