#### From Inspiral to Outburst: Modeling a Potential Luminous Red Nova from Betelgeuse
 - **Authors:** Naunet Leonhardes-Barboza, Andrea Antoni, Morgan MacLeod, Angela Twum, Enrico Ramirez-Ruiz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31755

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31755

 - **Abstract**
 Recent evidence suggests that Betelgeuse may host a stellar companion whose orbit will decay through tidal interactions, ultimately leading to engulfment and merger within the next several thousand years. If confirmed, this system could represent the nearest and best-characterized progenitor of a future luminous red nova (LRN). We investigate whether the shock-breakout and cooling emission preceding the LRN can be used to constrain the properties of the companion and its disruption within the stellar envelope. Using binary stellar evolution calculations, we evolve Betelgeuse with companions of $0.7$, $1.0$, and $2.0 M_\odot$ to the onset of common envelope evolution (CEE). We then perform one-dimensional radiation-hydrodynamic simulations that follow companion disruption, energy deposition, and shock propagation through the envelope. We find that the peak luminosity increases systematically with companion mass, from $2.44\times10^{41}$ to $6.17\times10^{41}$ erg s$^{-1}$ across the $0.7$-$2.0 M_\odot$ models, while the duration increases more modestly from $1.48$ to $1.99$ days and attains apparent magnitudes brighter than Venus as viewed from Earth. Tidal disruption contributes both kinetic energy and mass to the envelope, linking the companion's structure and mass to the energetics of the shock-heated ejecta. The monotonic luminosity sequence demonstrates that the early breakout emission can retain direct information about the engulfed companion. The interaction ejects $6.15$-$11.19 M_\odot$ of hydrogen-rich material, substantially modifying both the envelope structure and circumstellar environment prior to core collapse.
#### FastSpec: Fast and versatile stellar spectrum synthesis code
 - **Authors:** Oleg Kochukhov
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31763

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31763

 - **Abstract**
 Modelling data from modern spectroscopic surveys and time-resolved spectropolarimetric observations requires stellar synthetic spectra to be computed rapidly over wide wavelength ranges from line lists that increasingly reach hundreds of millions of transitions. Spectropolarimetric analyses of magnetically active stars additionally demand a solution of the polarised radiative transfer problem. We present FastSpec, a new LTE spectrum synthesis code designed around two goals: increased raw computational speed and broad astrophysical applicability, including the calculation of the full Stokes vector in the presence of a magnetic field. FastSpec computes emergent intensity and flux spectra from plane-parallel and spherical model atmospheres. Chemical and ionisation equilibrium is obtained from the updated SME equation-of-state package, continuum opacities follow the ATLAS family of routines, hydrogen bound-bound and bound-free absorption uses the occupation-probability formalism, and line absorption is evaluated with fast table-based Voigt and Faraday-Voigt profile algorithms. The transfer equation is integrated with DELO-Bezier formal solvers in both scalar and Stokes-vector form. Speed is achieved through aggressive OpenMP parallelisation, adaptive per-line wavelength windowing, and a memory-capped block-processing scheme that lets arbitrarily large line lists be handled within a user-defined RAM budget. The code synthesises spectra thousands of Å wide from line lists of $10^8$ molecular and atomic transitions in under a minute on a modern multi-core laptop while reproducing benchmark calculations from established codes. FastSpec combines the physical completeness of classical model atmosphere and spectrum synthesis codes with the speed required by large surveys and the polarised transfer needed for stellar magnetism studies into a single portable package.

Our results show how semi-analytic models can quantify how often subhalos reproduce individual perturbers in different dark matter models, and our modular framework can be readily updated as models of tidal stripping and late-stage gravothermal evolution in SIDM improve. #### Differential and integral scaling laws of the mass density of molecular clouds
 - **Authors:** Sava Donkov, Valentin Kopchev, Ivan Z. Stefanov, Todor V. Veltchev
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32344

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32344

 - **Abstract**
 In this work we investigate the mass density - size scaling law in molecular clouds (MCs). This relation reflects the fractal nature of MCs and plays a key role for understanding the physics, structure and evolution of these objects. We make use of the notion "ensemble of MCs", introduced in our previous work (Donkov, Veltchev \& Klessen, 2017), in which all MCs with the same probability density function (PDF) of mass density and effective size are represented by an abstract spherical cloud with the same PDF and size. In this spirit, the model is built on the base of abstract scales of the clouds' substructures (which are simply the radii of the spherical object). We consider two forms of the mass density - size scaling law: differential and integral, which in turn reveal the local and the global fractal clouds' structure. Both scaling functions are characterized by their scaling exponents, which can be explicitly expressed by the PDF of mass density, in the general case. Moreover, we derive a first order linear differential equation connecting the two scaling exponents and obtain its exact solution. As examples we apply this abstract construction to two PDFs: the so called power-law tail and the log-normal. Both have great importance for MC structure and evolution, as the latter corresponds to the earlier stages of clouds' evolution, when supersonic turbulence dominates the physical processes, while the former describes the latest stages of evolution, when star-formation takes place. The obtained results for the scaling exponents in both examples are qualitatively and numerically consistent with respective observations and simulations of MCs.

Observations indicate stellar abundance differences in a small fraction of these co-natal systems.  - **Abstract**
 Understanding the evolution of solids in protoplanetary discs is important to comprehend the formation of giant planets cores and terrestrial planets. The ALMA Large Program AGE-PRO studied key disc properties in a survey of 30 protoplanetary discs across three evolutionary stages: the embedded phase (0.5-1 Myr), the middle-age phase (1-3 Myr), and the end of the disc lifetime (2-6 Myr). Preliminary dust properties were estimated using 1.3 mm observations, but a multi-wavelength analysis provides a better constraint of important properties such as the dust disc mass, the dust temperature, and the maximum grain size. We present new ALMA 3.1 mm and 1.5 mm (Bands 3 and 5) continuum observations of the complete survey of 30 AGE-PRO discs. We fitted the observed visibilities and derived radial brightness profiles, spatially resolving 20 discs from the AGE-PRO sample at the two studied wavelengths. A multi-wavelength modelling was performed including also the radial profiles from the 1.3 mm observations. Our results replicate trends obtained from the single wavelength analysis: the dust disc size seems to be constant over age and the dust disc mass decreases with time, with a factor $\sim$10-40 uncertainty from opacity assumptions. This result implies much lower gas-to-dust mass ratios for the discs in our sample than previously reported. In addition, the derived spectral indices are consistent with optically thick emission in a large fraction of the AGE-PRO discs, in particular for the Ophiuchus and Lupus discs.
