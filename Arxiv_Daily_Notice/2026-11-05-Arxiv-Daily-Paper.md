#### Constraining Ganymede's Interior Structure and Bulk Composition from Its Present-Day Magnetic Field Strength
 - **Authors:** Jun Kimura, Ryohei Kawakami, Taro Matsuo, Sho Sasaki
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05727

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05727

 - **Abstract**
 Ganymede is the only Solar System satellite with a global magnetic field driven by a core dynamo. Although its small moment-of-inertia (MoI) factor indicates a differentiated structure of a metallic core, rocky mantle, and outer hydrosphere, its exact bulk composition and layer thicknesses remain highly uncertain. We developed a comprehensive, long-term thermochemical evolution model that couples thermal history with magnetic field generation. Building upon previous theoretical frameworks, this study systematically evaluates how diverse chondritic precursor compositions constrain Ganymede's internal thermochemical evolution and core solidification processes. Based on interior structures initially permitted by a wide range of chondritic compositions and geodetic constraints, we applied the observed equatorial surface magnetic field strength ($\sim$719 nT) as a dynamic filter. Our evolutionary simulations demonstrate that while magnetic dynamos can operate under entire melting, Fe-snow, and FeS-layer regimes, the requirement to sustain the observed field strength strongly restricts the viable interior models. Satisfying this constraint preferentially requires a relatively large core radius and a specific, non-eutectic bulk core sulfur concentration. Consequently, this coupled thermochemical approach highlights the main novelty of this study: characterizing the hydrosphere's density serves as an independent constraint to break the structural degeneracy of Ganymede's bulk composition. Our model indicates that L and CI chondrites yield parameter spaces capable of sustaining comparable field strengths under a low-density hydrosphere scenario (1,200 kg m-3), whereas the H chondrite composition can satisfy this criterion under a high-density scenario (1,300 kg m-3). These predictions provide a critical framework to be tested by the JUICE mission arriving in 2031.
#### Collective Characterization of Polar Crown Filament Eruptions
 - **Authors:** Ying Wang Yan Xu, Ju Jing, Vasyl Yurchyshyn, Haimin Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05902

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05902

 - **Abstract**
 Polar crown filaments (PCFs) are crucially related to the structure of solar polar magnetic fields. They are physically significant because of the ``rush-to-the-pole'' motion, which is an indicator of the poleward drift of magnetic fields from decayed active regions. The formation and evolution of PCFs offer important insights into the Sun's magnetic activity at high latitudes and the solar cycle variation. In this study, we present a collective analysis of 50 PCF disappearance events covering solar cycle 24 and 25. Among them, 24 show rotational motions, and nine have sufficiently clear magnetic configurations for comparison with numerical model predictions. In all nine events, the observed rotation directions agree with the model predictions, suggesting possible magnetic-flux-rope-like structures in these events. All except one of the PCFs in our sample disappeared through eruption rather than thermal disappearance, and most associated CMEs were slow ($<800$ km s$^{-1}$). For events accompanied by transient coronal holes, the estimated magnetic flux associated with the dimming regions is on the order of $10^{20}$--$10^{21}$ Mx. Time--distance analysis identifies 22 events with a clear slow-rise to fast-rise transition, with onset heights ranging from several tens to about 160 Mm and a mean onset velocity of about 2.8 km s$^{-1}$ after excluding the highest-velocity event. Together, these findings characterize the magnetic structure and dynamics of PCF eruptions.
#### Longitudinal-field particle acceleration in channels generated by strong electromagnetic waves in pair plasma
 - **Authors:** Kavin Tangtartharakul, Andrei M. Beloborodov, Carolina Herrera Hernandez, Mihail O. Cernaianu, Alexey Arefiev
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05948

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05948

 - **Abstract**
 Relativistically strong electromagnetic waves interacting with pair plasmas appear in astrophysical settings and may become accessible in future laboratory experiments using high-power lasers. Transverse modulations and filamentation can turn extended wavefronts into structures of a finite transverse width. We examine the interaction of such a finite-width wave incident on a cold, uniform pair plasma using a three-dimensional kinetic particle-in-cell simulation. The wave does not penetrate the plasma and instead advances by creating a fully evacuated channel. The wave loses energy at a rate that cannot be explained by plasma clearing alone. We find that the excess losses are caused by strong particle acceleration near the walls of the channel. The acceleration is driven by a longitudinal electric field associated with the transverse wave structure and the geometry of the self-generated channel.
#### Net Cooling Tables for Cosmological Simulations under Multiple UV/X-ray Background Models
 - **Authors:** Laura Di Federico, Klaus Dolag, Ildar Khabibullin, Luca Sala, Geray S. Karademir, Milena Valentini
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06360

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06360

 - **Abstract**
 We present three sets of net cooling tables for cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, computed with CLOUDY 23.01 for optically thin gas in photoionization equilibrium with three different UV/X-ray background models. Following the element-by-element approach, and in a format compatible with existing tables, we tabulate cooling, heating, and net rates separately for hydrogen and helium over an extended range of helium mass fractions and for 13 heavy elements, together with hydrogen species fractions, electron abundances, and mean particle masses. For gas of Solar composition, the choice of UV/X-ray background has little impact on the net cooling rates at low redshift, but the results increasingly diverge towards high redshift, particularly in diffuse gas near thermal equilibrium. Compared with widely used previous tables, cooling times in this regime differ by up to an order of magnitude at $z \gtrsim 4.6$, and the calculations disagree on whether the gas undergoes net cooling or heating over nearly 10% of the diffuse density-temperature plane at $z \approx 8$, corresponding to the intergalactic medium. These trends persist for gas composed exclusively of hydrogen and helium. The tables provide a consistent framework for assessing the impact of the radiation background on gas cooling in cosmological simulations.
#### Gaia DR3 Reveals the Kinematics of Young Stellar Objects in the Carina Nebula: Peripheral Expansion and Central Rotation
 - **Authors:** Xinyu Yang, Yuxin He, Chaojie Hao, Yiwei Dong, Y. Xu, Yingjie Li, ZeHao Lin, DeJian Liu, Jarken Esimbek, Jianjun Zhou, Gang Wu, Xindi Tang, Dalei Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06488

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06488

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the kinematics of the Carina Nebula using a sample of young stellar objects (YSOs) cross-matched with Gaia DR3, together with $^{12}\mathrm{CO}\,(1\mbox{--}0)$ molecular gas observations and APOGEE radial-velocity data. The results show that the proper motions of the YSOs in the four peripheral subregions of Carina display a systematic outward expansion, and that the two-dimensional expansion velocities of the subregions are approximately $0.57\mbox{--}4.56~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The YSOs in the Central Region exhibit ordered rotation around an axis at a position angle of $86.9\pm1.5^\circ$, with a rotation velocity of $1.75\pm0.03~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. Further momentum estimates suggest that the current expansion in the peripheral regions may result from the combined effects of feedback from massive OB stars and possible past supernova explosions. These results indicate that feedback from massive stars plays a dominant role in the dynamical evolution of the giant molecular cloud in the Carina Nebula.
#### Primary and Secondary Star Formation in the Spiral Waves of NGC 628
 - **Authors:** Bruce G. Elmegreen, Sean T. Linden, Kelsey E. Johnson, Angela Adamo, Daniela Calzetti, Varun Bajaj, Giacomo Bortolini, Matteo Correnti, Ana Duarte-Cabral, Kathryn Grasha, Goran Ostlin, Alex Pedrini, Jenna Ryon, Linda J. Smith
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06516

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06516

 - **Abstract**
 To determine the role of spiral arms and stellar feedback on the formation of stars, we examine the positions of optically identified and emerging star clusters in NGC 628. We find that the youngest clusters extend further into the spiral arms at small radii than at large radii, as expected when the angular flow rate through a wave-like arm increases with greater distance from corotation. The embedded or emerging star clusters (eYSCs) are identified relative to four distinct morphological features in the 7.7 micron image: spiral arm filaments, spiral arm spurs, spiral interarm feathers, and bubbles. Spiral arm-related eYSCs are presumably first-generation stars made from interstellar gas that was directly compressed by the density wave, while clusters along the rims of bubbles are secondary generations produced by feedback compression. The mass functions of the first and second generations differ: eYSCs in spiral arm filaments extend to significantly higher masses than eYSCs in bubbles. Secondary star formation continues downstream from primary star formation, removing the expected age gradient through the arms. Bubble eYSCs account for approximately one-third of all the emerging cluster mass. The outer arms have a more mixed distribution of spiral- and bubble-related eYSCs than the inner arms, potentially providing a way to distinguish wave and material arms and to find corotation in wave-like arms. Among the 17 most massive clusters with log M(Msun)>4, eight of them are in four tight pairs; in at least one case, the dynamical time for the pair is comparable to the cluster ages.
#### The formation and evolution of Wolf-Rayet stars with temperature-dependent winds
 - **Authors:** Guy O. Flint, Avishai Gilkis, Tomer Shenar, Christopher A. Tout
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06650

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06650

 - **Abstract**
 Massive stars strongly influence their environments through their radiation, stellar winds and core-collapse supernovae, while their own evolution is strongly shaped by wind mass loss. Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars are characterised by strong, optically thick winds, and the implementation of these winds remains an important uncertainty in stellar evolution models. Recent hydrodynamically consistent atmosphere calculations predict WR mass-loss rates that depend strongly on the temperature at the wind-launching region. We investigate the effects of incorporating these latest theoretical WR wind prescriptions into single and binary stellar evolution models at Milky Way, Large Magellanic Cloud and Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) metallicities. We find that the temperature dependence of the WR wind can significantly alter the evolution of stars near the threshold for WR formation. In particular, an implementation that transitions from a hydrogen rich WNL wind prescription to a classical WR wind driven by the hot iron opacity bump causes some SMC-metallicity models to spend an extended period at temperatures of approximately 70 to 90 kK. This produces a concentration of models in the temperature range occupied by most observed SMC WR stars, a feature that has previously been difficult to explain with stellar evolution models. Our results demonstrate that incorporating physically motivated, temperature dependent WR winds can improve the agreement between stellar evolution models and observed WR populations.
