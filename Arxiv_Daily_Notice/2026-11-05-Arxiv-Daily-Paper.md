#### A full chemical network treatment of supermassive black hole seed formation from dark matter annihilation or decay
 - **Authors:** Han Wu, Sarah Schon, Aaron C. Vincent
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03802

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03802

 - **Abstract**
 The recent discoveries of high-redshift quasars at $z\sim6-10.1$ present a challenge to conventional supermassive black hole (SMBH) formation scenarios: their central SMBHs are too large to have grown from early stellar remnants, even under efficient super-Eddington accretion. An alternative approach is the direct collapse of primordial, dust- and metal-free gas clouds into black hole seeds as early as $z\sim20$. To prevent fragmentation from highly efficient molecular hydrogen cooling and allow such a collapse, a strong flux of UV photons is required to photodissociate molecular hydrogen, suppressing star formation. We investigate whether the decay or annihilation of dark matter with masses below $\sim200$ eV can produce sufficient UV radiation, both from the cosmological background and within early dark matter halos. Using thermal simulations of a one-zone primordial gas cloud model with the Grackle chemistry and cooling library, we map the resulting gas behavior across a range of dark matter lifetimes, cross sections, and masses. From this, we determine regions of parameter space where direct collapse into an SMBH is possible and the corresponding maximum halo mass required.
#### Galactic-Scale Distribution of 10Be and Its Implications for the Early Solar System 10Be/9Be
 - **Authors:** Yusuke Fujimoto, Steven J. Desch, Jiro Shimoda, Shu-ichiro Inutsuka
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03808

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03808

 - **Abstract**
 Meteoritic inclusions indicate that the early Solar System contained a substantial abundance of the short-lived radionuclide 10Be. Whether it was inherited from the parent molecular cloud or requires local irradiation or late injection remains debated. Here we present the first three-dimensional simulations of a Milky Way-like disc that follow the multi-phase interstellar medium, cosmic rays (CRs), and the production and transport of 10Be by galactic CRs (GCRs) and in supernova remnants (SNRs). Nearly all 10Be is produced by GCR interactions; SNRs contribute at most about ten percent of the 10Be inherited by newly formed stars. The 10Be/9Be ratio traces the CR energy density, declining smoothly with galactocentric radius, and because stars form where supernovae inject CRs, their natal gas is CR-enriched roughly fivefold relative to the mean interstellar medium, which one-zone estimates miss. Accounting for a possibly higher Galactic star formation rate 4.6 Gyr ago, inheritance of GCR-produced 10Be can then explain the early Solar System's 10Be/9Be in our models, without late injection or local irradiation. Even when GCR-induced 10Be production in dense gas is reduced tenfold, the meteoritic value is reached in the innermost disc and missed by only a factor of a few elsewhere, so the main conclusion is robust to uncertain low-energy GCR penetration. The inferred birth radius of the Sun depends strongly on the CR diffusivity, ranging between 3 and 8 kpc, suggesting that improved modelling of low-energy CR transport could sharpen constraints on the birthplace of the Solar System.
#### The STARfall pathway: forging intermediate-mass black holes through stellar collisions in dense star clusters
 - **Authors:** Filippo Simonato, Manuel Arca Sedda, Taeho Ryu, Jakub Klencki, Giuliano Iorio, Sara Rastello, Lavinia Paiella, Cristiano Ugolini, Benedetta Mestichelli, Marica Branchesi, Abbas Askar, Peter Berczik, Guglielmo Costa, Mirek Giersz, Albrecht W.  - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03812

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03812

 - **Abstract**
 Intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs, $10^2 < m_{\rm BH}/\mathrm{M}_{\odot} < 10^5$) are elusive objects bridging the gap between stellar-mass and supermassive black holes, whose origin remains uncertain. They may form from the collapse of very massive stars (VMSs, $m_*\geq150\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$) assembled through repeated stellar collisions in compact and fast evolving star clusters. We investigate the formation of VMSs and IMBH seeds via repeated mergers and collisions as a function of cluster initial conditions, assessing the implications for globular clusters and dense high-redshift system. We develop STARfall, a semi-analytic tool that follows the evolution of a massive stellar binary via binary-single interactions, mergers, collisions, and stellar evolution, while consistently accounting for the host cluster evolution. Our results show that clusters with central densities above $10^6\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc}^{-3}$ can undergo a catastrophic sequence of stellar collisions, or "starastrophe", within $3$ Myr. Through this process, VMSs rapidly grow in mass and collapse into IMBHs with masses of up to $10^3-10^4\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}$, despite the effects of intense stellar winds. Applying our models to observed systems, we find that compact clusters in the Cosmic Gems Arc and Milky Way globular clusters could have hosted VMSs that collapsed into IMBHs in this mass range. Despite their longer core-collapse timescales, the inferred central densities of Little Red Dots may also support IMBH formation in the same mass interval. Our models show that VMS assembly requires two essential ingredients: short core-collapse times and large central densities. Compared to observed systems, our models suggest that clusters in the Cosmic Gems Arcs and several Galactic globular clusters could have nurtured VMSs that directly collapsed to IMBHs with masses $10^3-10^4\,\text{M}_{\odot}$.
#### Revisiting the Missing Baryon Problem: Baryon Redistribution Across Halo Evolution
 - **Authors:** Changjo Seo, Seyoung Jeon, Emanuele Contini, Yohan Dubois, Corentin Cadiou, San Han, J. K. Jang, S. Peirani, Christophe Pichon, Jinsu Rhee, Sukyoung K. Yi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03814

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03814

 - **Abstract**
 The baryon fraction provides a fundamental diagnostic of baryonic matter distribution within dark matter halos and the baryon cycle. While its cosmic value is well constrained, the baryon content within halos on nearly all scales is significantly lower than the cosmic mean; this is called the ``missing baryon problem.'' Using the high spatial, mass, and temporal resolutions of the NewCluster simulation, we investigate the baryon fraction evolution by tracing halo assembly histories from merger trees and gas tracer particles. We identify three distinct evolutionary phases: an early growth phase driven by cold accretion in low-mass halos, a suppression phase likely dominated by supernova feedback, and a recovery phase in massive halos enabled by deeper potential wells and hot-mode accretion. We further find that the redshift dependence of baryon fraction, particularly in low-mass halos, is influenced by the thermal state of the intergalactic medium, with suppression emerging after the surrounding gas becomes persistently hotter than the halo virial temperature. In addition, the scatter in baryon fraction at fixed halo mass correlates with the large-scale environment, with baryon-rich halos preferentially residing closer to filamentary structures. Overall, our results highlight that baryon fraction evolution is governed by the interplay between feedback and environment, requiring a time-resolved perspective on the baryon cycle.

 - **Abstract**
 Carbon radio recombination lines (CRRLs) arising from the cold neutral medium (CNM) of the interstellar medium are potential foregrounds in the 1-50 MHz band that LuSEE-Night will observe from the lunar far side. CRRLs are sensitive probes of the temperature, density, and radiation field of the CNM, and LuSEE-Night will provide sky-averaged measurements in a poorly explored frequency range. We model CRRLs across this band, accounting for doppler, collisional, and radiation broadening, and we estimate line strengths using two independent methods - fixing either the peak or the frequency-integrated optical depth - applied both uniformly across the sky and with region-dependent values informed by prior low-frequency observations. We simulate LuSEE-Night's expected response to these CRRLs and further convolve the beam-diluted spectra with LuSEE-Night's polyphase-filter-bank spectrometer response, including its zoomed-mode frequency resolution of $\sim$390 Hz, to obtain the expected observed brightness temperature. Using these simulated spectra, we estimate the integration time required to detect individual CRRLs at SNR=5 across the six sky models considered. Required integration times range from as short as 30 hours (in the most optimistic model where the optical depth across the sky is $\sim$10$^{-3}$) to 27.6 years (in the most conservative models where the optical depth across the sky is $\sim$10$^{-4}$). Stacking the 361 simulated C$\alpha$ lines between 10 and 50 MHz reduces the integration time needed for an SNR=5 detection to $\sim$11 minutes and $\sim$151 hours respectively, indicating that LuSEE-Night has realistic chances of making a stacked detection of C$\alpha$ RRLs within a realistic mission lifetime, even under the most conservative optical-depth assumptions.

 - **Abstract**
 (abridged abstract) Wolf-Rayet stars have high mass-loss rates and fast stellar winds. Their interaction with circumstellar material from previous evolutionary phases can produce complex nebular morphologies. Hydrodynamic instabilities can fragment wind-swept material, producing condensations, filaments, and gaps that alter the confinement of stellar wind and ionizing radiation. The role of fragmentation in the late evolution and dispersal of WR nebulae remains poorly constrained. PCG 11, with its fragmented morphology and regular scalloped rim, offers an opportunity to investigate these processes. We aim to characterize the structure of PCG 11 and investigate its ring-like morphology and scalloped rim. We constrain its three-dimensional geometry and determine whether the optical fragmentation pattern is also traced by ionized gas. We obtained high-resolution radio continuum images with MeerKAT at 2.6 and 3.1 GHz and ATCA at 5.5 and 9.0 GHz. We also detect an unresolved source coincident with WR 75ab, whose rising radio spectrum is consistent with thermal wind emission. PCG 11 captures a poorly observed stage in massive-star bubble evolution: the swept-up shell has developed a coherent instability pattern and become fragmented and porous, while remaining dynamically coupled to the stellar wind. It provides a clear view of how fragmentation and erosion reshape the circumstellar environment during a transitional evolutionary phase.

 - **Authors:** L.-H. Lin, F. Wyrowski, S. Neupane, D. Colombo, K. M. Menten

 - **Abstract**
 The vertical distribution of molecular gas in the Milky Way disk is poorly studied, especially in the 4th quadrant and the far side of the Galaxy. While surveys over 20 years ago suffered from coarse beam and non-Nyquist sampling, most of the recent surveys are limited to $|b|<1^\circ$ and/or focus on the Northern Galactic plane. We examine the molecular gas's vertical distribution and properties in the fourth quadrant to check the impact of Galactic structure on current star formation. We mapped two arm tangent regions ($l=[329^\circ.0,327^\circ.0]$ for the Norma-Outer arm, and $l=[310^\circ.0,308^\circ.5]$ for the Scutum-Centaurus arm) with the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line using the 7-pixel LASMA receiver of the APEX 12 m telescope, covering up to $b=[-2^\circ,+2^\circ]$ (or $|z|\sim200$ pc near the tangent points). The noise level of the $^{12}$CO 3-2 line is about 0.4 K [$T_{MB}$] in velocity channels of 0.5 km s$^{-1}$. The vertical distribution around the tangent regions of the spiral arm is fitted with two Gaussian components, the narrow one with an FWHM thickness of 25 pc and the wide one of about 100 pc. Two explanations are proposed: they are two sub-components of the thin disk (the narrowest tracing the spiral arm and the other the inter arms components), or they trace both thin and thick disks that show a smaller thickness when observed with CO 3-2 than when observed with CO 1-0. The latter indicates that the density distribution of the interstellar medium exhibits a steeper gradient at larger heights above the plane. Clumps in the two tangent regions are searched and divided into groups by relative height. Compared with the low relative height clumps ($|z|<50$ pc), the median relative height clumps ($50~\mathrm{pc}<|z|<100~\mathrm{pc}$) have lower excitation temperature and surface density, with fewer high values in the distribution. A similar tentative trend is found for the mass.
#### Fermi-LAT Detection of Extended GeV Emission toward the Middle-aged Supernova Remnant G156.2+05.7
 - **Authors:** Wei-Liang Xu, Qing-Wen Tang, Long Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04227

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04227

 - **Abstract**
 We analyse 17 years of Fermi-LAT $\gamma$-ray data toward the middle-aged supernova remnant (SNR) G156.2+05.7, supplemented by radio and CO observations and by published X-ray constraints. An extended $\gamma$-ray source is detected, well described by a 2D Gaussian template ($R_{68} \sim 0.84^\circ$). This description is preferred over a foreground CO template, whereas the Gaussian and a uniform disc are statistically indistinguishable under the AIC. The spectrum shows evidence for curvature, with a log-parabola preferred over a power law at the $\approx 3.4\sigma$ level. Multi-wavelength spectral energy distribution (SED) modelling, under the adopted assumptions, suggests a leptonic--hadronic joint scenario consistent with the data, with the Fermi-LAT spectrum favouring a hadronic contribution over a purely leptonic interpretation. However, the proton-dominated alternative remains competitive, so the partition of the GeV emission is model-dependent. In the adopted leptonic--hadronic model, the electron population accounts for the radio synchrotron emission and a leptonic $\gamma$-ray component, while a steep proton population contributes the remaining hadronic $\gamma$-rays. Adopting a reference distance $d = 2.7$ kpc, the fitted electron cutoff ($E_{e,\mathrm{cut}}\approx 1.2$ TeV) is compatible with the thermal-dominated X-ray emission, while the proton cutoff is fixed by assumption at $1$ TeV. The northeast and southwest quadrants show enhanced $\gamma$-ray emission, which may be related to a clumpy or asymmetric surrounding medium. A correspondence with the directions of optical H$\alpha$ filaments is suggested by the quadrant orientation but is not yet established. No significant long-term flux variability is found, consistent with steady emission from an extended source.
#### The Survival Paradox of Close-In Planets Orbiting Red Clump Giants
 - **Authors:** Wen-Xu Lin, Sheng-Bang Qian, Qi-Bin Sun, Li-Ying Zhu, Wen-Ping Liao, Ting Li, Fu-Xing Li, Xiang-Dong Shi, Tao Wang, Ergang Zhao, Huiting Zhang, Ping Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04544

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04544

 - **Abstract**
 Close-in planets orbiting core-helium-burning red-clump (RC) giants directly challenge tidal-engulfment theory, which predicts that planets within about 1 au of Sun-like stars should be engulfed during red-giant-branch (RGB) expansion. The existence of such survivors would imply missing physical processes in the late stages of stellar and planetary evolution. Yet only one such "forbidden" planet had been identified to date. Here we report three systems-24 Boo b, HD 12648 b, and TYC 4282-605-1 b-whose host stars are confirmed red-clump giants through asteroseismology, showing that each has already undergone dramatic expansion and contraction following helium ignition. Their planets now reside at orbital separations smaller than the maximum stellar radii attained during the RGB phase, implying that they should not have survived. Among them, 24 Boo b orbits at only 0.183 au with a 30-day period, making it the closest planet known around a red-clump giant. These discoveries demonstrate that this paradox is not an isolated anomaly but signals the emergence of a new class of evolved planetary systems. Their survival defies current models of stellar-planetary coevolution and points to unexplored physics shaping planetary architectures around post-RGB stars.

 - **Authors:** Apoorv Dashora, Ramesh Chandra, Pooja Devi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04833

 - **Abstract**
 Extreme ultraviolet (EUV) waves are large-scale, tsunami like disturbances on the solar surface associated with eruptive phenomena such as solar flares, filament eruptions, and coronal mass ejections (CMEs). Because their strong correlation with major solar eruptions, we present here a comprehensive statistical study of EUV waves and their connections to Moreton waves, CMEs, solar flares, type II radio bursts, and solar energetic particle (SEP) events during Solar Cycle 25 (December 2019-December 2025). This study utilizes EUV data from the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly onboard the Solar Dynamics Observatory and coronagraph data from the Large Angle and Spectrometric Coronagraph onboard Solar and Heliospheric Observatory. Our analysis reveals that 12.3% of the observed EUV waves are associated with Moreton waves, 95% with CMEs, 92.7% associated with flares, 44.3% with metric type II radio bursts, and 11.8% with SEPs. While nearly all EUV waves are linked to CMEs, their association with solar flares varies significantly, ranging from weak B-class events to major X-class flares. Furthermore, the majority of Moreton waves correlate with large- or medium-class flares, with only a small fraction associated with weaker C-class flares. Our findings may contribute to a better understanding of space weather events and, potentially, to their forecasting.

 - **Authors:** Huanyu Ren, Gabi Wenzel, Thomas H. Speak, Miya Duffy, Annapoorani Hariharan, Martin S. Holdren, D. Archie Stewart, Christopher N. Shingledecker, Reace H. J. Willis, Ilsa R. Cooke, Brett A. McGuire

 - **Abstract**
 Although a set of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) has recently been identified in the cold dark cloud TMC-1, several common PAH motifs remain absent, leaving the origin of this apparent structural selectivity unresolved. Triphenylene (\ce{C18H12}) provides a stringent test of whether relative thermodynamic stability alone can account for the emerging PAH inventory because it is the smallest fully benzenoid PAH predicted by Clar's rule, is exceptionally stable, and is a key subunit of larger detected aromatic frameworks such as coronene and fullerene-related species. To probe whether such thermodynamically favored carbon skeletons are selected under cold interstellar conditions, we synthesized cyanotriphenylenes and measured their first 6.5--16~GHz microwave rotational spectra as polar tracers for the non-polar parent. An astronomical search based on these spectroscopic parameters yielded no detection, suggesting a possible ``missing triphenylene'' anomaly: despite its exceptional thermodynamic stability, this fully benzenoid PAH may be relatively depleted in TMC-1. To rationalize this behavior, we introduce a ring-node graphic representation that classifies PAH skeletons by topology; within this framework, triphenylene, whose compactness is otherwise ambiguous, is classified as non-compact. This representation reveals a tentative clustering of the currently detected and undetected PAHs according to topology, suggesting that the current PAH inventory cannot be rationalized solely on the basis of thermodynamic stability and may instead reflect differences in formation and destruction kinetics. The observed selectivity may arise from two complementary effects: sequential growth decay along bottom-up growth pathways and rapid consumption of bay-region intermediates by reactive unsaturated small species.

 - **Abstract**
 Canes Venatici I (CVnI) is a distant Milky Way (MW) dwarf spheroidal (dSph) satellite. To date, there is no consensus on its evolution and the properties of its stellar population. This work is aimed at obtaining an accurate picture of the star formation history (SFH) and metallicity distribution of CVnI to reconstruct its evolution in detail, as well as to infer the possible effects of internal and external feedback. We used archival HST/ACS observations to obtain the deepest colour-magnitude diagram (CMD) to date, reaching $\sim$2 mag below the old main sequence turn-off. We derived the SFH and metallicity distribution of CVnI using a state-of-the-art CMD-fitting technique. We found that the SFH is dominated by a single star formation period, which peaked around 11.8 Gyr ago, well after the reionisation epoch, and remained active until $\sim$10.4 Gyr ago. The galaxy's global metallicity, [Z/H], ranges from -2.4 to -1.5 dex. Interestingly, while its star population is old and metal-poor, compared to other MW satellites of a similar size and luminosity that formed most of their stars in a single burst, CVnI experienced a delayed star formation period. We carried out an exhaustive set of tests with synthetic and real populations to ensure the reliability of these results. We suggest that the evolution of CVnI could have been regulated by its mass accretion history. The galaxy would have evolved above (but still close to) a theoretic redshift-dependent critical mass above which it is possible to maintain star formation. The combined effect of SN feedback would have made CVnI's halo mass subcritical at z$\approx$2, definitively quenching its star formation. We cannot rule out the possible influence of gravitational interaction of CVnI with the MW at early ages, as hinted at by the most recent calculations of its orbit.

 We present the luminous, long-lived Type IIn SN 2019qem and use it to reconstruct how much mass its progenitor lost and when.
