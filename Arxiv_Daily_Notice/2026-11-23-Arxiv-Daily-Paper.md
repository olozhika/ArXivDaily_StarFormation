#### PLATON 7: An ultra-fast exoplanet atmospheric retrieval code
 - **Authors:** Michael Zhang, Tyler Fairnington, Yayaati Chachan, Kimberly Paragas, Eliza Kempton, Heather Knutson, Jacob Bean
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26680

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26680

 - **Abstract**
 PLATON is an open source Python package for atmospheric retrieval, first released in 2018. We introduce PLATON 7, a complete rewrite of PLATON using Claude Fable 5. The biggest changes are that PLATON now uses JAX for all intensive calculations, and these calculations are now performed in single precision instead of double precision. These two changes speed up the code by a factor of ~60 with negligible decrease in accuracy, allowing it to compute a 0.8--5.3 um transmission spectrum at R=20k in 0.35 ms on a RTX 5090. We also introduce several new features, including support for two-limb retrievals, patchy clouds, and more opacities (particularly OCS and CS2). PLATON is available on GitHub at this https URL.
#### Water-ice formation in Ophiuchus follows the local irradiation
 - **Authors:** Stefan Meingast, Zhetai Cao
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26683

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26683

 - **Abstract**
 Water ice only appears on interstellar dust grains beyond a certain depth into a molecular cloud. This depth has so far been quoted as one extinction threshold per cloud. We measured the threshold along 43009 sightlines through the Ophiuchus cloud with the ice color-excess method, which converts archival WISE and Spitzer photometry of background stars into the peak optical depth of the 3 $\mu$m water-ice band. We sorted the sightlines by the 12 $\mu$m surface brightness of the cloud at fixed extinction, a proxy for the ultraviolet field that excites the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons at the cloud surface. The onset of ice formation lies at higher extinction toward sightlines with brighter 12 $\mu$m emission, ranging from $A_{K_S}^{\rm th} = 0.67$ to $1.15$ mag. It increases by 0.58 to 0.90 mag in $A_{K_S}$ per factor of ten in 12 $\mu$m brightness, depending on the adopted reddening coefficient. Because the 12 $\mu$m-bright and -faint sightlines lie in different parts of the cloud, we repeated the comparison within each of the five subregions, and four of the subregions show the same trend. The trend also persists among sightlines with a similar amount of diffuse material around them. A later onset in more strongly irradiated gas is expected from photodesorption by external ultraviolet light, although a comparison of its size with models requires a calibration of the 12 $\mu$m brightness against the strength of the field. The extinction at which water ice appears thus varies with the local environment, which complicates comparisons between clouds. Photometric ice maps resolve this variation across entire clouds, and the SPHEREx and JWST ice surveys can test it spectroscopically.
#### From Binary Wind to Radio Burst: Dispersion-Measure Evolution after Accretion-induced Collapse
 - **Authors:** Smadar Naoz, Victoria M. Kaspi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26696

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26696

 - **Abstract**
 Secular changes in the dispersion measures (DMs) of repeating fast radio bursts probe the source-local plasma. Such DM time variability has been interpreted with spherical, single-density-profile models. Accretion-induced collapse (AIC) of a white dwarf may instead follow binary interaction and occur inside a finite, anisotropic circumstellar structure. Here, we construct an angle-dependent toy model of aspherical AIC ejecta propagating through a pre-AIC free wind, shocked-wind bubble, swept-up external shell, and polar cavity, exploring a diffuse environment (number density of $1~{\rm cm^{-3}}$, located at $10~$pc) and a compact cloud (number density of $500~{\rm cm^{-3}}$, located at $0.3~$pc), bracketing ionized and initially neutral upstream gas. The unshocked ejecta initially yield ${\rm DM}\propto t^{-2}$, while crossings of the finite wind-bubble interfaces produce breaks and curvature in ${\rm DM}(t)$. In initially neutral models, shock ionization can overcome geometric dilution: the total DM reverses from decline to growth after approximately $10$--$20$ yr and later turns over as the finite reservoir is processed. Ionized-upstream models instead generally decline or flatten. Without fitting individual sources, our models span both signs and the approximate magnitudes of the secular DM-variation rates measured among repeaters. In this dynamical framework, DM evolution is set by shock propagation through the circumsource density and ionization structure, rather than the contemporaneous burst rate, distinguishing it from photoionization-driven scenarios. Consequently, in our model, an instantaneous \(d{\rm DM}/dt\) is not a unique indicator of source age. We suggest that the observed distribution of secular DM variation slope amplitudes and signs will be important for distinguishing among different models of DM variations in repeating FRBs.
#### The Effect of Dust Grain Growth and Stellar Metallicity on Gas Giant Planet Formation by Disk Instability
 - **Authors:** Alan P. Boss
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26804

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26804

 - **Abstract**
 The formation of gas giant planets by the gas disk gravitational instability (GDGI) mechanism depends sensitively on the thermodynamics of the protoplanetary disk gas: disk gas must be able to cool on orbital timescales for dense clumps to become gravitationally unstable and contract to form protoplanets. Since disk cooling is regulated by the opacity of the dust grains embedded in the gas, the properties of these dust grains are critical for the GDGI mechanism. Grain growth to larger sizes than assumed in traditional opacity models for solar metallicity, as well as protostars with sub-solar metallicity, should have lower opacity disks that are more conducive to planet formation by GDGI. Here we use a three dimensional radiative hydrodynamics and gravitation code to compute the evolution of the GDGI in $0.091 M_\odot$ disks with varied initial minimum values (1.3 to 2.7) of the Toomre criterion orbiting solar-mass protostars. Three sets of eight models are computed: one with the standard opacity, and two more with the opacity reduced by arbitrary factors of 10 or 100. As expected, the reduced opacity models result in enhanced disk fragmentation by the GDGI and the production of more self-gravitating clumps than the standard opacity models. Nevertheless, all three opacities form similar self-gravitating clumps at this early phase of clump evolution, with masses of 0.2 to 0.7 $M_{Jup}$ and semimajor axes between 6 and 13 au. Significant dust grain growth should enhance the ability of the GDGI to form protoplanets around super-solar metallicity protostars.
#### The role of gas on the collisional runaway path to supermassive black hole seeds
 - **Authors:** Boris Cuevas, Bastián Reinoso, Andrés Escala, Marcelo C. Vergara, Sebastián Valdebenito, Dominik R. G. Schleicher
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26885

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26885

 - **Abstract**
 The origin of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remains unclear, despite observational evidence for their rapid formation in the early Universe, as evidenced by active galactic nuclei (AGN) at high redshift. In this paper, we investigate stellar dynamics in dense, gas-embedded clusters. We explore how gas influences the runaway collision process and the subsequent formation of SMBH seeds in a global instability scenario. We perform a series of coupled N-body and SPH simulations using the AMUSE framework, with the stellar and gaseous components interacting gravitationally via the BRIDGE method. We use 12 initial condition models with equal-mass stars following Plummer distributions, with total masses ranging from $10^3$ to $5 \times 10^5~M_{\odot}$, virial radii from $0.005$ to $0.1$ pc, and stellar masses of $1$, $10$ and $50$ $M_{\odot}$. For each model, we perform four simulations with gas fractions ranging from 0\% to 20\%, resulting in a total of 48 simulations. The gas is sampled with the same spatial distribution as the stars, with a mass ratio per particle of $m_{\rm SPH}/m = 0.01$, where $m$ is the stellar mass. We find that in most of our simulations, higher gas fractions lead to the formation of more massive black hole, as the core collapses earlier, triggering collisions through gas--star interactions. Our results show increases in BH mass of $\sim 8\%$ for low gas fractions, to $\sim 44\%$ at 20\% gas fraction. The core collapse time is also shortened, with the strongest reduction at $20\%$ gas fraction ($\sim 27\%$). The transonic regime of our initial conditions is where dynamical friction is most efficient in principle. Although our simulations do not fully resolve this effect, gas--star interactions are the principal mechanism driving the enhancement in BH formation efficiency, in the absence of other dissipation mechanisms.
#### Disentangling Disk and Broad-Line Region Continuum Emission in a Sample of Active Galactic Nuclei
 - **Authors:** Manqi Fu, Shuying Zhou, Hengxiao Guo, Zhi-Xiang Zhang, Marcin Marculewicz, Mouyuan Sun
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27051

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27051

 - **Abstract**
 Recent continuum reverberation-mapping measurements of active galactic nuclei (AGNs) indicate that the inter-band time lags are significantly larger than those predicted by the light travel times across the standard thin model. In addition, the observed $u$-band excess suggests the presence of diffuse continuum (DC) radiation originating in the broad-line region (BLR). Spectroscopically, the Balmer and Paschen jumps in DC emission are unique indicators to quantify the contributions of the BLR to the observed continuum. Here, we study the DC contributions of 31 AGNs, whose spectra are obtained with the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS) on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and with X-shooter on the Very Large Telescope (VLT). In each spectrum, at least one of the Balmer and Paschen jumps is covered. For each object, we performed radiative transfer calculations using Cloudy within the Locally Optimally-emitting Clouds (LOC) model to construct source-specific DC spectral templates. We quantified the DC contribution by incorporating these templates as continuum components in spectral decomposition. The average ratio of the DC to total (i.e., disk+DC) continua is ~33% just below the Balmer jump (~3625 Å). The Balmer jump provides a stronger constraint on the DC component than the Paschen jump. Simulations based on mock spectra further demonstrate that an unsuitable template can introduce systematic, wavelength-dependent biases to the inferred DC fraction. Our results underscore the importance of employing appropriate DC templates in the spectral decomposition to infer the substantial DC contribution. Upcoming time-resolved spectra will enable more detailed modelling and tighter constraints on the disk and BLR.
#### The relation between DCN/DNC ratios and gas temperature in late-stage massive star-forming regions using Yebes observations
 - **Authors:** Yuqiang Li, Junzhi Wang, Aran Lyo, Juan Li, Siqi Zheng, Woojin Kwon, Seokho Lee, Jongsoo Kim, Huangxue Feng, Miguel Santander-García, José Jairo Díaz-Luis, Shaoyuan Lu, Fu Mo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27118

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27118

 - **Abstract**
 Deuterated molecules probe the physical and chemical evolution of molecular clouds and star-forming regions. Their abundances depend on temperature, density, and evolutionary stage. DCN and DNC are relatively abundant deuterated isomers that probe temperature-dependent deuterium chemistry. Although [HCN]/[HNC] is often thought to increase in warmer gas, the temperature dependence of [DCN]/[DNC] remains less well constrained. To investigate how [DCN]/[DNC] depends on the gas kinetic temperature, we observed the $J$=1-0 transitions of DCN, DNC, H$^{13}$CN, HN$^{13}$C, HC$^{15}$N, and H$^{15}$NC toward 101 late-stage massive star-forming regions using the Yebes 40m telescope. Gas kinetic temperatures were estimated from CH$_3$CCH 5-4, whose rotation temperature is closely related to the kinetic temperature of dense gas. These temperatures were adopted in the column density calculations and in the analysis of temperature-dependent abundance ratios. We find a moderate positive correlation between the abundance ratio [DCN]/[DNC] and kinetic temperature, which is statistically significant and indicates that DCN becomes progressively more abundant relative to DNC in warmer gas. A weak negative correlation with temperature is observed for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HNC})$, whereas no statistically significant correlation is found for $D_{\rm frac}({\rm HCN})$. The [HCN]/[HNC] ratios show substantial scatter and no statistically significant correlation with temperature, possibly due in part to optical-depth effects in the $^{13}$C isotopologues and the limited sensitivity of the $^{15}$N isotopologue observations. These results suggest that [DCN]/[DNC] may be governed not only by HCN/HNC isomeric chemistry but also by temperature-dependent deuterium fractionation pathways and the chemical evolution of dense gas.
#### Baryon-Enhanced Strong Lensing Signatures of Self-Interacting Dark Matter Subhalos
 - **Authors:** Meiwen Yang, Daneng Yang, Siyuan Hou, Yi-Ying Wang, Yiping Shu, Yue-Lin Sming Tsai
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27249

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27249

 - **Abstract**
 Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) is generally expected to suppress subhalo strong lensing through core formation, with subsequent gravothermal collapse potentially enhancing it. We show that compact stars can make a viable population of galaxy-hosting SIDM subhalos more efficient lenses than their cold dark matter (CDM) counterparts. When the baryonic potential weakens or reverses the inner velocity-dispersion gradient, SIDM halos suppress or bypass core formation, allowing inner densities to grow from early times. The evolved SIDM subhalos produce larger deflection angles and tangential caustics than in CDM. Such galaxy-hosting subhalos also dominate the galaxy-galaxy strong-lensing (GGSL) budget in clusters, yet lie far below the resolution of cosmological simulations. We calibrate a baryonic parametric SIDM model to predict their effects in the three most massive TNG300-1 clusters, finding GGSL cross sections 1.4--2.7 times those in CDM, comparable to the excess inferred in recent analyses. Baryon-enhanced SIDM lensing can thus account for the observed GGSL excess and predicts stronger perturbations from luminous satellites, testable through resolved distortions of background arcs and rings.
#### Interstellar extinction estimates for nearly 90 million stars based on synthetic photometry from Gaia XP spectra
 - **Authors:** Bao-Kun Sun, Yan-Hui Chen, Zheng Yu, Bai-Song Zhang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27303

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27303

 - **Abstract**
 Accurate stellar extinction measurements are essential for recovering intrinsic stellar properties and investigating the three-dimensional distribution of interstellar dust. We present reddening estimates for 87,270,425 stars using synthetic SMSS gri photometry derived from corrected Gaia DR3 XP spectra, combined with Gaia BP and RP, 2MASS JHKs, and WISE W1 photometry. Stellar extinction is determined through empirical SED fitting based on reference stellar loci constructed separately for dwarfs and giants. Comparisons with three external extinction catalogs show good overall agreement, with dispersions of 0.049-0.075 mag in E(B-V). The catalog covers approximately 99.99% of the sky. Using stars with sigma_d/d < 0.2, we further construct reddening maps in four distance ranges from 0 to 5 kpc, revealing prominent molecular clouds and large-scale Galactic dust structures. Extinction-corrected color-absolute-magnitude diagrams show significantly tighter stellar sequences and consistent red-clump features in different Galactic fields. The resulting catalog provides a homogeneous data set for stellar extinction correction and studies of Galactic dust structure.
#### Characterising stellar photometry with invertible neural networks: Joint inference of stellar parameters and extinction
 - **Authors:** Victor F. Ksoll, Isabelle Joncour, Prasanta Gorai, Ralf S. Klessen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27342

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27342

 - **Abstract**
 We introduce an extension to a simulation-based deep-learning approach for the characterisation of stellar photometry and investigate the feasibility of jointly estimating stellar properties and extinction. We used the PARSEC stellar evolutionary models to train a conditional invertible neural network (cINN) to jointly estimate full posterior distributions of stellar parameters and extinction. We tested our cINN on synthetic data and real Gaia+2MASS photometry of the two star-forming regions Taurus and Upper Scorpius. We compared our predicted extinction maps to two independent catalogues based on Gaia XP-spectra. On synthetic test data, our trained cINN exhibited a good predictive performance, achieving root mean squared errors (RMSEs) for the maximum a posteriori point estimates of about 0.16 mag for $A_V$, 0.07 dex for $\log(T_{eff}/K)$, 0.16 dex for $\log(g/cms^{-2})$, 0.2 dex for $\log(L/L\odot)$ and 0.1 dex for $\log(M_{ini}/M_\odot)$. Only $\log(age/yr)$ has proven harder to constrain with an RMSE of 0.56 dex. On the Gaia+2MASS observations of Taurus and Upper Scorpius, we retrieved reasonable extinction maps, exhibiting structures that are visually consistent with local clouds. Compared to the independent XP-spectra-based extinction studies, we found a good qualitative agreement and recovered the same prominent spatial structures. Quantitatively, however, we identified an average systematic overestimation of extinction in our approach of 0.5-0.7 mag compared to the spectroscopic studies. We investigated these offsets and identified photometric and distance uncertainties, which are currently unaccounted for in our approach, as likely causes. In conclusion, we demonstrated the feasibility of jointly estimating stellar parameters and extinction with our cINN approach, making it a promising avenue to further pursue for robust and efficient analysis of large photometric surveys.

 - **Abstract**
 In anticipation of BepiColombo's orbital insertion in 2026, this study revisits the rotation of Mercury, modelling it with a fluid core and a deformable mantle. For this well-established physical model, we provide an analytical solution based on a novel Lagrangian formalism. Our approach enables a comprehensive 3D solution for the mantle's orientation, including the phase in longitude at the perihelion. The model incorporates dissipative torques from core-mantle boundary friction and tidal deformations, conservative pressure torques, and the effect of the planet's apsidal precession.  We present an analytical model of a turbulent clumpy star-forming disk that reproduces the spatially resolved observations for 6 molecular lines and 3 continuum bands at 250-700~GHz in the central 30~pc of the Circinus Galaxy.
