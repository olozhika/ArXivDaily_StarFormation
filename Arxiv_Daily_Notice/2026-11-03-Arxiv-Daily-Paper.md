 JWST has extended the search for accreting black holes into the high-redshift, low-mass regime. We report a low-mass black hole candidate in MARTA 3887, a clumpy, metal-poor galaxy at $z=3.7694$, combining deep JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy, HST and JWST imaging, and archival VLT/MUSE data.  JWST NIRCam F115W observations towards the inner Orion Nebula revealed a diverse array of previously unknown dark features. The most likely cause was absorption or suppressed emission of the helium 1.083$\mu$m triplet which lies in that filter.  - **Abstract**
 Young low-mass objects in crowded, highly obscured star-forming regions are difficult to characterize. Deriving reliable physical parameters requires separating photospheric emission from complex extinction and circumstellar emission. We apply a multi-component spectral-modeling framework to JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra toward NGC 2024, obtaining adopted fits for 78 sources. The framework incorporates atmospheric models, dust and ice opacities, and optional thermal emission. #### Mean-element Parareal: Parallel-in-Time Integration of the Solar System
 - **Authors:** Michael Hippke
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01237

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01237

 - **Abstract**
 Long-term integrations of planetary systems have long been considered inherently sequential: each time step must start from the exact state at the end of the previous one, because any error in that state, however small, is amplified by orbital shear and chaos and alters the trajectory. Because a single step of a modern Wisdom--Holman integrator takes only ~100 ns - too short to split across cores - additional cores have previously only been used to run multiple simulations in parallel. We introduce mean-element parareal (MEP), a parallel-in-time scheme that accelerates a single Solar System integration (eight planets, general relativity) at the same accuracy as the serial integrator. Combined with a latency-optimized C kernel that integrates 5 Gyr on one core in 5.8 h, MEP completes a 5 Gyr integration in 52 min on 64 cores (a parallel speed-up of 6.7x, and 10.4x faster than the previous 9.0 h single-core record) or in 1.8 h on 16 cores (3.3x parallel speed-up, 5.0x faster than the previous record). MEP succeeds because its coarse propagator uses a numerical first-order mean-element theory to remove the short-period oscillations from the semi-major axes, preventing Keplerian shear from amplifying them into growing phase errors. Standard parareal with a coarse Wisdom-Holman integrator fails for planetary orbits because no inexpensive coarse integrator can resolve these oscillations. To our knowledge, this is the first measured multicore speed-up of a single long-term planetary integration and opens a pathway towards larger-scale parallelization.

 - **Abstract**
