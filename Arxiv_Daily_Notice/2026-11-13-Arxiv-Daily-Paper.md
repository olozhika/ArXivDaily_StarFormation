#### galpy with differentiable, accelerated backends: galactic dynamics in jax and PyTorch
 - **Authors:** Jo Bovy
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14614

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14614

 - **Abstract**
 We describe an extension of galpy, the Python library for galactic dynamics, that adds jax and PyTorch as computational backends alongside its existing numpy and C implementation. With it, galpy's gravitational potentials, orbit integration, action-angle transformations and their inverses, and distribution functions, including those for tidal streams, run on GPUs, can be compiled as part of a user's program, and can be differentiated with respect to their inputs and parameters, from the force at a point to the samples drawn from a distribution function. The user-facing design is kept simple: the active backend follows from the type of the arrays that are passed in, the galpy interface is unchanged, and the numpy path gives the same results as before. We describe the design of the backend framework and its implementation in each of galpy's modules, with examples of use, and we discuss its limitations. On a GPU, batches of orbits, samples from distribution functions, and stream models run one to five orders of magnitude faster than on a single CPU core. To illustrate the new capabilities, we present simple examples of fitting the Galactic potential with a hypervelocity star and of learning the inverse action-angle transformation with a neural network trained through galpy. An AI coding agent wrote essentially all of the code under the detailed direction and review of the human author, and we describe how we organized this work and how we established the correctness of the result. This work is the start of galpy's version 2, which is available at this https URL and documented at this https URL .
#### Type Ia-CSM Supernovae. I. Uniform Light-Curve Characterization and Inference on the Underlying Type Ia Explosions
 - **Authors:** Daichi Hiramatsu, Daichi Tsuna, Morgan MacLeod
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14620

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14620

 - **Abstract**
 We present the largest uniform study to date of 50 thermonuclear Type Ia supernovae interacting with circumstellar medium (SNe Ia-CSM). As the first paper in a series delineating the population properties of this class, this work focuses specifically on a uniform analysis of their light curves. Our results show that SNe Ia-CSM display a significantly more uniform light-curve morphology than interacting core-collapse SNe IIn, closely resembling the luminous-slow subgroup. The Ia-CSM sample spans approximately an order of magnitude in peak luminosity, total timescale, and radiated energy, with a median optical peak luminosity of $\approx2\times10^{43}$ erg s$^{-1}$ ($\approx-20$ mag). We find a distinct bimodality in the radiated energy distribution: a predominant population of energetic events with $\approx 2\times10^{50}$ erg and characteristic timescales of $\approx100$ days, and a secondary population that is roughly an order of magnitude less energetic with shorter timescales of $\approx50$ days. Decomposition of the luminosity contributions from underlying SN Ia and CSM interaction by the Ia-CSM spectral classification reveals a diverse range of underlying SN Ia subtypes spanning from luminous 1991T-like to faint 1991bg-like. Both SN Ia and CSM properties are broadly consistent with the core-degenerate channel resulting from common-envelope evolution followed by core merger, where the presence of dense CSM is dictated by the delay time between the common-envelope phase and the thermonuclear explosion. Subsequent papers in this series will utilize numerical simulations, semi-analytical modeling, spectroscopy, and host-galaxy environments to further constrain the progenitor and explosion pathways to SNe Ia-CSM.

 - **Abstract**
 The dust extinction curve, characterized by the total-to-selective extinction ratio, $R(V)$, varies with grain size and composition. Accurate $R(V)$ values are crucial for dereddening astronomical colors and understanding the interstellar medium. We measure extinction toward stars with Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) spectroscopy using a machine-learning-based relative of the "pair method," matching reddened and unreddened stars. We use XGBoost to predict stars' intrinsic colors from their spectroscopic parameters ($T_\mathrm{eff}$, $\log g$, $[\mathrm{Fe/H}]$) and subtract them from observed colors to measure reddening in combinations of the $g$, $r$, $z$, $W1$, and $W2$ bands. This approach directly measures reddening, unlike the common thermal-emission-based method of Schlegel et al. (1998; SFD). The reddening is accurate to roughly 0.02 mag in $g-r$ and $r-z$. We develop a forward model to map $g-r$ and $g-W1$ reddenings to $R(V)$ and find significant variation in $R(V)$, with $\sigma_\mathrm{NMAD}(R(V)) = 0.23$, in dusty regions ($E(g-r) > 0.1$) at high Galactic latitudes observed by DESI. In this footprint, $R(V)$ is bimodal, with modes at 2.9 and 3.4, likely due to large-spatial-scale correlations in the shape of the extinction curve combined with the relatively small number of distinct sightlines with substantial reddening. We find no strong relationship between $R(V)$ and other tracers of the interstellar medium, such as thermal emission, dust temperature, spectral emissivity index $\beta$, and $H\alpha$. However, the error in the SFD-predicted extinction correlates well with $R(V)$, implying that a single optical reddening combined with the far-infrared dust brightness can predict variations in the extinction curve.

 - **Abstract**
 Although photochemical hazes complicate the spectroscopic analysis of sub-Neptunes, the very presence of hazes can teach us about the habitability of these planets. Aside from affecting a planet's atmospheric and surface dynamics, hazes offer a source of organics potentially including prebiotic molecules like amino acids, nucleobases, and sugars. These organics are particularly compelling for temperate, water-rich planets where hazes may react with liquid water in condensate clouds or surface reservoirs. Hydrolysis reactions drive molecular reorganization and oxygenation, increasing the chemical complexity of the prebiotic inventory. The hydrolyzed samples exhibit a broader distribution in H/C and O/C ratios, and the molecular formulas suggest complex carbohydrate chemistry that challenges current theories of prebiotic organic delivery.

 - **Abstract**
 CosmoSim is an open-source tool for simulating gravitational lenses and reconstructing lens mass distributions, either by ray tracing a parametric lens model or through a Roulette expansion of the lens potential. It is also used to generate the large, labelled synthetic image sets needed to train machine-learning (ML) parameter estimators. We present the first application of CosmoSim to real data. A convolutional neural network trained only on CosmoSim images estimates Singular Isothermal Ellipsoid lens and source parameters directly from observed images. The predicted ellipticities differ systematically from the catalogued values, while the comparison of orientations is inconclusive. These results establish CosmoSim as a tool that can be applied directly to observational data.

#### How Intensity Threshold Shapes the Velocity Dispersion-Size Relation in Molecular Clouds
 - **Authors:** Yaru Qi, Xin Liu, Fujun Du
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14852

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14852

 - **Abstract**
 The velocity dispersion-size relation of molecular clouds, $\sigma_v = C\,(R/1\,{\rm pc})^{\gamma}$, has been reported with a power-law index ranging from $\gamma \approx$ 0.38 to 0.5 or higher, and a correspondingly varying normalization coefficient $C$, but the role of the intensity threshold in producing this scatter has not been quantitatively characterized. These two historically debated values, together with the broader $\gamma \approx$ 0.38-0.7 range reported in the literature, may largely reflect different positions along this single threshold-dependent curve and differences in sample composition. This threshold effect should therefore be considered when using Larson-type relations to assess the roles of turbulence and gravity. As an independent check, DBSCAN-identified $^{13}$CO and $^{12}$CO samples show the same monotonic trend in $\gamma$, though the curve shape differs quantitatively.

 - **Abstract**

 - **Abstract**
