#### "Mega-Earths" are likely an artifact of stellar activity
 - **Authors:** Casey L. Brinkman, Nicolas Cowan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12600

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12600

 - **Abstract**
 Empirical relations and planet formation simulations suggest an upper mass limit for rocky planets of $\sim$10M$_{\oplus}$, as cores above this will almost certainly undergo runaway gas accretion. Nevertheless, a small number of planets appear to have densities consistent with rocky Earth-like compositions above this threshold, indicating that unusual formation and evolutionary scenarios are capable of producing extremely massive rocky planets called "Mega-Earths." However, all of these planets orbit active stars with log$R'_{HK}$ $>$-4.75. We find a <1$\%$ probability that all literature ultra-massive rocky planets would coincidentally orbit such active stars, and find no astrophysical reason to expect Mega-Earths to preferentially form around active stars. We therefore conclude that Mega-Earths are artifacts of stellar activity, and we question the reliability of Gaussian Processes to enable mass measurements for rocky planets around active stars.
#### The GAPS programme at TNG LXXXII. The first atmospheric investigation of the gas giant WASP-13b at high resolution with GIARPS
 - **Authors:** D. Sicilia, M. Basilicata, G. Guilluy, G. Scandariato, F. Borsa, C. Di Maio, M. Brogi, A. Chiavassa, L. Fossati, F. Amadori, P. Giacobbe, A. S. Bonomo, A. Sozzetti, L. Mancini, G. Andreuzzi, S. Colombo, V. Fardella, A. Ghedina, G. Mantovan, G. Piccinini
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12677

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12677

 - **Abstract**
 Low-density transiting gas giant exoplanets are key benchmarks for atmospheric studies. Despite their large-scale heights, many of them remain poorly characterised. We present the first atmospheric investigation at high-resolution of the inflated (R$_\mathrm{p}$$\sim$1.4 R$_{\mathrm{J}}$), hot Jupiter (T$\sim$1500$ \mathrm{K}$) WASP-13 b aiming at constraining its atmospheric composition and refine its orbital geometry. We analysed four transits observed with GIARPS at the TNG. Updated stellar and planetary parameters were re-derived from the most precise literature values, and a new Rossiter-McLaughlin analysis was performed to measure the projected spin-orbit angle ($\lambda$) and stellar rotational velocity ($v\sin{i_\star}$). Atmospheric signals were searched using high-resolution spectroscopy, both with cross-correlation and single-line analyses, targeting key atomic and molecular species (e.g. \ion{He}{I}, H$\alpha$, \ion{Na}{I}, H$_2$O, CO). We find a well-aligned orbit ($\lambda$ = 2.7$\pm$1.1$^\circ$) and $v\sin{i_\star}$ = 4.5$\pm$0.1 km s$^{-1}$, improving upon previous constraints. No significant atmospheric absorption is detected in either VIS or NIR data. Injection-and-recovery tests exclude an isothermal atmosphere with metallicity [M/H]$\lesssim$10$\times$[M/H]$_\odot$ and simultaneously a grey cloud top pressure P$_{\rm cloud}\gtrsim10$ mbar, under the assumption of chemical equilibrium. WASP-13 b appears to belong to the class of low-density hot Jupiters with weak or featureless transmission spectra, most likely shaped by the presence of high-altitude clouds. Although a highly metal-enriched atmosphere cannot be fully excluded, current evidence disfavours this scenario. Further observations with next-generation facilities will be essential to characterise the atmospheric properties of this challenging target.
#### Exploring Case B I: The Footprints of Hidden Radiation Fields in H I Balmer Decrements
 - **Authors:** Maryam Dehghanian, Fransisco Guzman, Peter van Hoof, Chamani Gunasekera, Gary Ferland
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12715

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12715

 - **Abstract**
 Theory predicts that H I emission from photoionized clouds should lie close to the Case B limit established by Menzel and Baker, and observations of nearby nebulae have largely confirmed this expectation. However, recent observations of more distant high-redshift systems have revealed a small fraction with striking departures from Case B. What additional physics might be at work in these objects? This paper is part of a series exploring the physical processes that can produce such departures. In Paper I (D26), we show that Case B remains remarkably robust over a broad range of physical conditions, consistent with observations of nearby nebulae. Here, we explore one additional ingredient: a soft, non-ionizing radiation field. Such a continuum can strongly perturb the Balmer spectrum. The largest effects occur for radiation fields resembling log(Teff K)\sim 3.2-4.2 blackbodies, where photoexcitation from the metastable 2s level alters the hydrogen level populations and subsequent radiative cascade. The resulting Halpha/Hbeta and Hgamma/Hbeta ratios follow a different trajectory from dust reddening, allowing the two effects to be distinguished using multiple Balmer lines. In unresolved systems, a cool source located near the line-emitting gas may provide an additional radiation field distinct from the hot stars responsible for ionization. Balmer decrements may therefore carry information about otherwise hidden components of the radiation field surrounding the emitting gas.
#### Bridging Theory and Observation: Synthetic Far-infrared Insights into Star Formation Efficiency
 - **Authors:** Alexander Escamilla, Michael Y. Grudić, Anna L. Rosen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12729

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12729

 - **Abstract**
 The star formation efficiency per free-fall time ($\epsilon_{\rm ff}$) quantifies how efficiently giant molecular clouds (GMCs) convert gas into stars and is often observed to be orders of magnitude lower than expected for free-fall collapse. Observers typically estimate $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$ by mapping far-infrared (FIR) dust emission to infer gas surface densities ($\Sigma_{\rm gas}$) and counting embedded protostars, assuming simplified lifetimes and masses. Using the fiducial STARFORGE radiation-magnetohydrodynamics simulation of an $M_{\rm cl} = 2\times10^4 M_\odot$ GMC that self-consistently models star cluster formation and stellar feedback, we generate synthetic FIR observations and apply the same methodologies used in GMC surveys to investigate this discrepancy. We present the first $\epsilon_{\rm ff}-\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ analysis in a fully feedback-regulated GMC simulation that resolves the formation of stellar systems. Our synthetic measurements reproduce the low observationally inferred efficiencies, showing that feedback-regulated star formation naturally produces $\epsilon_{\rm ff} \sim 1-3\%$ without requiring extreme initial conditions. We also find that $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$ varies strongly over a GMC's lifetime, suggesting that much of the observed scatter reflects evolutionary sampling rather than intrinsic cloud-to-cloud differences. Finally, by comparing observational and simulation definitions, we show that methodological assumptions introduce systematic biases, with a transition near $\log \Sigma_{\rm gas} \approx 2.3 M_\odot {\rm pc}^{-2}$ that depends on resolution. Above this threshold, the smoothing of dense structure increases both the inferred free-fall time and enclosed gas, with the latter dominating and suppressing $\epsilon_{\rm ff}$. Below the threshold, the discrepancies are primarily driven by star formation rate assumptions.
#### Predicted state-to-state collisional rate coefficients from neural networks
 - **Authors:** Lisán David Cabrera-González, Carlos Cárdenas, Otoniel Denis-Alpizar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Chemical Physics (physics.chem-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12743

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12743

 - **Abstract**
 Molecular spectra observed in the interstellar medium are commonly analyzed using non-local thermodynamic equilibrium models, which requires accurate state-to-state collisional rate coefficients. Close-coupling calculations become prohibitively expensive for transitions from highly excited rotational states, therefore available datasets are often truncated at low initial states and temperatures; and since each new system also requires an accurate ab initio potential energy surface, many detected molecules have no collisional data. We employ two machine learning strategies to predict rotational state-to-state rate coefficients of molecules included in the train set beyond the range of initial states and temperatures computed so far, and predicting those of a molecule absent from training and validation sets. Feed-forward neural networks are trained on state-to-state de-excitation rate coefficients of 24 linear molecules colliding with para-H2. Scheme 1 includes all systems in the train+validation set, with 70%, 80%, and 90% initial-state boundaries and a 90% temperature boundary. Scheme 2 is trained on 22 systems described only by transition and molecular properties, and predicts two excluded systems, CS and CF+, whose astrophysical impact is explored with non-LTE calculations. For the 90% boundary, Scheme 1 predicts 99.4% of the test transitions within a factor of 2, and no prediction deviates from the reference value by more than a factor of 4.7. This model was used to complete 18 datasets. Scheme 2 reproduces the rate coefficients of two excluded systems within a factor of 3 for more than 92% of their transitions. Both schemes provide near-instantaneous complements to close-coupling calculations: Scheme 1 extends the range of a molecule already included in the training set with high accuracy, while Scheme 2 gives a reasonable first estimate for a molecule that has not been studied.
#### Decomposing the Kinematics of SNR G180.0-01.7 with Medium-Resolution Spectra
 - **Authors:** Juan-Juan Ren, Wei Zhang, Chao-Jian Wu, Chao Liu, Jian-Jun Chen, Hong Wu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12880

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12880

 - **Abstract**
 The exceptionally large angular diameter and intrinsic optical faintness of the Galactic supernova remnant (SNR) G180.0-01.7 (S147) have historically hindered a comprehensive spectroscopic census of its filamentary structure. We present the first dedicated medium-resolution (R ~ 7500) spectroscopic study of S147 using the LAMOST Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic survey of Galactic Nebulae (MRS-N). By implementing multi-Gaussian decomposition on the H$\alpha$, [NII], and [SII] emission complexes and applying combined spectroscopic and spatial criteria, we disentangle the shock-ionized filaments of the SNR from the pervasive foreground and background Diffuse Ionized Gas (DIG). Our analysis reveals a distinct bimodal radial velocity distribution for the SNR: a low-velocity population tracing the decelerated forward shock at a dense cavity wall, and a high-velocity population (with absolute radial velocities of ~ 70 $\pm$ 30 km/s) corresponding to ejecta within a low-density cavity. The co-existence of these populations provides kinematic evidence for the "supernova-in-a-cavity" scenario. Furthermore, we detect enhanced [SII]/H$\alpha$ ratios in the DIG along the eastern and northern boundaries of the remnant, signaling shock compression of the ambient interstellar medium and highlighting remnant's multifaceted feedback.
#### Redshifted Cool Gas in the Disturbed HI Disk of the Starburst Dwarf NGC 4214
 - **Authors:** Shulan Yan, Taotao Fang, Xiaochuan Jiang, Junfeng Wang, Qingzheng Yu, Chuanpeng Zhang, Ming Zhu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13039

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13039

 - **Abstract**
 We report redshifted cool gas toward the central star-forming region of the nearby starburst dwarf galaxy NGC 4214. HST/COS spectra show low-ionization absorbers redshifted relative to the systemic velocity, most clearly in SII ($\Delta v=19.8\pm2.6\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$) and supported by MgII ($\Delta v=30.3\pm3.0\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$), while PII remains tentative ($\Delta v=7.5\pm3.4\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$). The Keck/HIRES spectra also reveal a redshifted CaII absorption component ($\Delta v=25.6\pm1.5\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$). Because the absorption is detected against the central UV or optical continuum, the gas must lie in the foreground of the star-forming region and is moving away from the observer and toward the background UV source in projection. A VLA--THINGS HI spectrum extracted at the COS position within one VLA beam also shows a redshifted neutral component at $v_{\rm hel}=329.2\pm0.9\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$, offset by $38.2\pm1.3\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$ from systemic and by $27.6\pm1.0\ {\rm km\ s^{-1}}$ from the dominant local HI component. The FAST HI mapping further reveals an extended, kinematically disturbed neutral disk, with residual structures relative to a warped-disk model. Together, the redshifted UV absorption, the redshifted HI component, and the disturbed large-scale HI velocity field show that the central anomalous cool gas is observed in a galaxy with a disturbed extended neutral disk. This geometry suggests that the redshifted cool gas may trace inflow or recycled fallback toward the central region, although warped-disk or non-circular disk motions cannot be fully excluded.
#### Star Cluster Ecology VIII. -- Dynamically Forming S-Type and $α$Centauri-like Systems
 - **Authors:** Erwan Hochart, Simon Portegies Zwart
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13127

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13127

 - **Abstract**
 Aims: To analyse the impact of dynamically formed binaries emerging naturally from various star cluster environments on already established planetary systems. Methods: Numerical simulations replicating the star-forming region NGC 1333 are conducted. We consider three models, each using identical cluster realisations and simulation parameters. The cluster itself is initially sub-virial and fractally distributed. Model~A assumes planetary systems are initially in mean motion resonance. In Model~B, the same planetary systems are moved out of resonance, but remain intrinsically stable. Finally, Model~C removes all planets from the simulation. Ten independent realisations are integrated per model until 30 Myr. Results: Dynamically formed binaries emerge at similar rates across models, indicating planets play little role in their formation. We also find a higher production rate of wide binaries compared to previous numerical studies which we attribute to the sub-virial and sub-clustered initial conditions. S-type and binary S-type planetary systems naturally emerge in our simulations, while P-type systems are rare. For Model~A, the fraction of S-type or binary S-type systems is f_{\rm S}=0.02 and $f_{\rm Bin,\, S}=0.05, respectively. Rogue planets originating from a binary system are ejected with a substantially larger velocity than those around singletons. Planet-star collisions and planet capture events occur disproportionately within binary systems. Finally, several \alphaCentauri-like systems emerge in our simulations, suggesting that the nearest stellar system to the Sun may have a dynamical origin.
#### Flux Evolution of PSR B0540--69 and the Spectral Energy Distribution of its Pulsar Wind Nebula
 - **Authors:** Juan Zhang, Xuezhi Liu, Junyang Zhang, Hanlong Peng, Mingyu Ge, Songzhan Chen, Shanshan Weng, Shijie Zheng, Shumei Jia, Chengkui Li, Haisheng Zhao, Ju Guan, Yong Chen, Liming Song, Fangjun Lu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13182

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13182

 - **Abstract**
 The young Crab-like pulsar PSR B0540--69, located in the Large Magellanic Cloud, underwent a spin-down rate transition (SRT) in 2011 December, after which its pulsar wind nebula (PWN) was reported to brighten up to 2018. In this work, we extend the study of its X-ray and $\gamma$-ray flux evolution through 2026 by combining observations from multiple X-ray instruments with data from the Fermi Large Area Telescope, thereby constructing a flux history of the PSR$+$PWN system spanning more than two decades. We find that the enhanced X-ray luminosity of the PSR$+$PWN system has persisted for more than 14 yr after the SRT, up to early 2026, whereas the pulsed X-ray flux remains unchanged across all epochs. This confirms that the brightening originates from the PWN. In the $\gamma$-ray band, neither the pulsed nor the non-pulsed flux shows any detectable long-term variation between 2008 and 2025. Together, these results imply that the additional spin-down energy released at the SRT has been continuously injected into the PWN over decades, placing strong constraints on the energy budget and physical mechanism of the transition. Combining the non-pulsed X-ray and $\gamma$-ray fluxes measured here with previously published radio-to-UV data, we further construct a broadband spectral energy distribution of the PWN, which shows properties markedly different from those of the Crab Nebula.

 We report [O\,\textsc{iii}] narrow-band imaging of an extremely large-scale nebula, the Silk Nebula, discovered by the SWONS (Super Wide-Field [O\,\textsc{iii}] Narrow-band Survey), together with multi-fiber medium-resolution spectroscopic data covering the H$\alpha$, [N\,\textsc{ii}], [S\,\textsc{ii}], and [O\,\textsc{iii}] emission lines, acquired with the Large Sky Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope (LAMOST) as part of the Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic Survey of Nebulae (MRS-N). #### Fermentation heterotrophy can reproduce Enceladus's H2 and CH4 under limited serpentiniaztion
 - **Authors:** Sam Nasreldine, Antonin Affholder, Laura Molares Moncayo, Régis Ferrière, François Guyot
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13434

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13434

 - **Abstract**
 Saturn's icy moon Enceladus is a prime target in the search for life beyond Earth. Abundant H2 and CH4 in Enceladus's south-polar plume have been shown to be consistent with hydrogenotrophic methanogens feeding on products of long-lived serpentinization. However, thermo-permeability models make sustained core-wide H2 production by serpentinization uncertain. We therefore explore whether an alternative metabolism based on fermentation-driven heterotrophy could reproduce Cassini H2 and CH4 escape fluxes under limited serpentinization. We develop a coupled ecosystem model of fermentation and methanogenesis in a hydrothermal-ocean-plume framework, using glycine as a proxy for amino-acid-like fermentable organics. With effectively no abiotic H2 and CH4 input, purely heterotrophic communities with fermenters and methanogens can reproduce both Cassini-derived flux intervals given sufficient hydrothermal glycine-equivalent supply, while the estimated organic stock could sustain the required H2 production on gigayear timescales if fully available. We then test a scenario with input ranges for fermentable organic matter, H2, and CH4 informed by asteroid-sample measurements and geochemical estimates of limited serpentinization. Simulations within both Cassini-derived flux intervals are concentrated in communities with acetoclastic methanogens; Bayesian model comparison favors the same communities. This holds even allowing for greater abiotic H2 and CH4 inputs that reduce the fermentable-organic supply required to reproduce the plume. We therefore identify fermentation heterotrophy as a testable alternative to purely hydrogenotrophic, serpentinization-dominated biotic interpretations and motivate future plume searches for co-exported fermentation products, including acetate and ammonium, together with measurements of methane carbon isotopes.

#### Insights from the stability of methanol maser flare properties I: G9.62+0.20E
 - **Authors:** S.P. van den Heever, D.J. van der Walt
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13774

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13774

 - **Abstract**
 The brightest 6.7 GHz methanol maser in the Galaxy is associated with the high-mass star-forming region (HMSFR) G009.62+0.20E. Additionally, this methanol maser shows periodic flaring observed at 6.7 and 12.2 GHz. Several models have been hypothesized to explain the periodicity, including periodic accretion, spiral shocks in the gaps of circumbinary disks, pulsating stars, dicke's superradiance, and a colliding wind binary (CWB) system. We analyzed the time series of the 1.22 km s-1 feature of the 6.7 GHz methanol maser spectrum and the 1.25 km s-1 feature of the 12.2 GHz methanol maser spectrum over the monitoring duration of roughly 20 years, to determine an updated period, analyze the stability and repeatability of the flares, the temporal behavior of the flare profile, and the duty cycle of the flare profile based on the updated period. From these results, it is argued for or against any of the mechanisms that have been proposed to explain the periodicity. Lomb-Scargle (LS) and phase dispersion minimization (PDM) methods were used to determine the methanol maser period at 6.7 and 12.2 GHz. Once the period has been determined, phase-folding or period-folding was used to analyze the flare shape, flare stability, and duty cycle of the flares. The periods of the 6.7 and 12.2 GHz methanol masers were determined to be ~ 243.0 +- 0.3 days. The flares are highly repeatable, stable, and show almost identical shapes, as well as their duty cycles. A replicated kernel density estimator (KDE) of the flare shape reproduces the time series very well over a time span of ~ 8000 days. After careful consideration of the proposed mechanisms for periodic methanol masers, we conclude that all but one, the CWB model, have been ruled out as a possible driver of this flare profile. It has also been established that the CWB model can qualitatively explain the flare profile of the periodic methanol masers.
#### Adiabatic evolution of hadronic neutron stars
 - **Authors:** Domenico Logoteta, Francesco Zeno Constanzo, Ignazio Bombaci, Sebastiano Bernuzzi, Albino Perego, David Radice
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Nuclear Theory (nucl-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13926

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13926

 - **Abstract**
 In this paper we compute several structural properties of static and rotating neutron stars, considering both rigid and differential rotation. To this end, we use a finite-temperature beta-stable equation of state (EOS) extending the zero-temperature hadronic EOS derived by Fortin et al. (2017). We account for the formation of hyperons and the presence of a neutrino-trapped component. Our goal is to provide insights into the long-term evolution of a protoneutron star (PNS) formed after a core-collapse supernova (CCSN) or a remnant resulting from a binary neutron star merger (BNSM). The EOS is derived using a relativistic mean-field quantum many-body theory within the Hartree approximation. Coupling constants are determined using experimental data on hypernuclei and hypernuclear matter to better account for uncertainties in hyperon single-particle potentials. Our EOS models reproduce the typical features of cold neutron stars and PNSs for nucleonic and hyperonic compositions. In particular, they are consistent with measured masses (2.01 M_sun and 2.14 M_sun for PSR J0348+0432 and PSR J0740+6620) and dimensionless tidal deformabilities from GW170817. Thermal contributions act differently depending on composition and rotation state. Finally, our evolutionary scheme provides a simple way to interpret the long-term evolution of BNSM remnants or PNSs from CCSNe, complementing dynamical simulations unable to cover full evolutionary timescales.
