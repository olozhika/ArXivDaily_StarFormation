#### Scalar-Assisted Growth of Supermassive Black Holes: Boson-Mass Windows and Observational Constraints
 - **Authors:** Jorge Castelo Mourelle, Darío Núñez Zúñiga, Saeed Fakhry, Nicolas Sanchis-Gual
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18986

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18986

 - **Abstract**
 We study a phenomenological cosmological model of black-hole growth in which a finite baryonic reservoir and a bosonic scalar cloud feed the black hole simultaneously. The baryonic component accretes through an Eddington-like channel, while the scalar cloud transfers mass through an effective quasibound-state decay rate. Evolving the system directly in redshift within a flat $\Lambda$CDM background, we scan over $\mu$, $M_0$, $M_{c,0}$, and $M_{b,0}$ to assess whether scalar-assisted accretion can accelerate the formation of $10^9M_\odot$ supermassive black holes. We find that the scalar contribution is highly selective in parameter space. For the mass scales explored here, scalar transfer is most efficient for $\mu\sim10^{-19}-10^{-16} \mathrm{eV}$, producing rapid cloud depletion and significantly earlier black-hole growth, while outside this interval the evolution remains mainly baryonic. More generally, this efficient window reflects a preferred range of the dimensionless coupling $\alpha=\mu M_{\rm BH}$ and therefore shifts approximately as $\mu\propto M_{\rm BH}^{-1}$. Comparison with observed quasar masses and luminosities instead favors lower boson masses, broadly around $\mu\sim10^{-21}-10^{-20}\mathrm{eV}$. These results are not necessarily in tension, since the boson mass that maximizes early growth depends on the characteristic black-hole mass and evolutionary stage. This separation between dynamical efficiency and observational compatibility leaves open complementary roles for different bosonic mass scales in supermassive black hole growth and dark-matter phenomenology.
#### The mass functions of multiple stellar populations in 47 Tucanae
 - **Authors:** M. Scalco (1), J. Bruce (1), E. Vesperini (1), L. R. Bedin (2) ((1) Indiana University, (2) INAF-OAPD)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18996

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18996

 - **Abstract**
 We used a combination of James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Hubble Space Telescope (HST) observations to investigate the mass functions (MFs) of the different stellar populations in the globular cluster (GC) 47 Tucanae (NGC 104) over a wide stellar-mass interval. We identified the multiple populations along the lower main sequence using only JWST photometry and, in particular, colour combinations sensitive to variations in oxygen abundance. We derived the MFs of the first- and second-population (1P and 2P) stars separately and found that in the low-mass regime, the present-day MF of the 1P population is significantly steeper than that of the 2P population, which appears nearly flat. We also present the results of a dynamical model showing that this behaviour requires a different initial mass function (IMF) for the 2P and the 1P populations in the 0.1-0.4 solar masses mass range, thus providing a new fundamental constraint for the study of the star formation of multiple populations in GCs.

 - **Abstract**
 We present the first substellar object in a star-forming region spectroscopically confirmed with Euclid, namely J054158.70-021031.4. This young and faint source is located close to the embedded open cluster NGC 2023. It has a spectral type of L3, a reddening of Av = 3 mag, proper motion consistent with the nearby cluster NGC 2024, and a magnitude of $\sim24.5$ in the Euclid optical band. At the distance of 403 pc, its inferred mass lies close to the planetary-mass boundary. We discovered the object by searching for ultracool dwarf candidates in the Euclid Early Release Observations. This work demonstrates the potential of Euclid to unveil previously hidden populations of very low-mass objects, located in obscured regions where star formation is ongoing.

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19096

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19096

 - **Abstract**
 On April 21, 2023, a complex filament eruption produced an Earth-directed coronal mass ejection (CME) that triggered the first severe geomagnetic storm of solar cycle 25. The primary objective of this work is to understand the physical origin of the severe impact of this CME on Earth. We explore the CME's complex morphology by simulating the eruption using the thermodynamic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) model CORHEL-CME. We model the pre-eruptive structure as two intertwined magnetic flux ropes and validate the early evolution of the event by comparing synthetic observables generated from the model with extreme-ultraviolet and white-light observations. We then compare the simulated CME structure with in situ measurements. From our analysis, we conclude that the CME merged with a stream interaction region (SIR) before reaching Earth. This interaction compressed the CME sheath, producing a sustained negative $B_n$ component that, together with the associated enhancement in solar wind density and velocity, triggered the geomagnetic storm. The internal configuration of the magnetic flux rope, which also maintained a sustained southward component during its propagation toward Earth, intensified the storm to severe levels. This model-data comparison underscores the power of using MHD simulations, when validated by multi-viewpoint observations, to reveal hidden dynamical processes and improve the understanding and prediction of space weather impacts.

 - **Abstract**
 Obtaining accurate estimates of the gas column density $\mathrm{N(H_2)}$ is crucial to determine the physical conditions of the molecular gas in the Interstellar Medium. This paper introduces the "Multi-Tracer Column Density Synthesis" (MT-CDS) technique, a new empirical approach to derive high dynamic-range column density maps with interferometers such as ALMA. We have compared the integrated intensity maps of two density selective tracers, namely HNC (1-0) and $\mathrm{N_2H^+}$ (1-0), obtained with IRAM-30m with ancillary Herschel column density estimates in seven star-forming regions in Orion and derived individual temperature-corrected conversion factors for each of these tracers. We applied these conversion factors obtained at low-resolution into similar high sensitivity ALMA observations to derive independent column density maps describing both the low $N_\mathrm{HNC}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ and high $N_\mathrm{N_2H^+}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ column density components at high spatial resolution (4.5"). We also combined these independent maps into estimates for the total gas column density gas $N_\mathrm{tot}\mathrm{(H_2)}$ sampling more than two orders of magnitude in column density between $\sim 10^{21.5}-10^{23.5}$ cm$^{-2}$. A comparison with independent ArTeMiS column density maps at intermediate resolution (8") shows typical deviations of $\lesssim$0.2 dex from our MT-CDS gas column density estimates. We obtained individual column density maps in five of our targets at $\sim$2000 au resolution in which ALMA observations were available, that is a factor of $\sim$5 better than originally calibrated. These results demonstrate the potential of this MT-CDS technique to retrieve accurate, high dynamic range $N\mathrm{(H_2)}$ total gas column density estimates at interferometric resolution combining multiple molecular line tracers.

 - **Abstract**
 Powerful X-ray flares from young stars may rapidly alter the physical and chemical conditions of their planet-forming disks, but direct observational tests remain scarce. We present a Chandra-JWST/NIRSpec study of infrared spectral variability associated with pre-main-sequence (PMS) X-ray flares in the Orion Nebula. Combining six Chandra/ACIS-I observations with three NIRSpec-MOS visits, we analyze two-epoch spectra of 10 X-ray Flare stars and 12 matched non-flaring Controls among 84 observed disk-bearing PMS stars. We investigate three diagnostics: narrow 2.8-4.3 micron features, HI Br-alpha, and the CO fundamental band. This pilot study demonstrates the potential of coordinated Chandra-JWST observations to probe how young stellar magnetic activity affects planet-forming environments, motivating future monitoring to build larger superflare samples and extend them into the rare X-ray mega-flare regime.

 We present a systematic spectroscopic study of hydrogen Brackett-alpha (Br-alpha) and carbon monoxide (CO) fundamental-band (4.3-5.2 micron) emission from a homogeneous sample of 0.2-1.5 Msun pre-main-sequence (PMS) stars in the Orion Nebula observed with JWST/NIRSpec-MOS as part of our Chandra-JWST project. Br-alpha luminosity correlates strongly with accretion luminosity (Lacc), even after excluding sources within 2' of Theta1 Ori C, the nebula's main ionizing O-type star, whose radiation may bias proplyd Lacc estimates. Overall, the CO results tentatively indicate declining warm-gas emission as disks evolve, modulated by accretion-powered thermal and ultraviolet excitation, although variations in emitting area inherited from natal core conditions may also contribute.
#### Twisted Pseudodisk and Asymmetric Mass Accretion on the Circumstellar Disk II: Parameter Survey
 - **Authors:** Masahiro N. Machida, Shingo Hirano, Shantanu Basu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19297

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19297

 - **Abstract**
 Using three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic simulations accounting for ohmic dissipation, we investigate the long-term evolution of the infalling envelope for $\sim 100,000$\,yr after protostar formation. The primary control parameter is the initial misalignment angle $\theta_0$ between the rotation axis and the magnetic field, varied from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$. In all misaligned models ($\theta_0 \ne 0^\circ$), the pseudodisk (or infalling envelope) surrounding the circumstellar disk develops a highly distorted and complex morphology. Gas accretion from the envelope proceeds through discrete, channel-like flows, producing asymmetric streams rather than axisymmetric infall. Protostellar outflows are launched even in these complex environments, and inflow and outflow regions are strongly interwoven. Outflows transport angular momentum anisotropically, leading to time-dependent changes in disk orientation and outflow direction, which further enhance envelope asymmetry. We examine the relative orientation between gas flows and magnetic fields and find that, although statistical tendencies exist, accretion streams cannot be uniquely characterized as either parallel or perpendicular to the magnetic field, particularly in strongly misaligned cases. We also show that most infalling gas accretes at nearly the free-fall velocity, with only a minor fraction significantly slowed within the pseudodisk. Asymmetric accretion and stream-like structures persist beyond 10,000\,au, demonstrating that large-scale asymmetry can arise intrinsically. Our results show that complex streams and asymmetric envelopes naturally emerge from magnetized core physics, even in the absence of initial turbulence or external gas accretion.

 - **Abstract**
 Single hot subdwarfs are believed to form through mergers of helium-core white dwarfs, which can lead to large-scale magnetic fields generated during the merger, and may be observable at the surface once they evolve into the stable core-helium burning phase. As a post-merger product, the orbital angular momentum of the binary system that forms it, could lead to a rapidly rotating hot subdwarf.  - **Authors:** Evgenii O. Vasiliev
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19771

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19771

 - **Abstract**
 Dust grains produced in the supernova (SN) ejecta at early ages are partially destroyed in hot gas of the SN remnant. Large grains owing to their inertia can overtake the forward shock front of the remnant and move through the ambient interstellar medium. Using one-dimensional gas dynamics including the dust destruction we study the transport of dust grains inside the SN remnant and the ambient medium. Here we estimate the surviving and escaping mass fractions depending on the cut-off (maximum) size in a power-law distribution of grain sizes. We found that these fractions follow closely each other at late age of the SN remnant, i.e. almost all grains, which have survived in the remnant, leave it for the ambient interstellar medium. At the age of 30 kyr -- the onset of the radiative phase with density $n\sim 1$ cm$^{-3}$ with solar metallicity -- the escaping/surviving mass fractions for the cut-off size of several microns reach about $0.7-0.8$. The major part of these grains have size larger than $1~\mu$m and initially located at distances $\gtrsim 0.2$ of the ejecta core radius. Later dust grains at velocity of several hundreds km/s escaping the remnant continue to be destroyed due to kinetic sputtering in the interstellar medium. Grains with sizes of several microns are destroyed marginally and therefore will survive in the interstellar gas. After decelerating of grains in the medium the surviving mass fraction of grains produced in the SN remnant, which evolves in an ambient gas with $n$ from 1 to 100 cm$^{-3}$, is from $\sim 0.5$ to $\sim 0.3$, respectively, for the cut-off grain size $a_{cut} = 10~\mu$m. A decrease of the cut-off size in the distribution results in lower surviving mass fraction.

#### Rotochemical heating of neutron stars in 2D
 - **Authors:** Mikhail V. Beznogov, Jerome Novak, Dany Page, Adriana R. Raduta
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20423

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20423

 - **Abstract**
 The matter inside a spinning-down neutron star (NS) undergoes a slow compression that may drive it out of chemical equilibrium and, consequently, trigger non-equilibrium nuclear reactions that can heat up the star. This phenomenon is called rotochemical heating, and it is relatively well-investigated in the literature. Historically, it has been studied almost exclusively via 1D spherically symmetric models. However, rapidly rotating NSs are not spherically symmetric. We present pioneering simulations of rotochemical heating in full General Relativity in 2D axially symmetric models without relying on the slow rotation approximation. We explicitly follow thermal, mechanical, and chemical evolution of rotating NSs as they spin down taking into account both radial and angular distributions of temperature, composition, and mechanical structure. The simplest model is employed: only nucleonic composition, only the non-equilibrium modified Urca process as the main heating/cooling mechanism, and no superfluidity. We analyze the impact of the magnetic field strength as well as the effects of the heat blanketing envelope, initial rotation frequency, NS mass, and core EOS. Most of our conclusions coincide with the results of previous works on this topic, with one important exception. We have found a strong dependence of the cooling curves during the rotochemical heating phases on the core EOS, whereas in the literature it was regarded as very weak. The most likely explanation for this discrepancy is that the authors of the previous works have not considered EOSs with wide enough range of variation of the symmetry energy as a function of density.
