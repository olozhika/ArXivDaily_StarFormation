 - **Abstract**
 The deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio (D/H) has provided key insights into the origin and evolution of the Solar System. Using the James Webb Space Telescope, we detected deuterated methane at $48\sigma$ in a mature giant exoplanet, $\epsilon$ Ind Ab. We measure the planet's D/H ratio to be $(4.4 \pm 0.7) \times 10^{-5}$, which is similar to those of Uranus and Neptune but about twice those of Jupiter, Saturn, and the protosolar nebula. $\epsilon$ Ind Ab's heavy elements (C, O, N, and S) are uniformly enriched to a level similar to Jupiter's.  - **Abstract**
 The double detonation model is a viable channel to explain many observed Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia), with thin helium shelled white dwarf (WD) progenitors of 0.9--1.1 M$_\odot$ reproducing normal SNe Ia observables. Recent work has also shown that all C/O WDs $\leq$ 1.0 M$_\odot$ can sustain a helium shell detonation that triggers a detonation of the underlying core.  - **Abstract**
 The transition from atomic hydrogen (HI) to molecular hydrogen (H$_2$) is the first step in the evolution from diffuse interstellar gas to dense star-forming cores. HI narrow self-absorption (HINSA), produced by cold atomic gas mixed with molecular material, provides a direct tracer of the HI-to-H$_2$ transition in molecular clouds. Existing HINSA analyses typically model HINSA as a single Gaussian component with a uniform spin temperature. This approach cannot recover the radial HI distribution and yields large errors for young cores whose profiles deviate from a Gaussian shape. Here we present hinspheres, an open-source framework that models the core as onion-like spherically symmetric concentric shells, solves radiative transfer along the line of sight and jointly fits the HI abundance, temperature, and kinematic parameters. The framework recovers all parameters with a mean relative error of 4.6-6.8% on our synthetic data with different ages. A combined resolution-noise grid shows that the median recovery error remains below 16% for cores within 1 kpc observed at FAST resolution with noise levels below 0.2 K. Applying it to the dark cores L1574 and L1544, we reveal radial HI abundance profiles that span about three orders of magnitude, which were missed by the single-Gaussian method. The derived chemical ages are $6.5\times10^6$ and $8.7\times10^6$ yr, respectively. Compared with the commonly used single-Gaussian method, hinspheres characterizes the radial profile of HINSA structures more accurately, making it well suited to identifying and analyzing young cores undergoing the HI-to-H$_2$ transition.

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate whether Solar System dynamics can provide independent constraints on the present day cosmological deceleration parameter $q_0$, offering a complementary approach to conventional cosmological probes. By relating cosmic acceleration to a weak Hooke type radial perturbation, we derive bounds using planetary perihelion precession, trans-Neptunian objects, and Oort cloud dynamics.  - **Abstract**

An alternative explanation is devolatilization of the planetary abundance ratios with respect to the host star, where volatile elements such as O and S are progressively depleted compared to refractory elements such as Fe and Mg, as in Earth. We explore whether these elemental molar ratios are consistent with a devolatilization trend. Therefore, developing methods to probe these different timescales is a crucial part of understanding star cluster assembly and early evolution. Dynamical ages can be constructed from the kinematics of individual stars in clusters in the solar neighborhood using Gaia data.
