#### line racer: Rapid Calculation of Exoplanetary Radiative Opacities
 - **Authors:** David B. Hägele, Paul Mollière, Michiel Min
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33290

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33290

 - **Abstract**
 Detailed studies of exoplanet and brown dwarf atmospheres rely on precise knowledge of the spectral features of possible atmospheric species. These features are a result of the interaction of different molecules and atoms with the radiation of the host star or the intrinsic thermal radiation of the object. The interaction is described by the opacity of a species, which determines how much light is absorbed at a given wavelength. The strength and shape of these opacities are very dependent on the temperature and pressure of the atmosphere, which is why they have to be calculated for a wide range of conditions. line racer is a Python package that enables computing high-resolution opacities from large molecular and atomic line lists in an effective manner. It offers users a wide range of options to customize opacity calculations to their needs and available hardware. The code is designed for efficient parallelization on multi-core and multi-node systems and can produce outputs compatible with popular atmospheric modeling and retrieval tools.
#### The fate of Mercury's ejected mantle
 - **Authors:** Tuhin Ghosh, Mark Wyatt, Alan P. Jackson, Oliver Shorttle, Maximilian Sommer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33297

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33297

 - **Abstract**
 Mercury's unusually high core mass fraction, inferred from its high bulk density, remains difficult to explain. Mantle stripping by a giant impact provides a plausible origin, but without efficient removal, the ejected debris remains on Mercury-crossing orbits and later gets re-accreted. Here, we show that re-accretion of impact debris onto Mercury is inefficient, making a giant-impact origin plausible. We combine N-body simulations of giant-impact ejecta with a collisional evolution model and consideration of processes acting on small dust. We find that, when the ejecta are dominated by large, several hundred km-sized non-vaporized fragments, the debris disk remains optically thin and the collisional cascade is truncated near the radiation-pressure blowout limit, where micron-sized grains are removed. In contrast, if vaporized, the ejecta recondense into mm to cm-sized droplets and form an optically thick disk, in which radiation pressure cannot efficiently truncate the cascade. The debris can then be ground down to nanometre-sized grains, where collisions become predominantly non-fragmenting and dissipative rather than destructive. Subsequent inelastic collisions damp the eccentricities and inclinations of the nanodust, producing a geometrically thin disk whose lifetime is governed by removal through exposed surface layers, where grains acquire large charge-to-mass ratios through solar UV radiation and are rapidly removed by electromagnetic forces. In either scenario, collisional evolution strongly reduces the re-accretion of debris and alleviates a key difficulty for the giant-impact origin of Mercury's large core. More broadly, highly energetic giant impacts in the inner regions of planetary systems may lead to short-lived populations of nanometre-sized dust.
#### Planetary biotechnospheres, biotechnosignatures and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33404

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33404

 - **Abstract**
 The concept of planetary intelligence as collective intelligence is used to consider possible evolutionary paths of biotechnospheres that emerge on the intersection of the technosphere with the biosphere and support coupling of the technosphere with the biosphere, thus affecting planetary evolution. In mature biotechnospheres, the intelligence of technologies and the intelligence of life forms, including engineered life forms, could act in concert to perform various tasks (e.g. monitoring and restoring planetary environments and biodiversity; supporting space missions; terraforming cosmic objects). Space exploration can expand biotechnospheres beyond planets and create cosmic ecosystems. Humankind, other civilizations or their intelligent machines may produce biotechnosignatures (i.e. observables and artefacts of biotechnospheres) in the Solar System and beyond. I propose ten possible biotechnosignatures and strategies for the search for these biotechnosignatures in situ and over interstellar distances. For example, if a non-human advanced civilization existed and built biotechnospheres on Earth in the past, the descendants of its engineered bacteria could currently exist on Earth and have properties pertaining to the functions of the ancient bacteria in the biotechnospheres; intelligent technologies created by the ancient civilization could migrate to the Solar System's outer regions; these two scenarios are described as the Cosmic Descendants hypothesis. Interstellar asteroids, free-floating planets, spacecraft and objects gravitationally bound to flyby stars might carry extraterrestrial biotechnospheres and pass through the Solar System. In connection to the fate of post-main-sequence stars and their Oort clouds, the probability for interstellar asteroids to carry biotechnospheres or to be interstellar spacecraft is estimated as very low.
#### Over a Half-decade Study of Crab Giant Pulses Observed with I-LOFAR
 - **Authors:** Owen A. Johnson, David J. McKenna, Sai C. Susarla, Evan F. Keane, Letizia Vincetti, Joseph P. McCauley, Aaron Golden
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33524

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33524

 - **Abstract**
 This work details the analysis of 1,048,138 single pulses from the Crab pulsar detected with the Irish LOFAR station over a 6-year observation campaign spanning 154 epochs and 347.9 hours at 110-190 MHz. Of the detections in the sample 550,445 are giant pulses (GPs, with S/N $\geq$ 10), making it the largest analysed sample to date. The power law indices for the S/N, flux density and fluence distributions range from 3.0-3.4 for the main pulse population and 3.3-3.5 for the inter-pulse population. A subset of 5,474 GPs (with S/N $\geq$ 100) were used to model scattering across the observing band to investigate the entanglement of dispersion measure (DM) and scattering. It was found that DM and scattering have a strong correlation coefficient of 0.70-0.71 across the entire campaign. Removing the long-term trend using a running median windowed at 125-190 days lowers the correlation to 0.48-0.50 respectively. The implications of these DM and scattering observations from the perspective of both the Crab Nebula's structure and the solar wind are discussed, including a rise in DM from August to October 2024 indicating AU-scale density variation consistent with fine substructure within Rayleigh-Taylor shaped filamentary ejecta.
#### Fast radio bursts track cosmic star formation history with time delay: the effect of burst fluence
 - **Authors:** X.D. Jia, D. H. Zhai, J. P. Hu, S. X. Yi, F. Y. Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33906

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33906

 - **Abstract**
 Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are luminous millisecond-duration radio transients of extragalactic origin, yet their progenitor systems remain unidentified. The redshift evolution of the FRB event rate provides a powerful, model-independent discriminant between young progenitors and old progenitors, but recent studies have reached conflicting conclusions. In this work, we perform a forward-modeling analysis of the apparent non-repeating FRB sample from the CHIME/FRB catalogs to constrain the intrinsic FRB population. We find that the FRB rate follows the star formation rate (SFR) with little time delay when both dispersion measure (DM) and fluence are considered. In contrast, when only DM is taken into account, a significant time delay ($3 \sim 4$ Gyr) exists between the FRB rate and the SFR. The main reason for this discrepancy is that systematic biases exist in the mapping process from burst fluence to signal-to-noise ratio. These results support that at least a fraction of FRBs arise from a progenitor population whose event rate is delayed relative to star formation.
#### Mass Spectral Evidence for Iron Sulfate in Venus' Cloud Aerosol
 - **Authors:** Rakesh Mogul, Mikhail Yu. Zolotov, Michael J. Way, Sanjay S. Limaye
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33945

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33945

 - **Abstract**
 Venus' cloud aerosols play pivotal roles in the thermal, chemical, and optical properties of the atmosphere. Here, we present mass spectral evidence that the cloud aerosols contain abundant iron sulfates. Data from the Pioneer Venus Large Probe Neutral Mass Spectrometer (LNMS) suggest that cloud aerosols inadvertently collected into the inlets at ~48-51 km and thermally decomposed below the clouds, releasing several compounds into the LNMS. At 23 km and below, multiple iron species (54Fe+, 56FeO+, 54FeO+, 56Fe2O+, and 56Fe2O2+) and other decomposition gases were detected. Quantitative treatment of these mass signals suggested the release of Fe2O3, SO2, O2, SO3, H2SO4, and H2O, consistent with the thermal decomposition of hydronium-bearing ferric sulfates. The resulting mole ratio for iron to sulfate (2.0 +/- 1.1) aligns with hydronium jarosite ((H3O)Fe3(SO4)2(OH)6), which likely formed within the inlets after aerosol capture, dehydration, and loss of sulfuric acid. Our thermochemical calculations support these assessments. Speciation of the ferric sulfates in the native aerosols remains unconstrained (e.g., solution and/or particle phases). Peak fits to the thermal release profiles yielded the abundances of the bulk iron (0.8 +/- 0.4 mg m-3) and aerosol-derived compounds, including hydronium jarosite (2.2 +/- 1.2 mg m-3, 30 +/- 17 wt%), water (3.7 +/- 0.4 mg m-3, 50 +/- 10 wt%), and sulfuric acid (1.5 +/- 0.2 mg m-3, 20 +/- 5 wt%). Surface sourcing of the cloud iron is likely minor. Instead, cosmic dust influx could deliver iron to the clouds in a geologically short time (2.4 +/- 1.5 Myr).
#### The role of hierarchy in Galactic molecular clouds
 - **Authors:** Kotora Sasaki, Nario Kuno, Hajime Fukushima, Shingo Nozaki
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33956

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33956

 - **Abstract**
 To probe the role of hierarchical gas structures in massive star formation, we statistically analyzed 13 Galactic massive star-forming regions using FUGIN CO survey data. Applying a non-binary dendrogram to the position-position-velocity data, we classified the extracted structures into trunks, internal leaves (the densest substructures within trunks), and isolated structures, and compared their properties with an analysis of variance controlling for regional differences. In representative regions, the trunks correspond to morphologies such as filaments and hub-filament systems, or to kinematic signatures of structure-forming events such as cloud-cloud collisions. Across the sample, the trunks are extended, massive, and strongly bound reservoirs, while the leaves are significantly more massive, denser, and more tightly bound than isolated structures of similar spatial scale. Using YSOs and Hi-GAL clumps, we find that the leaves have a lower low-mass star formation efficiency than isolated structures, whereas their clump formation efficiency is comparable. At a fixed gas mass, the leaves show no higher low-mass star formation efficiency than isolated structures but convert a larger fraction of their gas into dense clumps. We interpret these contrasts, together with inward gas motions in the position-velocity diagrams, as indicating that the deep potential of the trunks sustains continuous accretion onto the leaves and promotes mutual interactions among them. These processes may raise the characteristic fragmentation mass, as theoretically expected for a dynamically accreting medium, so that a larger fraction of the gas forms massive clumps without a corresponding increase in low-mass star formation. Our single-epoch data constrain only these structural contrasts, but indicate that the gas available for massive star formation is organized on scales larger than a single core.
#### High-energy Electron Transport with Discrete Inverse Compton Scatterings
 - **Authors:** Ruihao Jing, Siming Liu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34320

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34320

 - **Abstract**
 After escaping from pulsar wind nebulae (PWNe), the transport of high-energy electrons in the interstellar medium (ISM) is usually described with the diffusion equation with a continuous radiative energy loss. However, when the electron energy approaches $\sim 100~\text{TeV}$ and the radiative energy loss is dominated by the inverse Compton scattering (ICS), the discrete nature of ICS becomes prominent, and the approximation of continuous radiative energy loss is invalid. Starting from the Klein--Nishina differential scattering rate, this paper constructs an integral-differential equation to describe the stochastic discrete nature of the inverse Compton cooling, and the equation is solved with the Fourier and Laplace transform method. Application to the TeV halo of Geminga pulsar leads to a diffusion coefficient {$\sim 15$\%} lower than that of the continuous energy loss model.

 - **Abstract**
 G22.7$-$0.2 is a middle-aged supernova remnant (SNR; $\tau \sim 3\times10^4$ yr) interacting with the nearby giant molecular cloud GMC 23.3$-$0.4. A handful of X-ray sources have been identified within the SNR radio shell by previous XMM-Newton and NuSTAR observations. #### A Large Envelope Can Explain Super-Puffs
 - **Authors:** Xiumin Huang, Darius Modirrousta-Galian, Dong Lai, Ren Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34674

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34674

 - **Abstract**
 Super-puffs are a class of exoplanets with extremely low bulk densities, having masses of a few to tens of Earth masses but radii comparable to that of Jupiter. Observations suggest that these inflated radii can persist for Gyr timescales, even for planets receiving relatively low stellar irradiation. The physical mechanisms responsible for maintaining such large radii, however, remain poorly understood. Here, we model the thermal evolution of super-puffs with substantial H/He envelopes using a one-dimensional planetary structure and cooling model. We follow the coupled cooling and contraction of the planetary interior and H/He envelope while self-consistently calculating the planet's hydrostatic and radiative--convective structure. We find that planets with H/He envelope mass fractions of approximately $40-60\%$ can retain Jupiter-like radii over Gyr timescales, provided that they begin with sufficiently hot interiors. Massive H/He envelopes substantially delay planetary contraction through two effects: (1) strong thermal blanketing, which slows the cooling of the underlying rocky core, and (2) Kelvin--Helmholtz luminosity generated by the cooling and contraction of the envelope itself, which helps maintain its high entropy and inflated radius. Our results suggest that super-puffs may represent a distinct class of planets that accreted large quantities of nebular gas during formation without undergoing runaway gas accretion.

#### Modeling time series of high-resolution spectra using the asymmetric Hilbert-Schmidt decomposition with application to radial velocity exoplanet searches
 - **Authors:** Kevin Tao, Thomas Loredo, David Ruppert
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Applications (stat.AP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34786

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34786

 - **Abstract**
 We describe a framework for building low-rank separable expansions to model spectroscopic data from systems with time-varying spectra, in settings where the spectral dimension is finely sampled (in wavelength), but the temporal dimension is sparsely and possibly irregulary sampled. The motivating application is detection and characterization of small exoplanets, e.g., Earth-like planets around Sun-like stars. Such planets cannot be seen directly, but can be detected via the minute Doppler shifts of the host star spectrum due to the star's motion as the planet and star revolve around their mutual center of mass. Current instruments are reaching the precision necessary to detect the ~10 cm/s motions corresponding to an exo-Earth. But the small Doppler shifts from such low-amplitude orbital motion can be masked or mimicked by the effects of stellar activity on the surface of the host star. e.g., starspots (dark regions), faculae (bright filaments), etc. One technique astronomers have used to try to model the stellar activity component of the signal is PCA, applied to an ensemble of spectra collected over time. But PCA neglects the continuous function nature of a spectrum at a given time, and the continuous evolution of spectra over time. We address model building from a function approximation perspective, via the asymmetric Hilbert-Schmidt decomposition (HSD). This provides a PCA-like low-rank decomposition of a dynamic spectrum, but it explicitly accounts for time dependence, both estimating temporal basis functions, and accounting for irregular temporal sampling when estimating spectral basis functions. We show that HSD improves the fidelity of a fixed-order model of the dynamic spectrum of a Sun-like star with a starspot, with mean squared error for spectral basis functions many times smaller than achieved via PCA when spectra are randomly sampled sparsely and irregularly in time.

 - **Authors:** Devanshi Singh, Mario Jurić, Željko Ivezić
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34790

 - **Abstract**
 We present pneo, a Bayesian classifier that computes the probability that a single-night tracklet belongs to a near-Earth object (NEO), using only its sky position, angular motion, and apparent magnitude. A tracklet does not determine the object's distance or radial velocity, so pneo integrates a model of the Solar System's small-body populations over all bound orbits consistent with it. The result is the exact posterior probability under that model, with no tuned scores or separately chosen priors: its classifications are optimal for the adopted model, and its probabilities are calibrated. On real discovery tracklets from the NEO Confirmation Page, the classifier is well calibrated and its selection is 1.14 times purer than that of digest2, the classifier now in use, at the same completeness. On simulated tracklets to that depth, its selection is 2 times purer than that of digest2 (96% against 48%). On the Rubin alert stream and Rubin Data Preview 2 its selection is 1.4-1.7 times purer than that of digest2, and its predicted NEO counts are close to those observed. By raising the fraction of follow-up targets that are NEOs, pneo could potentially double the effective capacity of NEO follow-up in the Rubin era.

#### A comparison of two- and one-fluid dust solvers for dusty, supersonic turbulence
 - **Authors:** Narges Vadood, Terrence S. Tricco
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34910

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34910

 - **Abstract**
 We model 3 and 10 $\mu$m dust grains in supersonic, turbulent molecular cloud conditions to compare two different numerical methods solving dust coupled to gas through a drag term. One method models dust and gas as two separate species (two fluid) and the other the combination of dust and gas as a single mixture (one fluid) using the diffusion approximation. Simulations are performed in a 3D periodic box using the Phantom code. The gas probability distributions are consistent with a lognormal distribution for both methods and grain sizes. The dust distributions are different in the two methods, showing discrepancies particularly in regions of low density. The most significant difference between the two methods is in the dust-to-gas ratio distributions. Both methods peak at the mean dust-to-gas ratio of 0.01, but the two-fluid method has wider distributions than the one-fluid method suggesting excess dust concentration in dense filaments. Filaments are where the one-fluid method results are most accurate. The narrowing of the one-fluid distribution is also caused by the stopping time limiter used to maintain the terminal velocity approximation. This artificially makes the mixture more coupled in low-density regions. Our overall conclusion is that both methods are viable for the study of dust in molecular clouds, but that the correct method should be chosen based on the numerical Stokes number regime of the calculation to avoid numerical artifacts.
