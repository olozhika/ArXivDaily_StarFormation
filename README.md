 - **Abstract**
 Context. The star-formation rate (SFR) is one of the key quantities that characterizes the star formation process and has been extensively investigated both observationally and theoretically. Aims. We aim to provide a coherent multi-scale view of star formation by connecting Galactic-scale conditions (kpc) to the properties of individual star-forming clumps at sub-parsec scales. Methods. We perform a set of numerical simulations with large-scale turbulent forcing designed to reproduce physical conditions typical of the solar neighbourhood. Using adaptive zooming down to 0.03 pc, we obtain a population of star-forming clumps that is analyzed and compared with observational catalogues in a companion paper. Results. #### Does the Escape Fraction Prescription Shape Reionization History and Morphology?
 - **Authors:** Pulkit Sheoran, Ankita Bera, Sultan Hassan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36046

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36046

 - **Abstract**
 The escape fraction of ionizing photons, $f_{\rm esc}$, is one of the least constrained quantities of the Epoch of Reionization (EoR), and the opacity of the intergalactic medium prevents its direct measurement at high redshifts. We constrain three power-law parameterizations, $f_{\rm esc}(z)$, $f_{\rm esc}(M_{\rm h})$, and $f_{\rm esc}(z, M_{\rm h})$, against JWST UV luminosity functions and EoR observables (the ionizing emissivity, the neutral hydrogen fraction, and the Thomson optical depth) using Markov Chain Monte Carlo, with priors that allow $f_{\rm esc}$ to either increase or decrease with redshift and halo mass. We then propagate the best-fit models into the semi-numerical simulation SimFast21 and compare their ionization morphology and 21 cm power spectra with those of a constant-$f_{\rm esc}$ model. All models reproduce the observations with nearly identical global reionization histories. The data favor an escape fraction that decreases with increasing redshift, from $\approx 0.8$ at $z = 5$ to $\approx 0.35$ at $z = 15$. The halo-mass-only model prefers $f_{\rm esc}$ increasing with halo mass, but this trend acts as a proxy for redshift evolution. When we vary both dependencies jointly, the data prefer a mass-independent $f_{\rm esc}$. At matched global neutral fraction, the ionization morphology is closely similar for all four prescriptions, and the 21 cm power spectra agree to within $\approx 10\%$. These results suggest that, once calibrated against the same observations, the details of the $f_{\rm esc}$ prescription, which are set by the physics of the interstellar medium, largely average out in both the global observables and the 21 cm power spectrum. Distinguishing them may require statistics beyond the power spectrum, such as sightline statistics of the Ly$\alpha$ and 21 cm forests, or forecasts that exploit their small differences in reionization timing.

 - **Abstract**
 We present discovery of SMASH J004005$-$733351, an intermediate-mass stripped helium star in the Small Magellanic Cloud with a dark companion. The system was identified in a new optical color-selected survey for stripped stars and confirmed through spectroscopic follow-up. We measure an orbital period of $P_{\rm orb}=19.95\pm 0.002$ hours and a radial-velocity semi-amplitude of $K=91.7 ^{+3.2}_{-3.1}\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$. Simultaneous modeling of the optical spectrum and ultraviolet-to-optical spectral energy distribution yields a stripped star evolutionary mass $M_{\rm stripped} = 3.84 \pm 0.54\,M_\odot$, temperature $T_{\rm eff} = 68.3 \pm 6.4$ kK, surface gravity $\log g= 4.76 \pm 0.12$, luminosity $\log(L/L_\odot)= 4.28 \pm 0.13$, radius $R= 0.99 \pm 0.09\,R_\odot$, and an unusually high surface hydrogen abundance of $X_{\rm H, s}=0.63 \pm 0.04$. Binary evolution models predict that the stripped star will refill its Roche lobe after core helium exhaustion, likely producing a stripped or ultra-stripped supernova and ultimately a double NS binary if the system survives. SMASH J004005$-$733351 is among the first known stripped stars with a compact object companion. The system provides a new empirical benchmark for the late stages of binary interaction that lead to (ultra-)stripped supernovae and double NS binaries.

#### Retention of primordial atmospheres through giant impacts
 - **Authors:** Darius Modirrousta-Galian, Jun Korenaga
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36446

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36446

 - **Abstract**
 Rocky planets the size of Earth or larger are expected to undergo one or more giant impacts during the final stages of their formation. Some of these collisions occur after protoplanetary disk dissipation, potentially driving the loss of primordial hydrogen-rich atmospheres through elevated surface temperatures. However, whether giant impacts promote atmospheric loss or retention remains unclear because they both heat planetary interiors and increase atmospheric gravitational binding. Here we model the evolution of rocky planets after their largest post-disk giant impact, accounting self-consistently for the concurrent increase in gravitational binding energy and mantle thermal energy. Whereas the entire accreted mass contributes to the increase in gravitational binding energy, only part of the impact energy raises mantle temperature. Mantle melting consumes energy as latent heat, while the impactor's metallic core transfers little energy to the mantle and remains superheated at depth. Giant impacts therefore increase gravitational binding energy more than mantle thermal energy, shifting planets towards atmospheric retention. Larger, atmosphere-bearing planets emerge preferentially through collisional growth. By contrast, smaller planets without such impacts tend to lose their primordial atmospheres under sustained stellar irradiation. At the population level, these results suggest that sub-Neptunes have, on average, more massive rocky interiors than super-Earths.
#### Three-flavor neutrino Boltzmann transport and flavor instabilities in long-lived neutron-star merger remnants
 - **Authors:** Harry Ho-Yin Ng, Jiabao Liu, Arthur Offermans, Shoichi Yamada, Elias R. Most, Luciano Rezzolla
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36486

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36486

 - **Abstract**
 Binary neutron-star (BNS) mergers are multimessenger sources whose kilonova emission and nucleosynthesis depend sensitively on neutrino microphysics and flavor conversion. Most BNS merger simulations assume $\mu$-$\tau$ neutrino degeneracy in a two-flavor treatment. We investigate the fast flavor instability (FFI) and the collisional flavor instability (CFI) in long-lived BNS merger remnants using axisymmetric general-relativistic three-flavor Boltzmann neutrino transport with a comprehensive treatment of muons and muonic interactions, following a handoff from 3D grey moment-based simulations. Our simulations largely reproduce the parent 3D simulations, recovering trapped weak equilibrium in the core, muonic non-equilibrium in the semitransparent outer disk due to prolonged demuonization, and cold weak equilibrium in transparent regions. Linear stability analysis reveals strong and widespread CFI in all three flavor sectors, including narrow resonance-like structures. Muonic interactions reshape the FFI distribution, shifting the $e\tau$-sector instability toward denser disk regions compared with the extended FFI outside the disk in the two-flavor model, while disk demuonization produces a strong $\nu_\mu$ excess and one-sided muon-lepton-number angular distributions that suppress FFI in the $e\mu$ and $\mu\tau$ sectors. Local three-flavor quantum kinetic equation (QKE) calculations reveal nonlinear coupling among flavor sectors that excites coherence even in an initially stable sector. Diagonal collisional repopulation is decisive for the nonlinear evolution and asymptotic flavor populations, and neglecting it overestimates flavor equilibration. We establish the necessity of three-flavor transport from both classical and quantum perspectives, and underscore the importance of consistently coupling flavor conversion with diagonal collisional repopulation in future CFI subgrid models.

 - **Abstract**

#### Faint and Fast Transients from Double Detonations of Low Mass White Dwarfs
 - **Authors:** Miranda Pikus, Abigail Polin, Dillon Hasenour, Paul Duffell
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37507

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37507

 - **Abstract**
 There is significant evidence that white dwarfs can explode via the double detonation mechanism, whereby an initial detonation in an accreted helium shell triggers a second detonation of the carbon-oxygen core. This scenario has been invoked for white dwarfs near a solar mass to produce Type Ia-like supernovae, but low-mass explosions have been relatively unexplored despite the most common white dwarf being near 0.6 solar masses. We predict faint and fast transients emerging from double detonations of white dwarfs with total masses around 0.6 solar masses and accreted helium shells up to 0.3 solar masses. The models are computed through the moving-mesh nuclear hydrodynamics code Cattails and the Monte Carlo radiation transport code Sedona. A continuum of core-powered and shell-powered transients are produced with no more than 0.03 solar masses of radioactive nickel in the core or shell material, respectively. The light curves are rapid and sub-luminous, peaking near -17 magnitudes within 5 to 10 days. The ejecta is dominated by intermediate mass elements and unburnt core that imprint noticeable features in the spectrum. This is the first parameter survey to investigate double detonations at such low masses, and these systems could produce a historically missed population of supernovae. Our predictive models could help large sky surveys find these events.
#### The Spatially-Resolved Recent Star Formation History of the M81 Disk
 - **Authors:** Vaishnav V. Rao, Eric F. Bell, Adam Smercina, Tobin M. Wainer, Andrew Dolphin, Benjamin Williams, Julianne J. Dalcanton
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37661

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37661

 - **Abstract**
 The nearby M81 galaxy---a tidally interacting $L^*$ spiral disk---offers a unique perspective on the interplay of secular and external processes in shaping galaxy properties. We present the most detailed, panoramic map of M81's stellar populations to date, using archival Hubble Space Telescope ACS photometry from the ANGST survey spanning the full optical extent of its disk. From color-magnitude diagram fitting, we recover spatially-resolved star formation histories (SFHs) over the last $\sim180\,$Myr. M81's global SFH is remarkably steady in the last $\sim180\,$Myr with slight enhancements seen $\lesssim15\,$Myr ago.
