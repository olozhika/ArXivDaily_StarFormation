#### Finite-temperature ab-initio-Informed Nuclear Equation-of-State Tables (FINEST): Nuclear matter uncertainties in core-collapse supernovae
 - **Authors:** Finia P. Jost, André da Silva Schneider, Almudena Arcones, Achim Schwenk, Evan O'Connor, Gerard Navó, Yeunhwan Lim
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Nuclear Theory (nucl-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33879

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33879

 - **Abstract**
 We construct new finite-temperature, composition-dependent equation of state (EOS) tables for astrophysical simulations by implementing a recently developed energy-density functional. The functional is constrained by ab initio nuclear matter calculations and neutron-star observations. A distinguishing feature of this functional is a non-monotonic density dependence of the nucleon effective mass, with an increase beyond saturation density. Using these EOS tables, we investigate the impact of current uncertainties in nuclear matter properties on core-collapse supernova (CCSN) evolution with spherically symmetric simulations employing an effective treatment of convective mixing. The magnitude of the EOS effects depends on the progenitor compactness. Within current constraints, variations in incompressibility, symmetry energy, and high-density pressure produce only modest differences during the early post-bounce evolution. In contrast, the effective mass dependence has a much stronger influence on proto-neutron star contraction and the explosion dynamics in a stellar model with high core compactness. Its non-monotonic density dependence leads to a qualitatively different contraction history and can alter the explosion outcome compared to mean-field effective-mass behaviors. Therefore, among the nuclear matter properties considered, the density dependence of the nucleon effective masses has by far the largest impact on CCSN dynamics. This highlights the importance of realistic effective-mass parameterizations in nuclear EOSs used in astrophysical simulations.
#### Core-Collapse Supernovae from Single and Binary-Stripped Massive Stars - I. Explodability and Nucleosynthesis
 - **Authors:** J. M. Pollin, S. Curtis, C. Fröhlich, E. J. Griffith, A. P. Ji, D. H. Weinberg, J. A. Johnson, R. A. Patton, C. E. Fields
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33954

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33954

 - **Abstract**
 Binary interaction can substantially alter massive-star evolution and pre-supernova structure, changing core-collapse outcomes and nucleosynthetic yields. We compare explodability and nucleosynthesis in 66 non-rotating, solar-metallicity single-star and binary-stripped progenitors spanning zero-age main-sequence (ZAMS) masses of $11$-$45\,{\rm M}_\odot$, using the spherically symmetric PUSH framework and nucleosynthesis post-processing. Both populations explode up to $22\,{\rm M}_\odot$, with binary stripping altering explodability at higher ZAMS masses. Successful explosions span similar energies ($\sim$0.4-1.3$\,$B) and remnant masses ($\sim$1.4-2.0$\,{\rm M}_\odot$). The largest nucleosynthetic differences occur at low ZAMS masses, particularly for material undergoing explosive O and Si burning. The $^{56}$Ni yields and iron-group abundances broadly agree with observations of core-collapse supernovae and the metal-poor star HD 84937. When compared with calculations using time-dependent mixing-length theory and a two-parameter explodability criterion for the same progenitors, our results reveal substantial differences in explodability, with agreement on successful explosions for only 5 cases across all three approaches. Nevertheless, elemental yield patterns can remain broadly similar for models that explode successfully across treatments. Salpeter initial-mass-function-weighted yields of most elements differ by $\sim$20% between populations, with K enhanced by a factor of $\sim$3.4 in the binary-stripped population, driven by increased production in the lowest-mass progenitors. Binary-stripped models show closer agreement with empirical core-collapse S, K, and Ca abundance ratios relative to Mg inferred from APOGEE, while Na remains overproduced in both populations. Together, our results highlight the need to account for binarity when interpreting supernova abundance patterns.
#### Halo formation and distribution around cosmic web
 - **Authors:** Hongfei Yu, Huiyuan Wang, Yangyao Chen, Ziwen Zhang, Hao Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34042

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34042

 - **Abstract**
 We use the ELUCID simulation to study how the local environment affects the formation of dark matter halos and their spatial distribution around the cosmic web. We describe the environment by local density and cosmic-web type. At fixed web type, the halo mass function (HMF) depends strongly on the local density, whereas at fixed density it depends only weakly, though significantly, on the web type; the character of both dependencies changes around the mean density. The halo formation redshift is primarily driven by local density rather than cosmic web. We then introduce the three-dimensional cosmic-web cross-correlation function (3D-WCCF), which expresses the distribution of halos around cosmic web in the local tidal eigenframe. The 3D-WCCF provides numerous anisotropic characteristics. Around all web types, halos are most strongly correlated along the axis of weakest compression, $\mathbf{e}_3$, and least along the axis of strongest compression, $\mathbf{e}_1$. Nearly all cluster-sized halos reside in knots and lie preferentially in the plane perpendicular to ${\bf e}_1$. The 3D-WCCF also provides important information on the geometry and size of filaments, sheets and voids. Moreover, early-forming halos are more strongly correlated with the web than late-forming ones, and this assembly bias is anisotropic around filaments, sheets, and voids, being strongest along $\mathbf{e}_3$, but nearly isotropic around knots.

 - **Abstract**
 EP241021a is a peculiar fast X-ray transient exhibiting a long X-ray plateau and an optical rebrightening, both challenging the traditional jet afterglow model. Whether its nature is a stellar explosion or a tidal disruption event (TDE) remains debated. Here we present late-time HST/WFC3 and Gemini-N/GMOS observations of its host galaxy at $z=0.748$. Nevertheless, an off-nuclear intermediate-mass black hole TDE or a stellar-mass black hole TDE induced by dynamical scattering in a dense stellar cluster cannot yet be excluded.

 - **Authors:** Manja L. Zimmerer, Thomas Siegert, Niklas C. Bauer, Tristan Bouchet, Patrik Ehrmann, Mika A. Gelowicz, Laura Eisenberger, Saurabh Mittal, Tomohiko Oka, Rudi Reinhardt, Manuel R. H. W. Skalka, Dimitris Tsatsis, Hiroki Yoneda, Arianna Vasini

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34259

 - **Abstract**
 The radioactive isotope 26Al is believed to be mainly produced by massive stars and ejected into the interstellar medium through their winds and supernovae. Due to its lifetime of 1.04 Myr, it traces recent nucleosynthesis and star formation. 60Fe is a similarly long-lived (lifetime 3.8 Myr) isotope, and only originating in ccSNe. Stellar evolution models in combination with Galactic Chemical Evolution (GCE) models tend to over-predict the 60Fe/26Al ratio. Population synthesis models that attempt to explain both, the 26Al mass in the Galaxy and its spatial distribution, only match the observational constraints with an enhanced star formation rate (SFR) of >5 Msun/yr. Classical novae may contribute to the 26Al production, potentially lowering the SFR inferred from massive stars and alleviating tension in the 60Fe/26Al ratio. However, previous studies suggest their contribution is negligible or < 10%. In this work we aim to determine the mass distribution of 26Al as a function of Galactocentric radius and estimate the contribution of (very) massive stars and classical novae to the 1.8 MeV signal. We are using 20 yr of INTEGRAL/SPI observations of the gamma-ray line at 1.8 MeV to test different assumptions on the radial structure of 26Al and compare the resulting mass distribution to different GCE models. For our baseline analysis, all tested source models prefer substantial nova contributions, with best-fit fractions of 77-84%. This alleviates tension in the 60Fe/26Al ratio and lowers the SFR from 26Al measurements accordingly to 1-2 Msun/yr. While it is difficult to gauge both contributions directly, next generation MeV telescopes can potentially identify individual massive stars, such as Gamma-2-Velorum.
#### Resolving a Clumpy Disk Wind in 4U 1957+115 with XRISM
 - **Authors:** Paul A. Draghis, Erin Kara, Ehud Behar, Jon M. Miller, Vera Berger, Joheen Chakraborty, Peter Kosec, Christos Panagiotou, Swati Ravi, Fatima Zaidouni
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34478

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34478

 - **Abstract**
 We present a high-resolution XRISM/Resolve observation of the persistent black hole candidate 4U 1957+115, revealing the first detection of an intrinsic disk wind in this source. The absorption is dominated by highly ionized Fe XXV and Fe XXVI and varies substantially throughout the observation, becoming prominent during the final $\sim40$ ks despite a modest decrease of only $\sim5$--$10\%$ in source flux. The observed evolution of the absorption cannot be explained solely by ionization changes driven by the modest continuum variability, favoring an inhomogeneous outflow with evolving line-of-sight properties. Time-resolved spectroscopy reveals substantially narrower absorption features in shorter intervals, with the inferred turbulent velocity decreasing from $\sim350~{\rm km/s}$ in the longer integration to $\lesssim70~{\rm km/s}$ in the shortest selections. This suggests that the broader absorption profiles measured over longer integrations may result, at least in part, from averaging over an evolving wind rather than from intrinsically larger turbulent velocities. Such time averaging can therefore affect the inferred physical properties of the wind and complicate efforts to identify its launching mechanism. Even though the wind carries only a small fraction of the radiative luminosity in kinetic form, its mass-loss rate may remain dynamically relevant. These results highlight the importance of time-resolved, high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy for characterizing the structure and variability of accretion disk winds.

#### Binary Progenitors of Electron-capture Supernovae
 - **Authors:** Liji Zhong, Yong Shao
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34699

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34699

 - **Abstract**
 Electron-capture supernovae (ECSNe) arise from a narrow progenitor parameter space between oxygen-neon white dwarf formation and iron-core collapse supernovae, governed by the competition between core growth and envelope removal. Binary interactions modify this balance through mass transfer and mergers. Using the binary population synthesis framework POSYDON, we model ECSN progenitors across metallicities from $Z/Z_\odot=2$ to $10^{-4}$, focusing on the initial parameter space, pre-supernova stripping, envelope-based classification, surviving companions, post-supernova orbits, and cosmic event rates. The lower edge of the binary ECSN initial primary mass lies near $7\,M_\odot$, owing to efficient convective overshooting in the POSYDON grids, whereas the upper edge increases with metallicity because stronger line-driven winds require larger initial masses to build carbon-oxygen cores within the ECSN range. Stable mass transfer and mergers occupy distinct regions in initial mass ratio and orbital period space. ECSNe in bound binaries are divided into hydrogen-rich Type II ($\sim30\%$), IIb ($\sim10\%$), and Ib ($\sim60\%$) outcomes according to residual hydrogen and helium envelopes. Across all channels, approximately one-third of ECSNe have a bound companion at explosion; the remaining two-thirds do not. Among these bound systems, approximately $90\%$ of the companions are main-sequence stars at explosion. These companions exhibit a bimodal spin distribution, including a rapidly rotating component associated with prior mass transfer. Evolved-star and compact-object companions each account for only a few percent. Combining the formation efficiencies with cosmic star formation and metallicity evolution models yields an intrinsic ECSN rate peaking at $z\sim2-3$ and declining at higher redshifts. Over cosmic history, ECSNe contribute approximately $1\%-4\%$ of all core-collapse supernovae.

 - **Authors:** Ivan Judah Manoraj, Satoshi Okuzumi, Kazuaki Homma

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34908

 - **Abstract**
 In the outer regions of protoplanetary disks, dust grains are often mantled with a layer of ice containing simple molecules. We find that stellar Lyman-$\alpha$ irradiation can produce sufficient photoproducts to produce an organic mantle with an {organic-to-rock ratio of $\approx 10\%$}, which could enhance grain sticking. Our results suggest that FUV-driven organic synthesis may facilitate dust growth and contribute to overcoming barriers to planetesimal formation.

#### The Impact of Flyby Interactions on the Rotation of Open Clusters
 - **Authors:** Kyle R. Tregoning, Jeff J. Andrews
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35805

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35805

 - **Abstract**
 The Gaia astrometric mission has provided the first evidence for the bulk rotation of open clusters. While this rotation may arise from residual spin inherited from a cluster's parent molecular cloud, it is unclear whether this explanation always suffices, as angular momentum is carried away by escaping stars on the timescale of two-body relaxation. In particular, the recent observance of significant rotation ($v_{\rm rot} = 0.81 \pm 0.06$ km s$^{-1}$) in the dynamically old ($\approx 4$ Gyr) open cluster M67 motivates a study of another potential source of spin for Solar neighborhood clusters: tidal torquing from gravitational encounters with passing giant molecular clouds (GMCs). For highly energetic encounters, significant angular momentum can be transferred, but the stars that receive the most angular momentum are ejected, leaving the cluster remnant with negligible net rotation. Even when considering a random walk of many GMC-cluster encounters, the integrated effects of these interactions cannot explain observations of dynamically old rotating open clusters. However, we find that within $\sim 5$ Myr post-encounter, some disrupted clusters could be confused as rotating clusters while their stars are still dispersing. Using the local density of GMCs, we estimate that up to $\approx 1\%$ of open clusters in the Milky Way may carry this faux-rotation signal.
