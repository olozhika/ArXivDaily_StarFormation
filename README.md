#### The Composition of M-dwarf Exoplanet Hosts: Atmospheric Parameters and O, Mg, Si, and Fe Abundances from IR Spectroscopy
 - **Authors:** Zachary G Maas, Natalie R. Hinkel, Gregory N. Mace, Cayman T. Unterborn, Catherine Pilachowski, Christopher Sneden, Isabella Flores, Joseph G. Schulze
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07037

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07037

 - **Abstract**
 Determining M-dwarf abundances is an important step to discerning the chemical evolution of the Milky Way and characterizing exoplanetary systems, especially since these objects commonly host small exoplanets. However, a dearth of M-dwarf abundance measurements exist compared to Sun-like stars, partially due to their complicated optical spectra dominated by molecular absorption lines and low luminosities. To overcome these issues, we have obtained high resolution H-band spectra of 40 planet-hosting M-dwarf stars and 2 M-dwarfs in wide binaries with FGK primaries with IGRINS on Gemini South and on the McDonald Observatory 2.7m telescope, and NIRSPEC on Keck. Abundances were derived by comparing each observed spectrum to synthetic spectral grids generated with MOOG and MARCS atmospheric models. Effective temperatures were derived using OH and H$_{2}$O lines and [Fe/H] from Fe I and FeH lines. Both Mg and Si abundances were determined using atomic features. Overall, we find that our abundances are broadly consistent with previous literature M-dwarf abundances. We complemented our abundances analysis with UVW velocities and Milky Way stellar population membership probabilities and identified 2 potential thick disk stars from our kinematic and chemical analysis: GJ 1252 and L181-1 which was a previously known thick disk star. Kinematic stellar population probabilities and a low [Fe/H] abundance makes Kepler 42 a likely thick disk star, although uncertain Mg and Si abundances make classification difficult. Lastly, we have determined Si/Mg and Fe/Mg molar ratios for M-dwarf exoplanet hosts and find the abundance distributions leads to exoplanet core-mass fractions between 20 - 40$\%$.

 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07044

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07044

 - **Abstract**
 We present a resolved far-infrared/sub-millimetre study of dust in the nearby spiral galaxy M81 using Herschel PACS/SPIRE and JCMT SCUBA-2 observations from 70 to $850\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. We model the dust emission using both single-temperature modified blackbody (SMBB) fits and point process mapping (PPMAP), comparing pixel-by-pixel SED-fitting with image cubes of dust surface density as a function of position and temperature. For fixed $\beta = 1.9$ and a dust opacity of $\kappa_{350\,\mu\mathrm{m}} = 1.92\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}\,\mathrm{g}^{-1}$, SMBB gives an integrated dust mass of $(1.77 \pm 0.09) \times 10^{7}\,M_\odot$, while PPMAP gives $(2.65 \pm 0.13) \times10^{7}\,M_\odot$. The higher PPMAP mass is expected because real lines of sight contain dust over a range of temperatures, whereas SMBB assumes a single effective temperature and can underestimate the dust mass. Both methods show enhanced dust surface densities along the spiral arms and a radially declining dust temperature, with the warmest dust in the central region. Variable-$\beta$ fits indicate only moderate spatial variation in $\beta$ for well-constrained pixels, but the strong $T$-$\beta$ degeneracy motivates a fixed emissivity index for the main analysis. Combining the dust maps with HI data, we find a positive radial gas-to-dust ratio gradient, with $d\log_{10}(\mathrm{GDR})/dR = 0.065\,\mathrm{dex}\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-1}$. Comparison with M81's metallicity gradient implies a declining dust-to-metals ratio, potentially consistent with more efficient grain growth in the inner disc. The star formation rate is modest, $\sim 0.27\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, and is strongly concentrated along the spiral arms. These results suggest that the dust, gas, and star-forming structures of M81 are strongly shaped by its spiral arms, while the central region hosts the warmest dust in the disc.

#### Double Neutron Star Mergers and Fast Blue Optical Transients: A Circumbinary Disk Connection
 - **Authors:** Aldana Grichener, Ruggero Valli, Mathieu Renzo, Ilya Mandel, Kaitlin M. Kratter, Magdalena Siwek, Koushik Sen, Neev Shah
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07326

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07326

 - **Abstract**
 Post-common-envelope evolution (CEE) circumbinary disks (CBDs) form when part of the ejected envelope remains bound and circularizes around the stripped core and its companion. Leveraging results of scale-free hydrodynamical simulations, we develop a framework to model the effect of such disks on subsequent binary evolution, and implement it in the population synthesis code COMPAS. Applying our framework to double neutron star (DNS) merger progenitors, we find that CBDs generally induce substantial orbital shrinkage, changing the merging DNS population and lowering the overall merger rate. We explore an extensive grid of binary-evolution and disk models, and find that CBD interactions reduce the DNS merger rate by up to $28\%$, primarily through 'premature' mergers between the neutron star (NS) and the giant's core. These mergers may power luminous fast blue optical transients (LFBOTs), occurring at a fiducial rate of $\simeq11 \:\rm Gpc^{-3} \rm yr^{-1}$. Our results indicate that natal kicks contribute little to LFBOT offsets, and that they should be associated with young stellar populations, though not necessarily active star-forming regions. Since LFBOT progenitors would otherwise produce short-delay DNS mergers, CBDs further challenge DNS mergers as the source of early r-process enrichment. For surviving DNS mergers, accretion from the disk leads to higher chirp masses, potentially explaining the puzzling mass of GW190425. Our framework provides a general tool for studying CBD-binary interactions in post-CEE systems.

 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07522

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07522

 - **Abstract**
 The Central Molecular Zone (CMZ) combines warm molecular gas, strong turbulence, and a star-formation rate below that expected from its dense-gas reservoir. The JVLA Ammonia C-configuration K-band Survey (JACKS) is designed to connect these environmental conditions to the thermal and kinematic structure of compact, potentially star-forming gas. JACKS follows the footprint of the ALMA Central Molecular Zone Exploration Survey (ACES) with 31 Very Large Array mosaic fields. Its K-band setup covers the metastable NH$_3$ $(1,1)$--$(5,5)$ inversion transitions, five non-metastable NH$_3$ lines, the 22-GHz H$_2$O maser transition, H64$\alpha$ and H66$\alpha$, and broad continuum windows. The nominal resolutions are approximately $1.5''$ ($0.06$ pc at the Galactic Centre) and $0.4$ km s$^{-1}$ for the NH$_3$ lines. We present preliminary imaging products that demonstrate water-maser self-calibration, compact and extended continuum emission, filamentary NH$_3$ structure, and complex compact sources with multiple velocity components. Tests that combine JACKS with the SWAG and HOPS ammonia surveys recover progressively larger angular scales. These early products establish the survey's ability to connect core-scale gas heating and kinematics to the cloud-scale CMZ environment; quantitative temperature, opacity, and core-population measurements will follow from the uniformly reduced data set.
#### Violent relaxation in hierarchical stellar systems: pathways toward massive black hole formation in the early Universe
 - **Authors:** Dominik R.G. Schleicher, Francesco Flammini Dotti, Matías Liempi, Tommaso Zana, Andrés Escala, Muhammad A. Latif, Roberto Capuzzo-Dolcetta, Marcelo C. Vergara, Lorenzo Paparella
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07546

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07546

 - **Abstract**
 Massive stellar systems have been detected in the high-redshift Universe via the James Webb Space Telescope using gravitational lensing techniques. Such stellar systems may evolve on short dynamical timescales, despite a formally large 2-body relaxation time. If the stellar system consists of a large number of gravitationally bound clusters, a 2-body relaxation time can be defined for the star clusters in analogy to the 2-body relaxation time for stars, implying a faster evolution determined by the number of stars per cluster. At their formation, clusters will go through a process of "cold collapse" as part of their early, non-collisional, evolution. This phase enhances the mass segregation between the more massive and less massive population of stars. We derive a criteria based on the mass of the two components that determines under which conditions the massive component may even decouple from the cluster itself. Mergers of clusters and multiple mergers on average tend to produce systems with larger cluster radii. For the formation of massive black holes via dense stellar systems, the most promising pathway consists in the cold collapse of a dense massive cluster, after few mergers (or possibly none). In such a cluster with a sufficient number of massive stars, the massive population of stars will dynamically decouple in the central region and go through core collapse, leading to the formation of a central massive object. In the case of multiple mergers, massive but more spatially extended stellar systems will form.

 Context: The stellar counterpart to the $200^\circ$-long HI Stream from the Clouds probes their tidally stripped stellar populations and constrains their interaction history and structure before infall into the Milky Way. Aims: We investigate the chemodynamics and possible origin of very metal-poor (VMP; [Fe/H] $<-2$) stars in the stellar Stream, targeting distant red giants selected from the Pristine--Gaia synthetic metallicity catalogue. Methods: We obtained high-resolution Gemini/GHOST spectroscopy of 33 candidate members, deriving radial velocities and metallicities. #### Multi-epoch monitoring of the faint northern component in 3C 84 during 2016-2021
 - **Authors:** Kotaro Niinuma, Motoki Kino, Nozomu Kawakatsu, Hiroshi Nagai, Gabriele Giovannini, Filippo D'Ammando, Monica Orienti, Kazuhiro Hada, Kiyoaki Wajima, Marcello Giroletti
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07782

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07782

 - **Abstract**
 We present the results of multi-epoch very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) observations of the sub-parsec-scale northern jet component of 3C 84 (known as the N1 component) at 7 mm from 2016 to 2021. The peak position of the N1 component exhibits positional variations of approximately 0.2 mas at a distance of about 2.5 mas from the central radio core (C1) throughout the observation period. Although a linear fit yields a small proper motion of $\mu_{\rm{Dec}}=0.054\pm0.019~\rm{mas}~\rm{yr}^{-1}$, corresponding to an apparent velocity of $\beta_{\rm{app}}=0.062\pm0.021$, this displacement is comparable to the observed positional scatter, and the hypothesis that N1 exhibits systematic motion is not statistically supported. In contrast, the peak flux density varies by approximately a factor of two on timescales of several months. We propose two possible explanations for the observed behavior of N1. One is that N1 is the counterpart of the southern hotspot C3, but its motion has been halted by a collision with a compact and dense cloud along its path; in this case, the flux variability may be caused by changes in the free-free absorption opacity of the non-uniform cloud. The other possibility is that N1 is part of the northern jet that is heavily obscured by foreground free-free absorption and is observed through a gap in the absorbing medium; in this case, the variability may reflect intrinsic changes in the jet and its interaction with a non-uniform ambient medium. These results provide new constraints on the physical origin of N1 and on the circumnuclear environment of 3C 84.
#### Multi-layer magnetic structure toward NGC 1333 revealed by a square-degree optical polarimetric survey
 - **Authors:** G. A. P. Franco, J. M. Girart, F. O. Alves
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07839

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07839

 - **Abstract**
 Magnetic-field studies of star-forming regions are complicated by overlapping structures along the line of sight. We investigate the large-scale magnetic field toward NGC~1333 in Perseus, accounting for foreground polarization through stellar distances and optical polarimetry. Our wide-field $R$-band survey covers nearly one square degree and provides polarization measurements for approximately 840 stars, with distances from Gaia DR3 and StarHorse. We used polarization-distance trends and Stokes parameters to identify line-of-sight components and estimate foreground polarization. After subtracting it in Stokes space, we characterized the field using circular statistics and the angular dispersion function (ADF), comparing it with molecular-line and complementary polarimetric observations. The data reveal a polarized foreground at 160-300 pc and a cloud component beyond 300 pc. After correction, the plane-of-sky field rotates smoothly from $126.3°\pm0.8°$ in the northwest to $162.2°\pm0.6°$ in the southeast, remaining well ordered in both regions. The ADF gives turbulent-to-ordered field ratios of $0.054^{+0.025}_{-0.022}$ and $0.095^{+0.021}_{-0.047}$, corresponding to plane-of-sky strengths of $59^{+44}_{-23}$ and $46^{+50}_{-13}~\mu\mathrm{G}$. Residual foreground fluctuations may affect these estimates. CO data show multiple velocity components with spatially varying contributions. Corrected optical polarization agrees with the large-scale orientation traced by Planck, while near-infrared and JCMT/POL-2 data reveal localized deviations in denser structures. Our results indicate a coherent cloud-scale magnetic field with a smoothly changing orientation, alongside localized variations in dense structures. They demonstrate the value of combining stellar distances and wide-field optical polarimetry to disentangle foreground contributions.
#### Mass loss in AF supergiants: At the verge of the Eddington limit and still with weak line-driven winds
 - **Authors:** Jiri Krticka, Jiri Kubat
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07986

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07986

 - **Abstract**
 We study global atmosphere models of AF supergiants. In agreement with previous studies, we found super-Eddington layers in the photospheres of these stars, which lead to density inversions. Our tests showed that the super-Eddington layers are caused by hydrogen ionization. The analysis of radial perturbations has not revealed any instability of such layers, but we show that super-Eddington layers in an optically thick environment are prone to instabilities due to nonradial perturbations. We predict the wind parameters of AF supergiants from our global wind code with a comoving-frame radiative force. Despite the existence of density inversions, the predicted mass-loss rates are significantly lower than those estimated from observations. Theoretical values approach the observational estimates only for the most luminous supergiants, which are close to the Eddington limit due to light scattering on free electrons. Part of the discrepancy between the mass-loss rates could be attributed to the influence of the clumping on the observational properties. Low mass-loss rates imply that the winds of AF supergiants may be strongly influenced by the magnetic fields, suggesting an explanation for the observed spectral variability of these stars.
#### Rotational Excitation of HC$_5$NH$^+$ by He: Collision Dynamics and Astrophysical Applications
 - **Authors:** Ziyu Xiang, Liman Tian, Tong Sun, Wenli Zou, Xiaolin Chen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08197

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08197

 - **Abstract**
 Reliable collisional rate coefficients are crucial for interpreting the rotational emission spectra of molecules in interstellar clouds. In this work, we construct a two-dimensional potential energy surface (PES) for the $\mathrm{HC}_5\mathrm{NH}^+$--He system at the CCSD(T)-F12B/cc-pVTZ-F12 level of theory. Rotationally inelastic cross sections are calculated using the quantum close-coupling (CC) method, and the corresponding collisional rate coefficients are obtained by thermal averaging over a Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution. We use these collisional data in a non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (non-LTE) radiative transfer model to investigate the excitation of $\mathrm{HC}_5\mathrm{NH}^+$ in the cold dark cloud TMC-1. A global $\chi^2$ minimization analysis of seven observed lines yields best-fitting values of $n(\mathrm{H}_2)=1.63\times10^{5}$\,cm$^{-3}$ and $N(\mathrm{H}_2)=5.09\times10^{11}$\,cm$^{-2}$, with reduced $\chi^2_{\min}=0.87$. The modelled line intensities are in good agreement with the observations. These results provide insight into the collisional excitation and radiative properties of $\mathrm{HC}_5\mathrm{NH}^+$ under the physical conditions prevailing in TMC-1.
#### The box within the Universe: anisotropies in periodic cosmological simulations I. Dark-matter-only simulations
 - **Authors:** Gábor Rácz, Till Sawala
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08223

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08223

 - **Abstract**
 The vast majority of cosmological simulations employ periodic boundary conditions to achieve statistical homogeneity and to mimic the infinite spatial extent of our Universe. In a cubic periodic domain the Green function breaks full rotational invariance and introduces a geometry-induced anisotropy in the gravitational interaction. We quantify how this intrinsic anisotropy affects cosmic structure growth and investigate the extent to which it biases the commonly used large-scale structure and halo-based observables in dark-matter-only simulations. We analyse an ensemble of 498 cosmological dark-matter-only $N$-body simulations in cubic $T^3$ topological manifolds, evolved to $z=0$, spanning box sizes from $L=6.25\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ to $L=100\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ at fixed mass and force resolution. We compare anisotropy-sensitive statistics across the suite, including the matter power spectrum, halo abundance, merger histories, shapes and alignments, and filament orientations. Finite periodic boundaries imprint orientation-dependent signatures aligned with the box geometry. These signatures propagate beyond the largest modes and can induce systematic shifts in both field-level and halo-level statistics. The detectable effects propagate to scales much smaller than the box size. Our results demonstrate that periodic-box anisotropy is a non-negligible source of systematics for small simulation volumes, including those commonly employed in modern hydrodynamical simulations.
