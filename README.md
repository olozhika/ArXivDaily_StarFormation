 - **Abstract**
 We present multi-wavelength observations and modeling of the largest sample to date of core-collapse supernovae (SNe) with Hubble Space Telescope (HST) ultraviolet (UV) spectroscopy within < 2 weeks of first light. Our total sample contains early-time UV spectroscopy from 12 Type II SNe (SNe II), 3 Type IIb SNe (SNe IIb), 3 Type IIn SNe (SNe IIn) and 1 Type I super-luminous SN (SLSN-I). This sample includes 11 objects with unpublished UV spectroscopy from HST Cycles 31 and 32 "Flexible Thursdays" target of opportunity programs, increasing the number of core-collapse SNe with early-time HST UV spectra by a factor of 2.4. The early-time near-UV spectra of most sample SNe II show similar Doppler-broadened P-Cygni profiles of metals (Mg, Fe, Ti, Cr), though with notable diversity in line velocities and absorption strengths. Comparison to non-Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (nLTE) radiative transfer model grids at similar phases indicates that most sample SNe II are consistent with low circumstellar material (CSM) densities at the time of UV observations (e.g., $\dot M = 10^{-6}$ M$_{\odot}$ yr$^{-1}$, $v_w = 50$ km/s) and standard kinetic energies (e.g., $E_k = 0.6-1.2$ B), while two objects, SNe 2024faq and 2023ixf, show UV/optical evidence for interaction with more confined, higher-density CSM. SN 2023ixf and sample SNe IIn exhibit relatively featureless early-time UV spectra despite displaying prominent optical Lorentzian emission lines from electron scattering in optically thick CSM -- this being attributed to differing properties of UV resonance versus optical recombination transitions as well as intrinsic CSM asymmetries. These results demonstrate the potential of early-time UV spectroscopy to constrain the outermost SN ejecta structure, explosion energetics/composition, and progenitor mass-loss histories of core-collapse SNe.
#### Mapping the multimessenger horizon for $γ$-ray counterparts of cosmic neutrinos
 - **Authors:** Tianyi Ding, Jose Alonso Carpio, Ali Kheirandish, Kohta Murase
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21752

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21752

 - **Abstract**
 The joint detection of high-energy neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays offers a powerful probe of the most energetic astrophysical accelerators, linking hadronic interactions to observable signatures across cosmic distances. Understanding the conditions under which these two messengers can be simultaneously observed is essential for identifying and characterizing the sources of cosmic neutrinos. In this work, we examine the detectability of $\gamma$-ray cascades associated with neutrino production in source environments that are gamma-ray transparent, focusing on how electromagnetic cascades evolve as $\gamma$-rays propagate through intergalactic space. By modeling photon interactions with the extragalactic background light and deflections in intergalactic magnetic fields, we determine the minimum neutrino fluence for transient emission and neutrino flux for steady emission required to produce a $\gamma$-ray counterpart detectable by $\gamma$-ray observatories, such as Fermi-LAT, HAWC, or LHAASO. This defines a ``multimessenger horizon" -- the region in flux-distance parameter space where neutrinos and $\gamma$-rays are jointly observable. We consider both energy-binned injection tied to IceCube's sensitivity and a continuous power-law spectrum extending from TeV to PeV energies. Our results reveal that detectability depends strongly on redshift, magnetic field strength, and photon energy, with higher-energy cascades suffering greater attenuation inside filamentary structures. The framework provides new constraints on source opacity, baryon loading, and neutrino production mechanisms, offering a predictive tool for interpreting current events and guiding future multimessenger campaigns.

 We present a multi-wavelength case study of the star T28 in the Chamaeleon I star-forming region and investigate the dust evolution of its highly Ca-depleted protoplanetary disk. We combine new observations from the MIKE spectrograph on the Magellan Clay Telescope with archival IRS spectrum and photometry. #### Neutron-Rich Disks, Nickel-Rich Ejecta: Can Direct-Horizon Collapsars Explain Early $r$-Process Enrichment?
 - **Authors:** Danat Issa, Ore Gottlieb
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21788

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21788

 - **Abstract**
 Very massive stars above the pair-instability mass gap may collapse directly into black holes surrounded by massive disks. These "direct-horizon collapsars" accrete at $\dot{M} \gtrsim 10\,M_\odot\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and have been proposed to eject a substantial amount of $r$-process material, powering "super-kilonovae". We present the first three-dimensional general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of direct-horizon collapsars with M1 neutrino transport, following a $\approx 150\,M_\odot$ helium core from collapse through the escape of magnetically driven outflows. Extreme accretion rates neutronize the disks to $Y_{\rm e} \approx 0.1$-$0.25$ and, at early times, allow them to satisfy the gravitational-instability and cooling criteria for fragmentation. However, the strong poloidal fields needed to eject the neutron-rich gas promptly also drain the disks and lift their electron degeneracy. The neutron-rich ejecta composition is set in a brief phase, such that the ejecta carry $\sim 0.1$-$0.3\,M_\odot$ of nuclei with $A \geq 69$, including $\lesssim 2\times10^{-4}\,M_\odot$ of lanthanides, alongside $\sim 1$-$4\,M_\odot$ of $^{56}$Ni. Assuming proportional retention and mixing of Eu and Fe, these yields imply $[{\rm Eu/Fe}]\approx-0.4$ to $+0.3$, and simple dilution estimates permit enrichment at $[{\rm Fe/H}]\lesssim-2.6$. Direct-horizon collapsars are therefore potential prompt contributors to iron and $r$-process enrichment at low metallicity. Their predicted Eu/Fe ratios fall below those of strongly $r$-process-enhanced stars, but uncertainties in the relative yields, particularly of iron, may close this gap. The ${}^{56}{\rm Ni}$-rich ejecta would power luminous supernovae with rise times of weeks, potentially accompanied by exceptionally powerful gamma-ray bursts, rather than lanthanide-rich super-kilonovae.

 - **Abstract**
 Jupiter's circumpolar cyclones (CPCs) represent one of the most extreme dynamical regimes of the Solar System, where rotation, stratification, and convection interact to organize a stable polygonal cyclone lattice at each pole. Yet the depth and vertical structure of these cyclones remain unresolved. Here, using Juno Microwave Radiometer observations acquired between May 2023 and May 2024, we retrieve the 3-D thermal/compositional structure of Jupiter's northern CPCs at depths beyond those detectable by Earth-based telescopes and spacecraft that have ever visited. #### Non-monotonic Age-metallicity Relations Across the Galactic Disk Revealed by APOGEE Red Clump Stars
 - **Authors:** Chun Wang, Yang Huang, Haibo Yuan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22091

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22091

 - **Abstract**
 Using APOGEE red clump stars and open clusters with precise ages, we investigate the age--metallicity relation (AMR) across the Galactic disk as a function of Galactocentric radius $R$ and vertical height $|Z|$. For stars older than $\sim$6--8 Gyr, the AMR shows little dependence on either $R$ or $|Z|$ within the uncertainties, suggesting rapid and well-mixed chemical evolution in the early disk. In contrast, younger populations exhibit a clear negative radial metallicity gradient, with the inner disk being more metal-rich than the outer disk, consistent with inside-out disk growth since $\sim$6--8 Gyr ago. Most importantly, at fixed location, the AMR for ages $\lesssim6$--8 Gyr is non-monotonic: [Fe/H] decreases toward younger ages, reaches a minimum at intermediate ages, and then rises again. Such complex behavior cannot be explained by continuous local enrichment alone, but instead points to the combined effects of a multi-phase gas-accretion history and radial migration. Episodes of metal-poor gas inflow can dilute the interstellar medium and reset its chemical evolution, while subsequent enrichment raises the metallicity again; radial migration further reshapes the observed AMR by mixing stellar populations formed at different radii and metallicities. A toy model incorporating inside-out growth, two gas-inflow episodes, and radial migration qualitatively reproduces the observed trends, highlighting the combined roles of episodic gas accretion and radial migration in shaping the chemical evolution of the Galactic disk.

#### Magnetic Flux Eruptions in Magnetically Arrested Disks: Magnetic Reconnection and Bubble Expansion
 - **Authors:** Guoqiang Zhang, Feng Yuan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Plasma Physics (physics.plasm-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22689

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22689

 - **Abstract**
 Using a high-resolution GRMHD simulation of a magnetically arrested disk, we investigate the physical mechanism driving magnetic-flux eruption. The horizon-threading magnetic flux declines mainly in off-equatorial regions, whereas localized hot spots associated with magnetic reconnection appear near the equatorial plane around the onset of flux decline. A non-axisymmetric, magnetically supported low-density structure, which we identify as a magnetic bubble, expands radially and azimuthally within a pre-existing low-density channel in the filamentary accretion flow. Based on these findings, we propose a physical picture linking reconnection, bubble expansion, and horizon magnetic flux decline. The reconnection occurs in the pre-existing low-density channel, and may reconfigure the field and promote bubble expansion. Magnetic-pressure gradients provide broadly distributed outward support for the expansion, while magnetic tension contributes a localized outward force near the equator. Bubble expansion may limit gas replenishment, helping to maintain the low-density region and reduce the total pressure near the equator. This pressure reduction may facilitate equatorward electromagnetic energy transport. In the simulation, the polar electromagnetic energy flux reverses from poleward to equatorward near the onset of the horizon-flux decline. The equatorward transport is concentrated in the bubble sector and carries electromagnetic energy from the off-equatorial magnetosphere to the equatorial region. This energy supply may sustain localized reconnection and plasma heating as the eruption develops.
#### Cataclysmic Variables from XMM-Newton and eROSITA
 - **Authors:** Daniela Muñoz-Giraldo, Beate Stelzer, Axel Schwope
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22739

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22739

 - **Abstract**
 Cataclysmic variables (CVs) are interacting binaries where a white dwarf accretes material from a late-type donor. The most evolved systems, referred to as period-bouncers, host degenerate donors. We present XMM-Newton and eROSITA, which provide complementary views of the CV population. We summarize the capabilities of XMM-Newton for timing and spectroscopic studies, and its ability to characterize accretion geometries, magnetic field strengths, and short-term variability across magnetic/non-magnetic CVs. These data allow studies of individual systems, including magnetic period-bouncers with soft X-ray spectra and faint luminosities that offer insights into the late stages of CV evolution. We then present an overview of eROSITA as the first X-ray all-sky survey in the soft band since ROSAT, and its impact on population studies. The eRASS:4 catalog expands the known X-ray sky and will enable volume-limited samples of CVs, complete up to 500pc for standard CVs and up to 250 pc for period-bouncers. Cross-matching eROSITA detections with optical catalogs produced the largest sample of X-ray-selected CV candidates to date, that will refine X-ray diagnostic diagrams, reassess the demographics of magnetic/non-magnetic systems, and identify new highly evolved CVs. Finally, we describe ongoing searches for unknown systems, including CVs selected through probabilistic cross-matching with Gaia, and period-bouncers identified through a multi-wavelength scorecard. These efforts led to the discovery of new period-bouncers and a promising detached period-bouncer candidate, that could validate alternative models for the evolution of CVs. Together, XMM-Newton and eROSITA provide an unprecedented view into the structure, evolution, and demographics of CVs, with eROSITA laying the groundwork for future studies of compact binary evolution, the Galactic Ridge X-ray emission, and related phenomena.
#### Solar atmospheric gravity wave phase differences: insights from MANCHA simulations and observations
 - **Authors:** Hirdesh Kumar (1,2), E. Khomenko (1,2), Tobias Felipe (1,2), C. Quintero Noda (1,2) ((1) Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias, 38205 C/ Vía Láctea, s/n, La Laguna, Tenerife, Spain; (2) Departamento de Astrofísica, Universidad de La Laguna, 38205 La Laguna, Tenerife, Spain)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22751

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22751

 - **Abstract**
 Phase-difference and coherence diagnostics are widely used to identify gravity waves in the solar atmosphere, but their sensitivity to opacity and radiative-transfer effects remains poorly understood. We investigate the robustness of gravity-wave signatures in velocity and temperature diagnostics and their appearance in spectroscopic observables. We perform k-/omega phase-difference and coherence analyses using three-dimensional radiative hydrodynamic simulations with the MANCHA code. Diagnostics are evaluated at constant geometrical heights and optical depths. Synthetic Fe I 6173 Å profiles provide bisector velocities, continuum intensities, and line-core intensities. We also analyse high-resolution quiet-Sun observations from the 1-m Swedish Solar Telescope (SST). Velocity diagnostics show similar phase differences and coherence at both sampling levels, including those derived from synthetic and observed bisector velocities, indicating their relative insensitivity to the sampling method. In contrast, temperature diagnostics show stronger and more extended gravity-wave signatures at fixed geometrical height. Synthetic continuum and line-core intensities retain these signatures but exhibit phase differences more closely resembling temperature diagnostics at constant optical depth. SST observations show similar qualitative behaviour, despite quantitative differences. Our results demonstrate that Doppler velocity provides a relatively robust diagnostic of gravity-wave phase propagation, whereas temperature and intensity diagnostics are more sensitive to opacity and radiative-transfer effects. Consequently, the latter may introduce biases in estimates of wave propagation speeds and energy fluxes.
#### oMEGACat-JWST I.: Project description and photometric data release
 - **Authors:** Maximilian Häberle, Callie Clontz, Massimo Griggio, Mattia Libralato, Isabella Carlton, Stefano Souza, Nadine Neumayer, Anil Seth, Peter J. Smith, Jay Anderson, Mayte Alfaro-Cuello, Holger Baumgardt, Luigi R. Bedin, Andrea Bellini, Steven Chen, James E. Davies, Anna de Graaff, Jeremy Hare, Oleg Kargaltsev, Kameron Goold, Michele Scalco, Zixian Wang, Matthew Whitaker
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23150

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23150

 - **Abstract**
 Omega Centauri (NGC 5139, $\omega$ Cen) is the most massive globular cluster in the Milky Way and the likely stripped nucleus of an accreted dwarf galaxy. Extensive HST observations have uncovered many of its peculiar features, including complex subpopulations and kinematic evidence for an intermediate-mass black hole via central fast-moving stars. High-precision astrometric follow-up is essential to constrain the orbits of these stars. We present oMEGACat-JWST, a deep photometric and astrometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core and surroundings that extends our previous oMEGACat efforts to near-infrared wavelengths. The superior sensitivity and angular resolution of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) provide significant astrometric and photometric improvements, particularly for faint stars. Our catalog is based on a custom reduction of NIRCam and NIRISS imaging across three JWST programs extending out to two half-light radii (10'). In total, our catalog contains approximately 677 000 sources. In the region overlapping with oMEGACat, we achieve high relative completeness (> 90% of HST sources recovered) while detecting over 86 000 new sources below HST's detection limit. Astrometric tests demonstrate a single-image precision as good as 0.5% of a NIRCam pixel (0.15 mas) at mF115W=18; at mF115W=23 the precision is still 1 mas. Color-magnitude diagrams based on our data show the well-known split between the primordial Helium and the Helium enriched populations and are particularly sensitive to light-element (CNO) variations of faint stars. oMEGACat-JWST provides the most complete photometric catalog of $\omega$ Cen's core to date. Combined with HST data, it enables multi-wavelength subpopulation analyses. Together with future monitoring observations, its high astrometric precision will improve our understanding of the kinematics of the central fast-moving stars in $\omega$ Cen.
