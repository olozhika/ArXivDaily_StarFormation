#### FORGE'd In The Early Universe II: Constraining The Pop III Initial Mass Function
 - **Authors:** Yasmine J. Meziani, Philip F. Hopkins, Pratik J. Gandhi, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère, Michael Y. Grudić, Shivan Khullar, Vasilii Pustovoit
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08598

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08598

 - **Abstract**
 We present a cosmological radiation-magnetohydrodynamics (RMHD) zoom-in simulation of $56$ Population III stars using FORGE'd in FIRE. Our simulation combines a cosmological initial condition centered on an isolated ultra-faint galaxy ($M_{\mathrm{halo}} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$ at $z=0$) with individual-star formation and feedback physics, reaching a maximum mass resolution of $\sim 10^{-2} M_\odot$ and spatial resolution of $\sim 10^{-3} \mathrm{pc}$. We follow continued star formation for just over $500$ Myr, from $z \sim 16$ to $z \sim 7$, substantially longer than what has been achieved before, and track star formation across five distinct minihalos. From our stellar population, we self-consistently resolve an emergent stellar initial mass function (IMF) and find $\alpha = 1.9$ for $dN/dM \propto M^{-\alpha}$ at $M_{\star} \gtrsim 2.2 M_{\odot}$. Additionally, we form both single and multiple stellar systems, including binaries and higher-order multiples, as well as stars with masses below $1 M_{\odot}$. At early times, the stellar mass distribution is more top-heavy, with a high-mass slope of $\alpha\sim1.4-1.5$, similar to previous Pop. III simulations, and steepens as star formation continues. This suggests that differences between our final IMF and previous studies may partly reflect the much longer evolution followed here, approximately five orders of magnitude longer than in several previous Pop. III sink-particle simulations. The first star-forming minihalo, which hosts the most massive stars in our simulation, has a shallower IMF slope of $\alpha\approx 1$, while later-forming minihalos produce lower maximum stellar masses in lower-density parent clouds.
#### FORGE'd in Fragments: Pop III star formation in a Milky Way-like progenitor halo
 - **Authors:** Vasilii Pustovoit, Shivan Khullar, Norman Murray, Yasmine J. Meziani, Claude-André Faucher-Giguère, Philip F. Hopkins, Michael Y. Grudić, J. Richard Bond
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08603

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08603

 - **Abstract**
 We present a radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) cosmological zoom-in simulation of Pop III star formation, drawing initial conditions from a Milky Way-like progenitor halo at redshift $z \sim 21$. We follow the collapse of primordial gas over 10 orders of magnitude, through to the formation of an accretion disk that becomes gravitationally unstable $\Delta t \sim -3$ kyr before the first protostar's formation. Prominent spiral arms then develop and fragment vigorously, producing $\sim 60$ sink formation events over the first $8$ kyr post-collapse evolution, with a total stellar mass reaching $> 150\,{\rm M_\odot}$ by $15$ kyr, the end of our simulation. The disk remains magnetically supercritical throughout: the mass-to-flux ratio falls from $\mu_\Phi \sim 10^2$-$10^3$ to $\sim 15$ by $15$ kyr, as the inflow brings in magnetic flux. The magnetic energy in the disk remains sub-dominant throughout, but grows by $\sim 3.5$ orders of magnitude after the first star forms. The disk starts sub-virial and reaches virial equilibrium within $\sim 2$ kyr. The total stellar mass of the population grows as $M_* \propto t^2$, confirming the prediction of accelerating accretion in self-gravitating turbulent collapse, at rates of $\dot{M} \sim 10^{-3}$-$10^{-2}\,{\rm M}_\odot {\rm yr}^{-1}$. The star formation efficiency reaches $\approx 90\%$ within the disk (after $\approx 11$ kyr; $\approx 80\%$ at $9.5$ kyr) and $\sim 50\%$ within a $0.1$ pc sphere. We find a top heavy IMF ($dN/dm \propto m^{-\alpha}$) with a high-mass slope $\alpha \approx 1$. The cosmological environment sets the boundary conditions of the disk: an envelope delivers $\sim 3\times 10^{-3}\,{\rm M}_\odot {\rm yr}^{-1}$ to the outer edge of a disk with a radius of $\sim 10^{3}$ AU; the disk spreads and, in our simulation, its fragmentation sites move outward, from $\sim 500$ to $\sim 1350$\,AU, until it has been depleted.

 - **Abstract**
 We developed TitanMPAS, a new Titan global and regional climate model, to simulate atmospheric dynamics and methane cycles. It integrates the non-hydrostatic MPAS dynamical core and TitanWRF physics parameterizations, with corrections to relax the thin-atmosphere assumptions on the gravity and curvature terms. TitanMPAS improves upon TitanWRF by incorporating a better moist convection parameterization and the observed surface properties. Long-term simulations ($>$100 Titan years) with a high resolution of $1^\circ$ and the realistic topography show that TitanMPAS is able to produce the stratospheric superrotation with typical equatorial wind speed over $\rm 120\,ms^{-1}$ and the maximum zonal wind speed exceeding $\rm 170\,ms^{-1}$ . Besides the good match of temperature profiles to the Cassini CIRS observations, the simulated wind, temperature and methane abundance in the troposphere match well with Huygens probe in-situ measurements. TitanMPAS also captures the major features of seasonal methane precipitation distribution patterns inferred from the observed cloud activities, as well as the vertical and latitudinal variations of methane abundance in the reanalysis of Cassini VIMS data. Resolution sensitivity studies show that higher resolution produces faster stratospheric superrotation, as well as stronger near-surface wind due to better resolved terrain variations. We further demonstrate that, despite the weak solar insolation, the near-surface temperature, pressure, wind magnitude and methane abundance all exhibit diurnal variations. Lastly, we show potential impacts of topography on methane transport and eddy momentum flux in both troposphere and stratosphere, which should be investigated in a future study.

 We performed a spectroscopic survey of all known white dwarfs with $M \geq 1.3~M_\odot$ and $T_{\rm eff}\geq11,000$ K identified through Gaia DR3 astrometry from the Gentile-Fusillo et al. (2021) catalog. Of the 293 white dwarf candidates that met our criteria, 259 had no optical spectroscopy or spectral classification available in the literature.  Context: HII regions are a key stage of massive star formation, where ionizing radiation reshapes the interstellar medium. Their continuum spectra trace thermal dust, free-free emission, and anomalous microwave emission (AME). Aims: We investigate how these components evolve as an HII region expands and correlate with gas and electron column densities across spatial scales. Methods: We post-process RAMSES radiation-magnetohydrodynamical simulations of a $10^4 M_\odot$ cloud with POLARIS.  - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09039

 - **Abstract**
 Gaia has discovered dormant compact-object binaries in which the compact object shows no observable interaction with its luminous companion. These systems, hereafter Gaia BHs and Gaia NSs for black hole (BH) and neutron star (NS) companions, provide a new probe of compact-object binary formation. These detached systems are difficult to produce through standard isolated binary evolution alone, and their formation pathways remain unclear. Previous $N$-body studies have shown that Gaia BH-like objects are formed efficiently in star clusters, while Gaia NS-like objects are not. This has been attributed to the BH subsystem dominating the cluster core. We performed direct $N$-body simulations of $10^4 \mathrm{M}_\odot$ star clusters in a Galactic tidal field and investigated how NS-main-sequence (NS-MS) binaries form during the late BH-depleted phase. We found that NS-MS binaries satisfying Gaia-sensitive criteria form only after BH depletion. NSs are present in the core throughout the evolution, but the strong encounters there are monopolized by the BH subsystem until it is depleted. Our results show that the dynamical formation of Gaia-sensitive NS-MS systems is not prevented in star clusters, but delayed relative to Gaia BH-like systems.

 - **Abstract**
 Recent observational studies have identified phosphorus-rich (P-rich) stars ([P/Fe]>1.0) in both Galactic globular clusters (GCs) including three confirmed cases and the field population.  - **Abstract**
 Astronomical datasets often consist of a grid of datapoints in two or more dimensions, such as images, spectroscopic time-series, and imaging time-series. Gaussian processes (GPs) are a powerful tool for dealing with correlated noise in these data, arising from both astrophysical and instrumental systematics. Despite this, popular GP optimisations such as celerite are largely restricted to 1D datasets. Extensions to 2D data typically come with limited flexibility or poor scaling. As a motivating example, this limitation is significant for transiting exoplanet spectroscopy with JWST, as current analyses typically assume each spectroscopic light curve is statistically independent, despite many potential sources of wavelength-correlation, including detector readout noise and stellar granulation. These GP optimisations are highly flexible, permit efficient analysis of 2D datasets containing millions of datapoints, and in many cases have a cheaper cost than transit light curve modelling. These optimisations are key to pushing the limits of exoplanet spectroscopy to smaller targets around bright host stars, as we increasingly hit instrumental and astrophysical noise floors. We suggest further applications to the analysis of ground-based high resolution spectroscopic time-series, extreme precision radial velocities, and high-contrast imaging, drawing on the connection that certain 2D GP models represent a probabilistic generalisation of PCA. Applications may even extend to machine learning and geostatistics, as we present novel optimisations for the Linear Mixing Model and the Linear Model of Coregionalisation.

 - **Abstract**
 Primordial non-Gaussianity is a unique probe of the physics of inflation, but its large-scale-structure signature is difficult to measure because it appears on the largest, most cosmic-variance-limited modes. The standard view of local-type primordial non-Gaussianity is isotropic: it induces a scale-dependent correction to tracer bias, $\Delta b(k)\propto f_{\rm NL}k^{-2}$, on large scales. Here we show that the same large-scale response can be reformulated as an anisotropic, nonlocal environmental-bias effect. Motivated by the spectral cosmic-web hierarchy of Kitaura and Sinigaglia, we define a Hessian field $\mathcal{H}_{ij}(\bf{x})\equiv\partial_i\partial_j\nabla^{-4}\delta(\bf{x})$, whose trace obeys $\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{H}=\nabla^{-2}\delta$ and therefore carries the same $k^{-2}$ enhancement that appears in the Dalal et al. scale-dependent-bias term. Diagonalizing $\mathcal{H}_{ij}$ defines four large-scale web environments, analogous to knots, filaments, sheets, and voids, although operating on scales much larger than the conventional nonlinear cosmic web. We assign one weight to each environment and test the construction with the AbacusPNG suite of local-PNG $N$-body simulations, using $f_{\rm NL}=\{-100,-30,30,100\}$. We show that these four weights reproduce the large-scale $k^{-2}$ rise of the tracer bias. We further show that existing halo catalogs without PNG can be post-processed to imprint an effective $f_{\rm NL}$ signature. Beyond mock generation, the resulting cosmic-web response motivates an observational statistic based on changes in tracer occupation of large-scale environments relative to a matched Gaussian reference, complementary to the conventional low-$k$ power spectrum.

 - **Abstract**
 Self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) haloes can become extremely dense following gravothermal core collapse, motivating their use as explanations for unusually concentrated gravitational lenses. We follow initially Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) haloes through core formation and collapse deep into the short-mean-free-path regime, and compute finite-source magnifications, critical curves, and source-plane lensing cross sections for isolated spherical haloes with elastic, velocity-independent self-interactions, including external shear in selected calculations. The core collapse stage at which SIDM haloes are optimal lenses coincides with the epoch at which the characteristic core surface density is close to critical, which typically occurs during the early stages of core collapse. These results demonstrate that central density alone is insufficient to characterize SIDM lensing and that its evolving mass distribution must be modelled explicitly.

 The stability of spin rates and emission regions of radio-emitting neutron stars (pulsars) has enabled remarkable advances, including the detection of exoplanets, testing theories of gravity, and stimulating the vigorous exploration of a new window in gravitational wave astronomy an nanohertz frequencies. An ideal astrophysical clock would emit identical pulses at a constant rate, propagating along single geodesics through a perfect vacuum. In reality, the timing precision achievable with pulsars is limited by spin noise, variability in the beam produced in a complex magnetosphere, and multipath propagation through a turbulent interstellar medium, with additional limitations from uncertainties in the changing pulsar -- observatory vector and instrumental effects. Here we present the fundamentals of pulsar timing, its current limitations, and prospects for improvements in timing precision. We develop metrics to assess the timing quality of a pulsar and use them to evaluate sources currently included in pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) as a means for characterizing the quality of future pulsar discoveries. With increases in telescope sensitivity, pulsar timing precision will increasingly be limited by propagation effects in the interstellar medium. We identify sources of non-Gaussianity in timing residuals and separate out effects that are chromatic or achromatic, as well as effects that vary on short (seconds to minutes) and longer (days to months) timescales. Modern wide-bandwidth receivers provide simultaneous arrival times across a range of frequencies, enabling the robust mitigation of chromatic interstellar effects and the identification of non-Gaussian events superimposed on random fluctuations. We recommend changes to timing procedures that leverage instrumentation advances to improve timing precision.
#### Forecasting Fundamental-Plane Tests of Intermediate-mass Black Holes in Radio-selected Dwarf Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Ramananda Santra, Samik Mitra, Shahram Abbassi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09720

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09720

 - **Abstract**
 A radio/X-ray Fundamental Plane (FP) test of an intermediate-mass black hole is informative only when the compact radio emission, hard X-rays, accretion state, and host association refer to the same accretor. We used published VLA, VLBA, \textit{Chandra}, and \textit{HST} observations of a radio-selected dwarf-galaxy sample to determine when present nondetections are constraining and what observations are required for a stronger test. The VLBA follow-up reveals that compactness alone does not establish association with the target dwarf, while for the best nuclear candidates the radio component relevant to the FP has not yet been isolated on the intermediate physical scales between the VLA and VLBA measurements. We therefore recast the FP as a forward question: for an assumed black-hole mass, how likely is a given radio observation to detect the compact core? The calculation includes intrinsic FP scatter, X-ray uncertainties, the radio spectral index, the accretion state, and source size relative to the beam. The existing VLBA nondetections have low completeness across the adopted intermediate-mass range and do not provide useful black-hole mass upper limits. Meaningful tests require deeper resolution-matched radio imaging, recovery of intermediate-scale structure, and contemporaneous measurements of the intrinsic hard-X-ray component. Because the calculation returns a mass-dependent detection function for each source, the same framework can be applied to larger dwarf-galaxy samples to quantify survey completeness and build selection functions for population studies of low-mass black holes.
#### Multi-phase Carbon Structure and Dynamics in NGC 6334: Insights from CO, [C I], and [C II]
 - **Authors:** S. Neupane, F. Wyrowski, N. Schneider, A.G.G.M. Tielens, R. Simon, J. S. Urquhart, R. Güsten, M-Y. Lee, D. Russeil, G. Garay, R. Higgins, K. M. Menten
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09871

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09871

 - **Abstract**
 We present high-angular and velocity resolution observations of the emission lines of [CII] at 158 micron, [CI] at 609 micron, and the 12CO and 13CO (J=3-2) transitions toward the central region of NGC 6334, providing a detailed multi-phase view of carbon in this massive star-forming complex. By combining morphology and spatial scales information, kinematics, line profiles, and line ratio analyses, we quantify the distribution and physical state of ionized and atomic carbon, and carbon in its molecular form in CO. [CII] emission is the most extended, permeates the mapped region and is dynamically complex, while 13CO is compact and quiescent, and 12CO and [CI] occupy intermediate regimes connecting dense regions to transitional diffuse layers. We interpret these emission distributions such that the brightest [CII] emission traces photodissociation regions (PDRs) close to the H II region bubbles located along the central filament, while the extended [CII] emission arises from the more diffuse warm gas, indicating either cloud formation or dispersal processes. Remarkably, the [CI] is more extended than the 13CO, but less than [CII] and may probe the gas velocity structure resulting from colliding flows or cloud-cloud collisions. Line ratios involving [CII], such as [CII]/[CI] or [CII]/CO are systematically high, revealing widespread diffuse emission and localized UV-illuminated surfaces. Line ratio analysis and K-means clustering of [CI]/13CO versus dust continuum (cold 870 micron and warm 70 micron) emission reveal stratified chemical structure that UV photons penetrate deeply into the clumpy ridge (Av~18), suggesting a porous PDR. These results together depict that NGC 6334 is a clumpy, feedback-dominated PDR ridge with coexisting carbon phases, where [CI] and [CII] also trace dynamical processing through multiple velocity components linked to cloud-cloud collisions.
