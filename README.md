#### Insights into low-ionization structures in planetary nebulae: near-IR longslit spectroscopy
 - **Authors:** M. B. Mari, S. Akras, L. Konstantinou, D. R. Gonçalves, C. G. Díaz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17452

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17452

 - **Abstract**
 Context. Low-ionization structures (LISs) are small, dense clumps within planetary nebulae (PNe) that stand out in low-ionization emission lines, but the physical processes responsible for their excitation and the nature of H2 remain poorly understood. Aims. We aim to characterize the excitation conditions and mass content of LISs in two PNe through near-IR longslit spectroscopy of these structures, combining H2, Brg, and [Fe II] diagnostics. Methods. We obtained H and K band spectra with GNIRS at Gemini North for LISs in NGC 6818 and NGC 7354 --objects with confirmed H2 emission in their LISs. We applied H2 excitation diagrams, vibrational and rotational temperature diagnostics, classical near-IR diagnostic diagrams, and spatial profile analysis to constrain the physical conditions of the molecular and atomic gas phases. Results. H2, Brg, and [Fe II] emission are detected in all structures studied. The H2 level populations are consistent with predominantly thermal, near-LTE excitation, with excitation temperatures in the range ~1860-2450 K. The [Fe II]/Brg ratios are well below the values expected for shock or X-ray dominated regions, favoring photoionization as the dominant excitation mechanism of the atomic gas. A clear spatial stratification is observed in both objects, with Brg peaking closer to the central star and H2 1-0S(1) and [Fe II] co-spatial at larger distances. The warm molecular masses derived from H2 emission lines are consistent with those estimated independently from H2 column densities. Conclusions. The diagnostics presented here support a photoevaporation scenario in which LISs are dense clumps being progressively ionized from outside by the radiation of central stars, while their H2 cores remain self-shielded. A slow internal shock may release Fe into the gas phase. These results extend the molecular mass trend established previously to a larger sample.
#### Euclid DR1 Legacy Science on Ultracool Dwarfs: New URLD candidates in Euclid and SPHEREx
 - **Authors:** N. Lodieu (1,2), M. Žerjal (1,2), N. Vitas (1,2), C. Domínguez Tagle (1,2), E. L. Martín (1,2), J.-Y. Zhang (3), S. Tsilia (1,2) ((1) IAC, La Laguna, Tenerife, Spain, (2) ULL, La Laguna, Tenerife, Spain, (3) University of Western Ontario, Canada)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17519

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17519

 - **Abstract**
 Unusually red L dwarfs (URLDs) probe extreme atmospheric conditions in the substellar regime, where enhanced dust opacity, cloud structure, and surface gravity effects challenge standard brown dwarf models. As free-floating analogues of young giant exoplanets, they provide key benchmarks for interpreting low-resolution infrared spectra from current and future wide-field space missions. The goal is to identify new red L dwarf candidates both photometrically and spectroscopically in the first data release (DR1) of the Euclid space mission. The area covered is about 1,900 and 50 square degrees in the wide and deep survey, respectively. We used colour cuts in multiple colour-colour diagrams combined with point-source and quality criteria to identify candidate red L dwarfs in both the Euclid wide and deep survey fields. We cross-matched photometric candidates with the SPHEREx mission and Euclid red grism spectra to confirm spectroscopic properties and evaluate bolometric magnitudes and colours. We identified 1202 and 38 red L dwarf candidates in the Euclid wide and deep surveys, respectively. Spectroscopic follow-up and archival cross-matching confirm a substantial new population of red L dwarfs. Distance-independent diagnostics show that these objects occupy distinct loci characterised by enhanced condensate opacity and significant redness. Euclid vastly expands the census of unusually red L dwarfs. While preliminary spectral classifications suggest many of these new candidates are intermediate-age (Pleiades-like) objects, a subset exhibits extremely red signatures indicative of very young, low-gravity (<=10 Myr) atmospheres, providing vital benchmarks for young exoplanet research.
#### Observations of stellar feedback: tracers, diagnostics, measurements, and physical interpretation
 - **Authors:** Anna F. McLeod
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17592

 - **Abstract**
 Stellar feedback is central to galaxy evolution, yet the coupling of radiation, stellar winds, and supernovae to the multi-phase interstellar medium remains uncertain. This review treats this as a problem of inference, framed around four qualitative axes of ignorance: observational diagnostics are indirect; stellar population synthesis models must be ground-truthed before being used to infer feedback quantities; recent high-redshift observations have opened a new regime that may require re-calibration; and stellar feedback and the affected environment are interdependent. We examine the inference chain, from observables to physical quantities and from those quantities to how energy and momentum are transferred to the surrounding medium, across the principal diagnostics of stellar feedback: ionised gas, mechanically-driven bubbles, giant molecular cloud lifetimes, galactic outflows, and Lyman-continuum escape. We ask how much observations reveal about the link between the feedback-driving massive stars and the feedback-driven interstellar medium. We further discuss how stellar population synthesis assumptions, environment (e.g., metallicity, dust content), and galaxy properties (e.g., stellar mass, gas content) affect feedback measurements, and how high-redshift observations are testing calibrations established in the nearby Universe. The emerging picture is that interpreting a feedback measurement requires explicit control of the inference chain and of its physical context; feedback should therefore be contextualised, i.e. interpreted in terms of mechanism, gas phase, environment, and spatial and temporal scale. Progress will depend on measuring feedback-related quantities across the full range of star-forming systems and, more importantly, on obtaining observations (real, and synthetic from post-processed simulations) that match the same tracer and spatial scale across the same systems.
#### SN Progenitors of SNRs in OB Associations in the LMC. I. Assessment of Methodology Using N63A
 - **Authors:** Yuka Terada, You-Hua Chu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17665

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17665

 - **Abstract**
 Color--magnitude diagrams (CMDs) can constrain the progenitor masses of supernova remnants (SNRs), but mass estimates for massive stars are affected by limited temperature sensitivity, extinction, crowding, and nebular contamination. We use the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) SNR N63A and its OB association LH83 as a benchmark to evaluate CMD-based mass estimates. Among the tested diagnostics, the extinction-corrected MCPS $B$ vs. $U-B$ CMD provides the most useful constraint on the massive-star population. The $V$ vs. $B-V$ CMD is less reliable, while the reddening-free $W$-$Q$ diagram does not improve individual mass estimates because of its larger photometric uncertainties. Gaia EDR3 recovers bright stars missing or saturated in MCPS but provides weaker separation among the hottest stars. For seven spectroscopically classified stars, the $B$ vs. $U-B$ CMD-based masses are lower than the HR-diagram masses inferred using the same MIST tracks by $8.0~M_\odot$ on average, with a scatter of $7.0~M_\odot$. Isochrone comparisons suggest that most of the massive stars in LH83 formed about 7-15 Myr ago, complicating progenitor mass estimates based on the present-day mass function. Nevertheless, the masses of D243-1a and D243-2, stellar lifetimes predicted by the MIST models, and the empirical bubble-size-progenitor-mass relation suggest a progenitor mass of order 25-30 $M_\odot$. Our results highlight the potential and limitations of this approach for studying other LMC SNRs in OB associations.
#### Squeezing Drives Southward Magnetic Field Enhancement During 10 May 2024 CME-CME Interaction
 - **Authors:** Ziwei Wang, Liping Yang, Xueshang Feng, Jiansen He, Hui Tian, Fang Shen, Zhenyong Hou, Rui Wang, Xiao Zhang, Ming Xiong
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17807

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17807

 - **Abstract**
 The southward magnetic field carried by coronal mass ejections (CMEs) is a key driver of space weather events, yet how interacting CMEs amplify this field remains debated. The 10 May 2024 event, which produced the strongest geomagnetic storm since 2003, offers an ideal testbed for this question. Combining multi-spacecraft observations with high-resolution controlled numerical experiments, we track the CMEs continuously from their origin near the solar surface to beyond Earth, and find that inherent squeezing during CME-CME interactions generates the sustained enhancement of the southward field, exceeding 60 nT in magnitude near Earth. First driven by the dynamic pressure of the trailing CME and later maintained by magnetic and thermal pressures at the CME-CME interface, squeezing transforms the preceding CME from a bulky cloud into a flattened "pancake" structure, prevents expansion-induced field decay, and maintains an amplified southward field over long distances. The strength of this enhancement scales with the intensity of the squeezing. Our findings establish a direct link between CME-CME interaction and strong southward fields, laying a physics-based foundation for understanding and forecasting the geoeffectiveness of interacting CMEs.
#### What constrains the neutron-star crust? Present data, future measurements, and EOS doppelgängers
 - **Authors:** Vishal Parmar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Nuclear Theory (nucl-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17920

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17920

 - **Abstract**
 The neutron-star crust plays a central role in pulsar glitches, thermal evolution, and stellar oscillations, yet its properties remain difficult to constrain. We use Bayesian inference with a unified crust-core equation of state to assess what present nuclear and astrophysical data tell us about the crust and what future stellar measurements can add. Within this model, present crust constraints are dominated by chiral effective field theory, with laboratory data providing additional information and current stellar observations having a smaller effect. The adopted theoretical bands determine how tightly the crust properties are constrained, while the treatment of neutron-rich surface energy affects their inferred values. Forecasts centred on a single reference equation of state show that more precise measurements of radius, tidal deformability, and total moment of inertia mainly constrain crust thickness. They provide little additional information about the crust-core transition, crust mass, or crustal moment of inertia and do not recover the precision lost when the chiral effective field theory constraint is removed. Two models with nearly identical global stellar observables can have crust masses and crustal moments of inertia differing by about a factor of two, yet remain indistinguishable at the assumed precision. Precise global measurements therefore need not uniquely determine the crust. Observations that directly probe the crust are needed to distinguish these models.
#### Numerical experiments on sunspot penumbral flows
 - **Authors:** Tanayveer Singh Bhatia, Sami K. Solanki, Aswathi Krishnan Kutty, Robert H. Cameron, Damien F. Przybylski
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17946

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17946

 - **Abstract**
 We present 3D radiative magnetohydrodynamic simulations of sunspots with a self-consistently generated penumbra. We analyze the impact of resolution, surrounding opposite-polarity fields and subsurface large-scale flows on the structure and stability of the simulated penumbra. Extended penumbral filaments are obtained for a subset of the simulations. These were run at 25 km (15 km) horizontal (vertical) resolution and with initial conditions favoring fields with strong horizontal components in the penumbral region. The surface velocities in these simulations are consistent with observations of Evershed flow. The main driving mechanism remains the same as illustrated previously in Rempel (2012). The most successful simulations produce extended penumbrae using a potential magnetic field top boundary, in contrast to previous simulations of extended penumbra with an artificial enhancement of horizontal magnetic field at the top boundary.
#### Asteroseismology of blue straggler stars in $ω$ Centauri. Stellar collisions as a possible formation scenario
 - **Authors:** Jadwiga Daszynska-Daszkiewicz, P. Gora, P. Walczak, A. Miszuda, W. Szewczuk, M. Heller, F. R. N. Schneider
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17995

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17995

 - **Abstract**
 Context. Blue stragglers are intriguing stars that appear more massive and younger than expected from single-star evolution, yet their evolutionary histories remain elusive because of the multiple possible formation channels. A promising and poorly explored avenue for addressing this problem is asteroseismology, which provides the most stringent constraints on internal structure and evolution. Aims. We investigate five SX Phoenicis stars from the core of the globular cluster \omega Centauri that pulsate in two radial modes and occupy the blue-straggler region of the colour-magnitude diagram. Seismic models are constructed assuming a collisional origin and compared with our previous seismic studies based on single-star evolution. For comparison, we also explore a binary-evolution scenario involving mass transfer for one of the stars. Methods. The evolution of the parent stars is computed with MESA. The collision is then modelled with the MMAMS code, which reconstructs the merger remnant using an entropic variable. The subsequent evolution is followed with MESA up to the current age of the cluster. Finally, we construct seismic models reproducing the observed frequencies of the two radial modes. Results. We present the first seismic modelling of SX Phe stars (blue stragglers) based on the collision-formation scenario. Convective-core overshooting is essential for rejuvenation and for obtaining seismic models in the main-sequence phase of evolution, but it is not always sufficient. Whether a merger remnant undergoes rejuvenation depends in a complex way on the masses of the parent stars, metallicity, and collision time. We identify three evolutionary behaviours: no, delayed, and immediate rejuvenation. The collisional seismic models imply younger ages and higher masses than the seismic models based on single-star evolution.
#### JCMT--Venus: Daily Monitoring of HDO and SO$_2$ Absorption in Venus' Mesosphere
 - **Authors:** Wei Tang, David L. Clements, Jane Greaves, Malcolm J. Currie, Supachai Awiphan, Helen Jane Fraser, Masataka Imai, Ing-Guey Jiang, Xiaohu Li, Kim Miskovetz, David A Naylor, Bannawit Pimpanuwat, Anita M. S. Richards, Gerald Schieven, Stephen Serjeant, Sirinrat Sithajan
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Earth and Planetary Astrophysics (astro-ph.EP)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18271

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18271

 - **Abstract**
 We present three multi-week, disc-integrated James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (JCMT) campaigns monitoring HDO and SO$_2$ absorption in Venus' upper mesosphere at altitudes of approximately 80--110 km. Obtained in February 2022, July 2023, and August--September 2023, these observations provide dense temporal sampling and complementary observing geometries, enabling the disentanglement of short-term, long-term, and local solar time (LST) variability, which has been challenging in previous studies. We have four main findings. First, SO$_2$ in the upper mesosphere, being largely absent in February 2022 but present in both 2023 campaigns, may follow the same long-term trend observed at cloud tops. Second, long-term variability in H$_2$O is identified here for the first time and is interpreted as an anticorrelated response to the long-term change in SO$_2$, likely through H$_2$SO$_4$ formation. Third, H$_2$O is broadly stable within each campaign, although occasional short-term variability is present in the form of a depletion event. Finally, short-term variability is likely present for SO$_2$, but it does not dominate the overall behaviour. In the absence of strong long-term differences, the daily SO$_2$ abundance evolution is consistent with an asymmetric LST profile peaking near midnight, which is well reproduced by an independent model. These results are enabled by a dedicated reduction and retrieval pipeline that mitigates the strong standing-wave baseline structure in the spectra and quantifies systematic uncertainties, providing a methodological framework for future studies facing similar challenges.
